This radio program examined the legal challenges facing families in post-revolutionary Iran and how they are adapted to modern requirements.
A central theme was the methodology for aligning Islamic jurisprudence with contemporary needs, avoiding blind imitation of Western legal systems.
Professor Ahansafi illustrated how the 2012 Family Protection Law expanded the concept of child support to include broader educational and medical expenses.
Professor Hedayatnia explained that the legislature updated the definition of spousal support by referencing religious rulings, shifting it from a limited to an illustrative list to cover healthcare costs.
Despite these achievements, significant legal gaps remain regarding the financial support of relatives, particularly the elderly and children with special needs.
The overall conclusion is that dynamic jurisprudence has the capacity to address social changes but requires more precise legislation for vulnerable groups.
