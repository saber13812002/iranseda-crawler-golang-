The program began with the recitation of romantic poetry before transitioning to a discussion on Islamic ethics regarding the relationship between teachers and students.
Citing narrations and real-life anecdotes from prominent scholars, the broadcast emphasized the necessity of mutual humility, dignity, and compassion between mentors and learners.
Examples of the fatherly behavior of great scholars, such as Ayatollah Haeri and Ayatollah Borujerdi, were shared to illustrate the depth of this bond.
The second part of the show featured a reading from the novel "The Leftover Son," which tells the story of Salar, a widower living in 1963.
Salar, who was rejected by his family, lives in Tehran while grappling with childhood memories and his unique relationship with his grandmother.
The narrative delves into the details of Salar's daily life, including his work for Daye Jamshid and the domestic tensions with his wife, Azar.
Finally, the program concluded by answering a religious question about the rulings of Hajj and announcing schedule changes for Radio Ma'aref programs.
This broadcast served as a blend of ethical education, literary storytelling, and responses to listeners' religious inquiries.
