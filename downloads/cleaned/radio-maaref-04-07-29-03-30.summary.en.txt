In this program, Professor Bagher Sabahi examines the role of Mulla Sadra's Transcendent Theosophy in the thought of Imam Khomeini. The professor emphasizes that Imam Khomeini viewed Sadra's philosophy not as a Hellenic system, but as a derivation from Islamic religious texts, using it as a primary model for interpreting Quranic verses and traditions. By accepting key Sadrian principles such as the primacy of existence and substantial motion, Imam Khomeini was able to elucidate many religious issues through rational argument and mystical intuition. A crucial point is that Imam Khomeini believed the pinnacle of philosophy lies in theoretical mysticism, requiring one to go beyond Transcendent Theosophy to reach mystical wisdom. Utilizing concepts like existential poverty, he offered novel interpretations of Quranic verses and initiated the spiritual path by understanding one's own "nothingness." Ultimately, by critiquing certain aspects of Ibn Sina's work and leveraging Sadra's insights, Imam Khomeini demonstrated that familiarity with this school is the key to a deeper understanding of divine laws and knowledge.
