The radio program emphasized the necessity of continuous follow-up on decisions from the presidential level down to executive and expert staff to prevent management dilution. Citing the current consensus and cooperation among government sectors, it highlighted a favorable opportunity to restructure the bureaucracy and reduce administrative layers. Reviving production units and prioritizing manufacturing were identified as the primary keys to the country's economic progress. Ensuring the timely supply of essential goods was deemed crucial for stabilizing exchange rates, securing food safety, and maintaining price stability. Making the import process of essential commodities competitive among various countries was proposed as a strategy to lower both domestic and international prices. Ultimately, the goal of these policies is to guarantee the livelihood of citizens by allowing them to purchase necessary items without fear of sudden and severe price hikes.
