In this radio program, the commentator interprets the phrase "dedicating their hearing to beneficial knowledge" from the Hamasa sermon as a defining trait of the pious. He describes knowledge as the food of the human soul, emphasizing the need to scrutinize both the content and the source to avoid the negative influence of foreign media and unhealthy material. The commentator asserts that the value of any knowledge is determined by its subject, identifying the knowledge of monotheism as the highest form of cognition. Beneficial knowledge is defined as that which serves both worldly and spiritual needs, and must be accompanied by sincerity, practical application, and rationality. Key indicators of such knowledge include fostering humility, fear of God, and increased effort in religious practice. Conversely, non-beneficial knowledge includes information that is useless if unknown, unprofitable if known, or known but not acted upon. Ultimately, the goal is for individuals to achieve spiritual growth and perfection through the integration of knowledge and action.
