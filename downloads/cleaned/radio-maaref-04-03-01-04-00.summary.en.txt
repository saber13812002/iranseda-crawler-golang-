The first segment provided practical advice for managing crowds during the Hajj rituals, specifically recommending that pilgrims bypass congested areas to perform the stoning of the devil efficiently. It also detailed the rulings regarding proxy stoning for those with valid excuses and the precise timing for leaving Mina after the Zuhr prayer on the twelfth of Dhul-Hijjah. Subsequently, religious responses addressed questions about modesty and interaction with minors, the sufficiency of Friday prayer over the Dhuhr prayer, and rulings on purity and fasting. A key point emphasized was the principle of presumption of purity, advising against obsessive checking of infant diapers. In the Parsaiyan program, the importance of showing special respect to the Sayyids, as descendants of the Prophet, was highlighted through anecdotes about the etiquette of prominent Shia scholars. This section illustrated the spiritual status of Sayyids by recounting the behavior of figures like Sheikh Abbas Qummi and Ayatollah Behjat. Finally, the Zalaal Tarikh program began a critical analysis of the protests against Caliph Uthman, examining his correspondence with governors and the reactions of the protesters. This historical analysis focused on the political and social dynamics and governance challenges during the early period of Islam.
