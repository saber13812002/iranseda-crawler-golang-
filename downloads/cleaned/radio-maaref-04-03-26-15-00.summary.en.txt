This radio program discussed animal rights from an Islamic jurisprudential perspective, emphasizing that these rights are absolute and supported by religious narrations.
The guests highlighted that harming, unnecessarily confining, or insulting animals is considered a sin, and even cursing an animal without cause is forbidden.
However, keeping dogs is subject to strict limitations due to their inherent impurity and public health concerns within Islamic law and domestic legislation.
Legal experts argued that keeping animals in public spaces must not cause nuisance, disrupt public order, or promote vice.
The history of parliamentary attempts to legislate regarding dangerous animals and dog ownership was reviewed, noting that most bills have failed to pass.
According to existing laws, animal owners bear clear civil and criminal liability if their animals cause harm to others or create disorder.
The final conclusion is that while religion values animal rights, keeping dogs in public spaces in Iran is impermissible due to conflicts with religious norms and public health standards.
