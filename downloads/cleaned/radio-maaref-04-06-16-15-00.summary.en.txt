In this radio program, two jurists discussed the Islamic legal ruling on "retaliation in kind" during war and its specific limits.
The primary legal basis for this permission is the Quranic verse "And if they attack you, attack them in return," which was revealed to address Muslim concerns about enemy aggression during sacred months.
Scholars have offered various interpretations of the word "like" in this verse, with some arguing for equivalence in the degree of aggression and others for similarity in the type of action used.
A more probable view suggests that the verse simply grants permission for self-defense without mandating exact parity in quantity or method, as such precision is impractical in the chaos of war.
It was emphasized that any military action, including retaliation, is strictly conditional upon the authorization of the religious authority or the legitimate ruler.
Significant restrictions apply, notably the prohibition of using weapons of mass destruction that would harm civilians or destroy the environment.
The Quranic phrasing limits the scope of retaliation to the actual aggressors, explicitly excluding non-combatants from being targeted.
Ultimately, while retaliation is permitted, it is not unlimited and must be exercised within the bounds of Shariah principles and under the supervision of the rightful authority.
