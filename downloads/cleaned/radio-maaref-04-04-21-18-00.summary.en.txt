In the radio program "Ganj-e Sadat," Hujjat al-Islam Falavi emphasized the virtue of spending in God's path (infak) as a key to moral cultivation and problem-solving, citing Quranic verses and narrations from Imam Ali. He defined correct infak as an act rooted in Sharia, where the giver, the wealth, and the purpose are all free from sin. According to narrations, wealth not spent in God's path is not true sustenance, rendering the owner merely a treasurer for others. Spending in obedience to God is considered the greatest blessing, whereas using wealth for sin brings punishment. The benefits of infak include gaining God's pleasure, spiritual stability, the prayers of angels, and full rewards in both this world and the hereafter. God promises that those who spend in His path will never face injustice or deprivation, and such acts lead to increased blessings. Ultimately, infak is beneficial not only for the individual but also for the family and society, liberating one from stinginess and fear.
