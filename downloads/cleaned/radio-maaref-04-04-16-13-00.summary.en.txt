The text begins with a narrative from the book "Neshan-e Hassan," where Hassan learns of his mother's anxiety regarding his brother Hussein's condition. Following this, a sermon by Hojjat al-Islam Sadeqi emphasizes the importance of preserving the Ashura movement as a school for honorable living. He defines the concept of Wilayat as the manifestation of God and views any objection to divine actions as stemming from Satan's ignorance and arrogance. Citing the Quran, he describes hell as the result of internal sins like envy and pride, which turn faith into ashes. Referring to the Prophet's dream to Imam Hussein, he presents his martyrdom as a divine decree to preserve religion and extinguish the fire of tyranny. The presence of Karbala's women, especially Zainab (SA), is credited with preserving the Prophet's honor and sustaining the Islamic Revolution against enemy storms. Finally, the text concludes with a prayer asking God to keep the nation, the system, and the youth steadfast on the path of Karbala's martyrs.
