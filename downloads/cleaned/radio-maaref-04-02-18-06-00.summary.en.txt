This segment of the program analyzes the jurisprudential rule of "La Zarar" (no harm) and provides a detailed conceptual definition of "harm." By examining linguistic and theological perspectives, the speaker argues that harm is ultimately defined as pain, suffering, or the absence of desirability, standing in contrast to benefit, which is characterized by pleasure and joy. A central philosophical debate addressed is whether harm is an existential reality or a state of non-existence. While some jurists, such as Ayatollah Khoei, classify harm as a matter of "non-existence," the speaker supports Ayatollah Sistani's interpretation that harm is an existential entity. This view posits that harm is a "condition resulting from diminution" or a change in state, rather than merely the absence of something. Consequently, the opposition between harm and benefit is one of contradiction, with both being considered existential realities. This analysis underscores that preventing potential harm holds significant jurisprudential and logical priority due to its existential nature.
