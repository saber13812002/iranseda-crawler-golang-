This radio segment recounts the historical narrative of the Battle of Camel, focusing on the martyrdom of a young man named Muslim who was sent to deliver the Quran to the opposing army. Despite having both his hands severed, the young man clutched the Quran in his arms and stood bravely against spears and swords to convey Imam Ali's message of justice. His mother, Umm Muzrih, witnessed his martyrdom and carried his body back to Imam Ali's camp, an event that deeply moved the onlookers. The program also described the battlefield formations and Imam Ali's command to Muhammad ibn Hanafiya to lead the charge with the banner. A discussion followed regarding the influence of maternal traits on a son's bravery, comparing this to the Imams Hasan and Husayn. After the historical segment, the program transitioned to answering listeners' religious questions. Key rulings included the prohibition of eating hedgehog meat, the lack of obligation to repay money previously consumed with consent, and the conditions for looking at images of women with proper hijab. Additionally, the program addressed the rules for night prayers, handling lost items, changing the intention of a vow, and the recommended etiquette for visiting shrines with wudu or ghusl.
