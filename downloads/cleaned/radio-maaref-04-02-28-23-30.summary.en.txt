This discussion highlights the promotion of pilgrimage culture as a key, conscious strategy employed by Safavid rulers to consolidate power and religious identity. The Safavid kings deliberately focused on restoring and revitalizing the shrines of Imams and Imams' descendants while neglecting Sufi masters to strengthen the infrastructure of pilgrimage. Military and pilgrimage journeys by rulers like Shah Ismail and Shah Abbas to Khorasan and Iraq served not only to counter external threats but also to facilitate public access to holy sites and foster emotional bonds with the Ahl al-Bayt. This policy reached its peak during Shah Abbas's reign, marked by his famous trips to Mashhad and the restoration of religious structures along pilgrimage routes. The segment concludes by emphasizing the necessity of Muslim unity and rejecting divisive nationalism, which contradicts the Quranic teachings of brotherhood. This Safavid approach transformed pilgrimage into a tool for social and political cohesion that continues to this day.
