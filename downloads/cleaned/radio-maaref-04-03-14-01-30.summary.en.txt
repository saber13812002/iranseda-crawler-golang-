In his Mid-Sha'ban statement, Imam Khomeini emphasized the necessity of fighting both Marxism and capitalism, as well as deviant interpretations of Islam, while urging reliance on God and acceptance of the path's costs. He argued that hardships are not signs of failure but rather catalysts for great success, and that officials must not retreat out of fear of enemies. Despite global unity against Islam, Iran's steadfastness has weakened the West's soft power and led to the failure of sanctions. The second part narrates the novel "Jameh-ye Dast Pareh," where the protagonist, Salare, encounters his wounded son, Sohrab, in a wheat field during the 1963 uprising. Salare struggles with the fear of identification and the dilemma of saving his son while maintaining secrecy, leading to a tense interaction with his companion, Zabbieh. This narrative highlights the conflict between personal concerns and the political-social struggles of that era.
