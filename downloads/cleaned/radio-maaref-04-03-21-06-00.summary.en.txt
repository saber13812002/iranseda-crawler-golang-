This tafsir of Surah Al-Ma'idah examines the nature of the questions God asks the prophets on the Day of Judgment. These inquiries are not indicative of the prophets' ignorance but serve to rebuke the nations and emphasize their accountability, with the prophets referring the final answer to God's knowledge. The speaker refutes the claim that prophets forget their knowledge on that day, instead affirming their inherent and hidden knowledge. It is explained that hidden knowledge exists in two forms: intrinsic and acquired through revelation, with the Quran frequently attributing such knowledge to the prophets. The text also critiques the interpretations of Sheikh Tusi and Tabarsi regarding the phrase "We have no knowledge," clarifying that it is not a denial of knowledge in general. Finally, the speaker rejects accusations against Shia Islam for attributing intrinsic hidden knowledge to the Imams, asserting that their knowledge of the unseen is derived from divine teachings and revelation, not from their own essence.
