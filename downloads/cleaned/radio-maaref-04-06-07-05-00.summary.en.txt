This radio program argues that the spiritual rewards of seeking knowledge in Islamic traditions are not limited to religious studies but extend to all secular and university-based disciplines. Two essential conditions are highlighted for these rewards to apply: the knowledge must be beneficial to society by addressing its needs, and the student must pursue it with the intention of drawing closer to God and serving humanity. Citing a narration from Imam Kazim, the speaker emphasizes that fulfilling societal needs carries a reward comparable to or even greater than performing the Hajj pilgrimage. The pursuit of knowledge is also framed as a practical response to Quranic calls such as "migration" and "exodus," representing a spiritual departure from egoistic limitations. The discussion then transitions to Mulla Sadra's philosophical concept of the "Imaginal World" (Alam al-Mithal), exploring the intermediate level of existence between the sensory and rational realms. It is argued that mental perceptions possess a distinct existence independent of physical matter and space. The program concludes with a closing prayer and an introduction to upcoming segments, including a philosophy lecture and a morning reflection show.
