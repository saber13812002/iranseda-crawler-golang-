Mohammad Hafezifard reflects on the golden era of the Homa team, highlighting the loyalty of players like Tavanai and Alidoosti to their club. He emphasizes that the team's family-like bonds and weekly gatherings built parental trust and protected young players from social dangers. Hafezifard recounts the intense atmosphere of derby matches against Esteghlal and Persepolis, noting the significant psychological pressure exerted by the fans. He also discusses the crucial role of local dirt pitches in discovering raw talent and fostering the moral development of young athletes. In his view, state-run football lacks financial transparency and genuine competition, necessitating a shift towards privatization for sustainable growth. He criticizes the structural flaws in the federation, particularly the disconnect between youth academies and senior teams and the neglect of experienced coaches in junior categories. Finally, Hafezifard stresses the importance of utilizing the expertise of football veterans to rescue Iranian football from its current crisis.
