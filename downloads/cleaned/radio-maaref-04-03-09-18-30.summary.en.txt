In this story, Zayd and his wife Hanieh have a dispute with Samra, who owns a tree located in their courtyard. Samra enters their home without permission, prompting Zayd to seek a peaceful resolution by referring the matter to the Prophet of Islam. Despite the Prophet's multiple offers to compensate Samra with ten other trees or to purchase the tree, she stubbornly insists on her right to access her specific tree. Upon seeing Samra's refusal to respect the neighbors' rights, the Prophet orders the tree to be uprooted and tells her to plant it elsewhere. This ruling highlights that respecting the privacy and rights of others takes precedence over personal property claims. The Prophet emphasizes that God does not permit even the slightest harm to Muslims. Zayd learns the importance of justice and following religious guidance in maintaining neighborly rights. The story concludes with a prayer for the audience to uphold the rights of others and follow the Prophet's example.
