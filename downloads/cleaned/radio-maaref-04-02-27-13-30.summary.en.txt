The radio program "Andisheha," featuring Dr. Azudin Razanjad, examined the historical formation of the Babi and Baha'i sects. These groups originated from the Shaykhi movement founded by Sheikh Ahmad Ahsa'i, which was later led by Sayyid Kazim Rashti. Following Rashti's death, internal disputes led to the selection of Mirza Ali-Muhammad Shirazi, who initially claimed to be the Bab and later asserted prophethood and the abrogation of Islam. Shia scholars declared him an apostate, and he was executed by Amir Kabir, although foreign powers like Tsarist Russia began interfering in the sect. After the Bab's execution, Mirza Husayn-Ali Nuri (Baha'u'llah) succeeded him and established the foundations of Baha'ism while in exile in Acre. Following Husayn-Ali's death, his son Abbas Effendi (Abdu'l-Baha) transformed Baha'ism into an international organization with direct support from Britain after World War I. The program concluded by emphasizing that these sects have exploited Muslim divisions and foreign support to undermine Islamic resources.
