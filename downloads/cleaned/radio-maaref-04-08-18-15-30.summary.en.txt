This radio program discussed the philosophy and definition of political jurisprudence, emphasizing the need for a precise conceptual framework.
The most critical element in defining jurisprudence is its ultimate objective, or intent, which serves as the benchmark for evaluating its effectiveness.
Unlike materialist views that limit jurisprudence to worldly affairs, the primary goal of Islamic jurisprudence is defined as achieving servitude to God in both this life and the hereafter.
This concept of servitude extends beyond ritual worship to encompass a genuine spiritual state of being a true servant of the Divine.
The Islamic government is tasked with creating an environment that facilitates the people's ability to attain this state of servitude.
In addition to this primary goal, intermediate objectives such as proper governance, promoting good, and forbidding evil are also integral to political jurisprudence.
The scope of Sharia in Islam is comprehensive, governing human life from the cradle to the grave in both worldly and spiritual dimensions.
This holistic perspective renders secularist and positivist approaches, which exclude religion from practical life, insufficient and incorrect.
