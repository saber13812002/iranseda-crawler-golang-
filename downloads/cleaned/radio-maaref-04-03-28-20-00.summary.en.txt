The program examines the challenges of organ donation in Iran and the critical role of cultural awareness in increasing public knowledge.
Statistics reveal that thousands of lives are lost annually due to organ shortages and family unawareness, despite a much higher potential for donation.
Psychologists argue that grief and the fear of confronting death are primary barriers to decision-making, necessitating psychological support and counseling for families.
Sociologists emphasize that transforming organ donation into a social culture is a time-consuming process that requires sustained education and awareness efforts.
Fear of death and a lack of understanding regarding the difference between a coma and brain death are key reasons for public resistance to this act.
To overcome these obstacles, media must create an awareness-driven environment and provide accurate information to shift negative perceptions.
Organ donation is not only a humane and courageous act but also guarantees the saving of lives and the preservation of families.
Ultimately, correcting misconceptions and managing family emotions are the keys to increasing the rate of organ donation in society.
