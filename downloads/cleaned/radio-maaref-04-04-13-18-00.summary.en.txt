The radio program "Ganj-e Sa'adat" begins with an interpretation of a Quranic verse emphasizing the virtue of giving away what one loves most. The guest speaker highlights the practices of Imam Ali and Imam Reza to illustrate that true charity involves sacrificing wealth, status, or even one's soul for God. The martyrdom of Imam Ali Akbar is presented as the ultimate example of this sacrifice, as he offered his life, which was Imam Hussein's most precious possession, on the battlefield. This act of devotion not only deeply affected Imam Hussein but also serves as a model of absolute love and obedience to God. The Prophet Muhammad promised that anyone who gives away their beloved possessions in God's path will be personally rewarded with Paradise on the Day of Judgment. In the second part of the program, a short story titled "The Guardian's Clothes" is read, depicting the emotional bond between a mother and her son, a member of the IRGC. The son references Imam Ali Akbar's martyrdom to address his mother's fears, while she acknowledges his status as a trust from God, recalling a previous divination result. This narrative demonstrates how religious values and self-sacrifice remain vibrant in Iranian families, even during moments of farewell and the fear of losing loved ones.
