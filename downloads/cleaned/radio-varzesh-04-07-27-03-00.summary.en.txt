Mehdi Farani, the former coach of the national junior handball team, expresses regret over tactical errors and the lack of mental preparation that led to a fifth-place finish. He highlights the intense financial and technical competition in Asia, noting that teams like the UAE are disrupting the balance by recruiting European players. Farani also shares his experience coaching in Afghanistan and forming a refugee team in Iran, detailing the financial and administrative challenges involved. He emphasizes the critical need for the education system to support handball to foster its growth and laments the public's lack of appreciation for coaches' hard work. The coach, who has been battling chronic illnesses for years, finds solace in solo travel to escape the pressures of his professional environment. He believes that coaches must control their emotions to maintain order, although they inevitably become emotional during critical moments like player injuries. Farani concludes by reaffirming his selfless love for the sport and his hope for establishing a strong foundation for handball in Iran.
