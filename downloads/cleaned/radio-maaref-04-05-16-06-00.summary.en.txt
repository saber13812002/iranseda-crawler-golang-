This session provided a detailed critical analysis of the hadith "My community will not agree upon error," revealing that all its transmission chains contain significant biographical flaws.
The examination of the narrators demonstrated that many are considered weak, and specific chains, particularly those involving Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn Abbas, appear to be fabricated.
Evidence suggests these narrations were likely constructed during a period when Shia scholars held political power in Baghdad to address legitimacy issues and gain Shia acceptance by including Shia narrators in the chain.
Sunni scholars, while acknowledging the meaning of the hadith, have deemed it weak and indefensible from a chain perspective, with some Shia scholars also labeling it as fabricated.
To compensate for the weak chain, theologians like Al-Ghazali and Fakhr al-Razi have relied on the concept of "meaning-based consensus," arguing that the core message was transmitted through multiple sources.
However, this argument for meaning-based consensus remains debated and cannot serve as a valid substitute for a sound and continuous chain of transmission to the Prophet.
Ultimately, the hadith lacks chain authenticity according to hadith science principles, and the claim of its consensus-based transmission is technically unacceptable.
