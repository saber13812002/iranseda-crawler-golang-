In the first part, Ayatollah Karimi emphasized the necessity of moral guidance by citing a hadith from Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) regarding the transient nature of worldly status and life. He interpreted the Prophet's words about the inevitability of humiliation after honor and death after life as a call to utilize temporary opportunities for spiritual growth and preparation for the afterlife. The Prophet described the world as a mere waystation for travelers, urging believers not to become complacent. The second part of the discussion shifted to a jurisprudential debate on the validity of inferences from indicators (amara). The author of "Mishkat al-Usul" rejected the general validity of such inferences by comparing obligatory knowledge to intuitive knowledge. However, the speaker proposed a nuanced view, arguing that only the intrinsic implications of an indicator are valid, while accidental or incidental implications are not. This distinction addresses previous objections to inductive foundations and highlights the importance of differentiating between indicators and names in legal reasoning.
