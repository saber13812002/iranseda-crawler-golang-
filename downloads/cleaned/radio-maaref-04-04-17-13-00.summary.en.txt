This radio program, dedicated to the mourning period of Imam Sajjad (AS) and the event of Karbala, highlights the central role of this Imam in preserving the message of Ashura. The speaker explains that Imam Sajjad's severe illness on the day of Ashura was a divine necessity to ensure the continuation of Imamate after the massacre of the Prophet's family. His presence in Kufa and Sham, along with his powerful speeches, instilled fear in his enemies and earned the admiration of the people, a fact reflected in the poetry of figures like Farazdaq. For forty years, Imam Sajjad kept the memory of his brother Imam Hussein's (AS) martyrdom alive through constant weeping, fasting, and organizing mourning gatherings. By witnessing the tragic scenes of Karbala and remembering the severed hands of his uncle Abbas (AS), he consistently conveyed a spirit of resilience and grief. The core message is that the culture of Ashura, characterized by steadfastness and resistance against tyranny, is a vital asset for the appearance of Imam Mahdi and the establishment of a global Islamic government. Finally, Imam Hussein's (AS) last will to his son, urging him to never oppress the helpless, is presented as the key moral lesson of this historical event.
