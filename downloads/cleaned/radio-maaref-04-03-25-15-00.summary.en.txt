In this radio program, two jurisprudential experts refute the notion that the ritual impurity of an animal implies its worthlessness or justifies its mistreatment, emphasizing instead the Islamic recognition of animals' right to life and sustenance. Citing religious texts and jurisprudential works, they classify the unjust killing of animals as a grave sin and mandate that owners must provide for their animals' needs. However, they clarify that the freedom to keep pets is not absolute and must be regulated to ensure public health, safety, and the rights of other citizens, particularly in confined spaces like apartments. Islamic jurisprudence, through principles such as the prohibition of harm and injustice, strictly forbids animal abuse and the disruption of social life. The experts also note that developed nations similarly enforce strict regulations and limitations on pet ownership. Consequently, there is no contradiction between the Islamic rights of animals and the necessity of regulating their keeping in modern society.
