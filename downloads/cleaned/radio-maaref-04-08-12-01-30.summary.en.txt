The lecture by Hojjat al-Islam Rostamnejad focuses on the reciprocal impact of belief and action, arguing that correcting one's mindset is a prerequisite for correcting behavior. Citing Quranic verses, he explains that belief in monotheism and God as the provider eliminates the roots of stinginess and shame, thereby encouraging generosity and justice. Action is then presented as a mirror of one's inner self, as individuals behave according to their inherent nature and existential structure. A key point in the discussion is that action is not merely a sign of character but acts like a living entity that influences the individual's future and destiny. This interpretive perspective suggests that our actions, once performed, become agents that reshape our personality. The second part of the program introduces the book "Those Twenty-Three" and shares the captivity memories of Mr. Kababiani. These freed soldiers maintained their dignity and destroyed sensitive documents while standing firm against the hardships and torture inflicted by the enemy.
