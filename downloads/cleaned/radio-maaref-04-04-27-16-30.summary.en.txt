This discussion traces the roots of post-prophetic political conflicts to the event of Saqifah. There, the Muhajirs and Ansar debated succession, with the former ultimately securing power by citing the hadith that leaders must be from Quraysh. Abu Bakr’s invocation of this hadith effectively ended the negotiations, sidelining the Ansar. A crucial aspect of this hadith is its reference to twelve leaders, a point that confuses Sunni scholars who avoid aligning it with the Shia concept of the Twelve Imams. The consequences of this decision manifested in the tragedy of al-Harra, where Yazid and Marwan branded the necks of Ali’s companions. In contrast, Imam Sajjad (AS) maintained neutrality, turning his home into a safe haven for 400 families during the chaos. Even Marwan sought refuge in the Imam’s house and remained unharmed out of respect for him. This approach ensured safety for the Imam and the refugees, standing in stark contrast to the violence perpetrated by the ruling authorities.
