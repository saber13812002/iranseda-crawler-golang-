This radio program begins by recounting the memories of Sharif Qanati, a cleric and resistance commander in Khorramshahr, who saved the city from falling to Ba'athist forces through his bravery and religious leadership. Qanati rejected the enemy's military tactics, emphasizing the power of popular jihad, and was martyred on October 15, 1980, after standing firm against even the army commanders. The program then shifts to the testimony of Ibrahim Farakhnia, a former POW, who describes the harsh conditions and physical abuse endured in Iraqi detention camps. The arrival of Hajj-Agha Aboud Torabi in the camp improved the morale of the detainees by teaching them the proper code of conduct for prisoners of war. In 1988, Saddam Hussein ordered the prisoners to be taken to Karbala and Najaf for negative propaganda purposes. The Iranian POWs accepted this trip conditionally, using the opportunity to distribute religious messages and flags from their buses to the Iraqi public. This act not only neutralized the enemy's propaganda but also moved many Iraqis to tears, impressed by the steadfast faith and dignity of the Iranian captives.
