The text begins by highlighting the pivotal role of the Islamic Revolution in Iran, which reintroduced religion to the public and global sphere. The speaker argues that the primary challenge for Islamic societies is not a lack of theory, but rather a failure in practice and fidelity to principles after the truth has become clear. Citing the sermons of Hazrat Zahra (SA) and Imam Ali (AS), it is explained that hypocrisy and deviation from justice pose the greatest threat to Islamic systems, even after the apparent acceptance of faith. The concept of the "Noble Government" is defined as a system where the dignity of the people takes precedence over the interests of rulers, minimizing the gap between the two. The text also emphasizes the necessity of distinguishing between core principles and trivialities in governance, while avoiding the extremes of secularism and conservatism. Finally, the crucial and unparalleled role of believing women in the revolution's success and the preservation of its values is highlighted.
