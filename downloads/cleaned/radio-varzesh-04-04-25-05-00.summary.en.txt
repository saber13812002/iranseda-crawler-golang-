Mr. Polfarzaneh, former manager of the Revolution Bowling Club, discusses the history and structural challenges of bowling in Iran. He notes that despite producing the first bowling ball in Iran in 2005 and hiring foreign coaches, the sport suffered from a lack of federal support and competition with billiards. Since 2014, the seizure of the Revolution Club and the loss of key coaches have caused a significant drop in player averages from a global standard of 215 to below 200. Farzaneh attributes this decline to a lack of academic infrastructure, a shortage of certified coaches and referees, and insufficient investment in youth talent identification. He highlights the high potential of Iranian players, which was confirmed by international experts during a trip to Finland, and emphasizes the need to separate the bowling federation from billiards. Farzaneh announces his intention to invest in establishing a specialized training center for coaches and players to improve the sport's quality. He believes that focusing on education and talent development will help bowling achieve its rightful place in global competitions.
