This radio program begins by examining the book "Sif al-Amma" by Mulla Ahmad Naraqi, which is regarded as a unique and scholarly response to the doubts raised by English writer Henry Martyn. To write this work, Naraqi mastered Aramaic, Syriac, and Latin, and studied Christian sources to refute Martyn's claims through logical and dialectical arguments. The book consists of three sections and aims to prove the prophethood of the Prophet of Islam by utilizing the opponent's own premises to resolve doubts in the reader's mind. The second part of the program features the memoirs of Malek Deliri, a freedom fighter who was taken prisoner during the Iran-Iraq War. He describes the severe hardships in the prisoner camps, such as extreme thirst and torture, as well as the secret cultural and religious activities conducted by the prisoners during the month of Muharram. Deliri also highlights the moral and material support provided by the prisoners of Karbala 5 in the adjacent camp and the spiritual resistance of the captives against the enemy's psychological and physical pressures. Finally, the book "Your Name is Mustafa," which narrates the life of martyr Mostafa Sadrzadeh from his wife's perspective, is introduced.
