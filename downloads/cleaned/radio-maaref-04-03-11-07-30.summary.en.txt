In this radio program, the importance of logical decision-making and prioritizing tasks in a believer's life was emphasized through a complex ethical scenario.
The key point is that when facing others' needs, one should not act purely on emotion but manage conflicts by considering logical solutions, such as coordinating with the person's relatives or using public services.
The discussion then expanded to the concepts of "humility" and "self-improvement," explaining that true humility must be rooted in free will rather than coercion.
Humility for the sake of God is a virtue that should not be used as a cover to hide internal flaws or deficiencies.
One of the main ways to avoid criticizing others is to pay continuous attention to one's own faults and engage in self-improvement.
A person should be like a farmer tending to their spiritual field, mastering their ego and moral vices.
Finally, the call to virtues must be accompanied by action, as individual self-improvement serves as the best role model for others.
