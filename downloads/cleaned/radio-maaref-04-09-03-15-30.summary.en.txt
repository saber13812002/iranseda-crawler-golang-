In this radio program titled "Fiqh Payva," the philosophy, definition, and history of political jurisprudence were discussed.
A key segment analyzed the historical 1965 dialogue between Imam Khomeini and Ayatollah Seyed Kazem Hakim.
Both scholars agreed that establishing an Islamic government is a religious duty during the period of occultation.
Their main disagreement concerned the timing of the uprising; Imam Khomeini advocated for immediate action, while Ayatollah Hakim cautioned against hasty moves due to potential bloodshed.
Imam Khomeini argued that shedding blood for the sake of establishing an Islamic government is valuable, citing the uprising of Imam Hussein as a precedent.
Political jurisprudence was presented as a contemporary necessity and a continuation of the Islamic duty to enjoin good and forbid evil.
The active involvement of jurists in politics, including the efforts of Ayatollah Khomeini and other scholars, was highlighted as a significant step in this path.
The program concluded with the recitation and translation of verses from Surah Al-Ma'idah, emphasizing monotheism and the rejection of shirk.
