This program examines the rational foundations of the rule "repelling probable harm" in both Mu'tazili and Imami schools of thought. The Mu'tazili scholar Qadi Abd al-Jabbar argues that repelling harm, whether certain or probable, is a rational obligation based on intellect. He identifies the "obligation of reflection" to know God and the "obligation of repentance" to avoid eternal punishment as the primary applications of this rule. Furthermore, he asserts that when multiple methods exist to avert harm, reason dictates choosing the one requiring the least effort, or allows for choice if options are equal. The later Mu'tazili scholar Mulla Hameed Khwazmi reinforces this view, emphasizing the necessity of learning religious duties to prevent harm caused by ignorance. In the Imami tradition, scholars from Sheikh Mufid to Allama Hilli have accepted this rule, applying it to concepts like taqiyya, the necessity of Imamate, and repentance. Over time, these theological discussions gradually merged into fiqh and usul works, gaining a more practical application.
