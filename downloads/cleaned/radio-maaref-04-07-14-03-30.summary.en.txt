In this program, Professor Shakarin discussed the criticisms directed at the cosmological theory of the multiverse and the principle of indeterminacy. He categorized these critiques into two main groups: the theory's underlying assumptions and its failure to meet scientific standards. Key objections include conceptual ambiguities, the lack of falsifiability, and the inability to make precise scientific predictions. The "Boltzmann Brain" paradox was also highlighted as a significant epistemological challenge, suggesting that disembodied minds with hallucinated memories are statistically more probable than real, embodied minds. This logic leads to absolute skepticism, making it impossible for an individual to verify the reality of their own experiences. Consequently, the multiverse theory is deemed unacceptable due to this logical and epistemic self-contradiction. The second part of the program addressed various jurisprudential questions from listeners regarding financial support for mothers, prayer rulings, and the distribution of Khums.
