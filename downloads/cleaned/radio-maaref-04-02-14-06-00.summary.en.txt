The program clarifies the fundamental difference between Istikhara and Tafal, emphasizing that Istikhara is a permissible tool for resolving uncertainty and finding spiritual peace, whereas Tafal, which seeks material gain through divination, is strictly prohibited.
Makassed Ardabili is cited to reinforce that Istikhara does not imply claiming knowledge of the unseen or believing that God's decree is limited, but rather serves to instill hope and remove hesitation in personal matters.
The discussion then shifts to the jurisprudential rulings on hunting during Ihram in Surah Al-Ma'idah, where intentional hunting of land animals is declared forbidden for pilgrims.
The Quran uses this prohibition as a divine test to evaluate believers' faith in the unseen and their reverence for God, using both small and large game as the means of this trial.
It is highlighted that God possesses active knowledge of the deepest secrets and intentions of humans, meaning He does not need tests to understand His creation, but rather to distinguish the pious from the transgressors.
This divine examination aims to identify those who fear God in the unseen and adhere to His limits, contrasting them with those who overstep boundaries.
Finally, it is concluded that true knowledge of the unseen belongs exclusively to God and the divinely appointed saints, and no human method can uncover these hidden truths.
