The radio program begins by emphasizing the importance of learning from past mistakes, citing a story by Ayatollah Yazdi about a scholar who wept for failing to learn from his own history. The discussion then shifts to the exegesis of a verse from Surah Yunus, which condemns the claim that God has a child as "speaking without knowledge." The commentator defines the Quranic term "Sultan" as logical proof or evidence, based on Ibn Abbas's interpretation, establishing that the Quran's motto is to demand proof for one's claims. Consequently, the method of inviting people to faith must be grounded in wisdom, logic, and rational argumentation rather than baseless assertions. It is argued that a Muslim's belief in the fundamental principles of religion must be derived from logical reasoning that leads to certainty. The concept of "speaking without knowledge" is further extended to include giving religious rulings without knowing the opinion of one's marja, which is strictly prohibited. Specifically during Ramadan, making false claims about God or the Prophet, or answering religious questions without proper knowledge, can invalidate one's fast. Therefore, listeners are urged to be cautious and avoid transmitting hadiths or rulings without verifying their authenticity and source.
