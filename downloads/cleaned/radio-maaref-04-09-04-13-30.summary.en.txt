This radio program recounts the captivity memories of Mr. Esadollah Nekhai, detailing the harsh conditions and physical torture endured in Iraqi camps such as Mosul and Salah al-Din. The prisoners faced severe shortages of water, food, and fresh air, often having to break window panes just to breathe. Despite these hardships, a spirit of self-sacrifice and altruism prevailed among the captives, who frequently shared their meager rations and cared for the wounded at night. One of the most dramatic scenes involved the brutal torture of Ajahan Torabi, whose calm resistance prevented an Iraqi officer from executing the group. These memories illustrate that captivity served as a university for cultivating Quranic ethics and a sense of freedom rather than merely a period of suffering. The program concludes by emphasizing that the Fatimi model of selfless service remains alive in society and among the younger generations.
