The speaker critiques the materialistic and symbolic view of the Sacred Defense, arguing that its true value lies in its spiritual essence rather than specific locations or objects. He asserts that martyrdom and jihad should not be reduced to historical nostalgia but must be understood as a living, ongoing truth. From his perspective, Islam is simultaneously a religion of peace with humanity and a religion of war against aggressors and oppressors; thus, taking up arms is permissible and even a divine honor only when defending justice, rights, and the oppressed. In this context, jihad serves as the "garment of piety," protecting the community's moral values against corruption and tyranny. The speaker notes that current regional conflicts are a continuation of this struggle against power-seekers, rather than a concluded past event. Finally, he identifies the Quran as the primary source for resolving the society's moral, social, and political crises, emphasizing the necessity of drawing from this source to address both internal and international challenges.
