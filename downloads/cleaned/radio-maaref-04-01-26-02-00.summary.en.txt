In this program, Professor Mohammadi refutes the claim that the Quran lacks a specific threat of punishment for not wearing hijab, citing the Mi'raj narrations and emphasizing that the Quran provides general principles while details are found in hadiths. He explains that even if not wearing hijab is considered a minor sin, persisting in it or committing it openly elevates it to a major sin. The issue is also analyzed from a political-security perspective, where hijab is viewed as a critical front for maintaining the integrity of Islamic society against external infiltration. The program further addresses listener questions regarding whether ancestors see their descendants' sins, the concept of genetics in Islamic narrations, and the obligation to obey the Supreme Leader as the deputy of the Imam. The professor dispels the notion of boredom in Paradise by highlighting its infinite variety and eternal nature, contrasting it with the limited pleasures of this world. Finally, he offers guidance for parents on teaching monotheism to children, recommending the use of simple analogies like the argument from order and the inherent saltiness of salt to prove God's existence.
