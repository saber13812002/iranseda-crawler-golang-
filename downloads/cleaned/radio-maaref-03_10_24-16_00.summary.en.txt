This radio program explains the concept of "persuasion" as the key to changing adolescent behavior and internalizing values.
The most effective strategy for persuasion is to create or remind the individual of fundamental needs, such as security, rather than using scolding or threats.
Building a closer relationship with teenagers requires full acceptance, two-way communication, and granting them a sense of freedom.
In response to listener questions, the host emphasized avoiding negative labels like "liar" or "thief" and instead identifying the hidden needs behind the behavior.
For six-year-olds facing behavioral challenges in preschool, explaining the situation clearly and engaging in logical dialogue is more effective than punishment.
Affirming and encouraging positive behaviors through meaningful looks and simple words significantly boosts teenagers' self-confidence and intimacy.
Finally, creating a safe space for admitting mistakes prevents teenagers from hiding the truth and lying, thereby smoothing the path for behavioral correction.
