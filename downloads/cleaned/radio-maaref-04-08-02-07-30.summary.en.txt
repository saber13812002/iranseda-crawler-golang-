In this radio program, Hojjat al-Islam Falahab discusses the dangerous vice of "judgment" rooted in egoistic desires. He describes judgment as a pervasive phenomenon that extends from questioning God and prophets to criticizing family, society, and even oneself. Citing religious narrations, the speaker highlights how the Jews' false accusations against Prophet Moses and the Quraysh's against Prophet Muhammad exemplify this moral flaw. A key point is that misjudging God essentially means constructing a false deity that contradicts the truth of monotheism. Furthermore, judging oneself as "unlucky" or "unique" is identified as a form of this vice that diminishes one's spiritual stature. The primary solution involves continuous self-improvement, refraining from judging others, and avoiding situations that invite criticism. The speaker advises against focusing on misfortune and instead emphasizes relying on love for the Ahl al-Bayt and trust in God. This approach fosters spiritual growth and inner peace rather than self-deprecation.
