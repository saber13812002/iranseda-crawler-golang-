Sajjad Ghanzadeh, a prominent Iranian karateka, suffered a hand injury during training just two days before the Olympic competition, placing significant physical and mental pressure on him. Team coach Seyed Shohrab Heravi discussed the team's persistent efforts to secure a gold medal quota for the athlete amidst various challenges. Regarding the doping case of Bahman Eskari, Heravi clarified that while WADA confirmed the use of the substance, the ban was reduced to one year due to its therapeutic nature and lack of intent to enhance performance. In the final match, Ghanzadeh, despite his technical superiority, was struck by an illegal and heavy blow from his Saudi Arabian opponent, which caused him to lose consciousness and fall to the ground. The Japanese medical staff present at the scene determined that Ghanzadeh was unable to continue, leading to the opponent's disqualification for the illegal strike. This incident was viewed as a moral and legal victory for Ghanzadeh in defending his rights, despite the physical setback. Heravi also highlighted Ghanzadeh's history of resilience in global competitions, noting that he had previously continued competing even with a broken nose.
