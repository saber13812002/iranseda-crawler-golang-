In this radio program, Dr. Mohammad Malekzadeh argues that US-Iran negotiations are not aimed at peace but serve as a tool to contain independent powers and reduce military costs. He warns that this tactic may create internal divisions in Iran by misleading moderate forces who believe the US seeks de-escalation. The Iraqi elections were analyzed as an opportunity to promote a resistance-based nationalism focused on fighting corruption and foreign dependence. The status of Hezbollah in Lebanon was also discussed, emphasizing that the group has maintained its social standing and deterrent power despite significant economic and political pressures. Finally, Zohran Mamdani's victory in the New York mayoral election was interpreted as a sign of the growing influence of social Islam in the West. This event demonstrates that the resistance narrative has successfully penetrated beyond regional borders to impact the domestic politics of Western powers.
