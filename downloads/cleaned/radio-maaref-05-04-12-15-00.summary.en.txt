The text combines a religious verse with a philosophical lecture on the foundations of human rights. The speaker argues that human rights are rooted in divine revelation and the inherent dignity of humans, lacking a solid philosophical base when disconnected from religion. He emphasizes that Islam defined human rights with specific details, extending beyond the contractual generalities of the West to include the rights of animals and objects. In contrast, modern Western human rights, derived from the natural law school, became unstable and experimental after secularization, lacking even consensus within the West itself. The speaker identifies fundamental differences in anthropology and sources of knowledge as the primary causes of disputes over the definition and scope of human rights. He also highlights political double standards in the application of these rights by Western powers and criticizes the superficial approach of religious seminaries in using narrations for practical societal issues. Finally, the text stresses that justice and alleviating people's needs are not only social duties but also a path to divine proximity and the realization of God's justice.
