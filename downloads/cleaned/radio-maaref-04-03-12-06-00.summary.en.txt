Ayatollah Javadi Amoli, in his exegesis of the Surah of Light, categorizes the Quran's guidance into two paths: acquired knowledge based on reasoning and intuitive knowledge based on presence. He emphasizes that sections related to the purification of the soul and the knowledge of prophets are rooted in intuitive, direct experience rather than mere intellectual deduction. The scholar argues that the divine essence cannot be grasped through mental concepts, as God lacks a specific nature, genus, or definition, and can only be known through direct spiritual presence. Citing narrations from Sheikh Saduq and Sheikh Mufid, he demonstrates that even rational proofs ultimately depend on this intuitive knowledge, as the tools of cognition themselves are created by God. The text also references Sheikh Mufid's critique of Sheikh Saduq, highlighting that purely rational arguments are insufficient for establishing monotheism and that revelation is essential for completing human knowledge. Furthermore, the distinction is drawn between forgettable worldly sciences and the innate divine nature that remains intact on the Day of Judgment. The conclusion is that while human nature is inherently inclined toward its Creator, understanding hidden truths and specific details requires the guidance of revelation and prophets. Thus, reason and innate nature serve as foundational elements that are strengthened and completed by divine revelation to achieve comprehensive knowledge.
