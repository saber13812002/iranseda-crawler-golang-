The radio program begins by highlighting the critical importance of managing fear in children to ensure their long-term mental and physical health.
An expert in Islamic traditional medicine warns that severe fear and sleep disturbances can weaken a child's heart and lead to a fragile personality.
Parents are advised to avoid suppressing children's natural energy and play, as doing so can root deep-seated anxiety and spiritual weakness.
The second segment focuses on the virtue of humility and the dangers of arrogance, explained by a Quranic scholar.
He cites Quranic verses to argue that pride and boasting exclude individuals from God's love and hinder both spiritual and material growth.
The discussion then shifts to the historical dynamics between universities and religious seminaries in modern Iran.
It is explained that while universities were influenced by Western ideologies, seminaries maintained independence by avoiding direct political involvement.
These distinct approaches resulted in different educational outcomes and shaped the generations emerging from each institution.
