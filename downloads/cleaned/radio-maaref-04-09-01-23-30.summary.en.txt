This program recounts Ayatollah Fazel Golpayegani's childhood memories to illustrate the self-sufficient and natural lifestyle of rural communities. The narrative details agricultural practices such as grinding wheat with clay pots and winnowing, alongside livestock management using winter shelters. The production of fresh dairy products and a traditional barter system replacing cash transactions highlight the economic resilience of the village. Homes were constructed using local materials like mud, brick, and wood, while qanat water was utilized for irrigation, demonstrating optimal use of natural resources. The absence of hospitals meant that illnesses were treated with local herbal remedies known by the villagers. In the concluding segment, the speaker addresses medical staff, emphasizing the profound value of caring for patients and war casualties. This service is described as not only ethically significant but also highly rewarding in the eyes of God.
