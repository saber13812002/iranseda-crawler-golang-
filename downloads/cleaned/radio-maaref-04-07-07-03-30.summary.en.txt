In this program, the multiverse theory and the random origin of the universe, proposed by Hawking and Bartsman, were examined and critiqued. Professor Shakarin argued that this view lacks scientific validity because it relies on extremely weak probabilities and incorrect assumptions, such as the eternity of time. Furthermore, the theory conflicts with the principle of uncertainty and modern physics foundations, failing to provide a coherent explanation. A major criticism is the conceptual ambiguity in defining the "universe" and the impossibility of empirical testing or observation. According to the philosophy of science, a theory that is not falsifiable and cannot be tested with empirical evidence is not considered scientific. Therefore, this theory has more of a philosophical nature than a scientific one and cannot replace precise cosmological explanations. The second part of the program was dedicated to answering religious questions from followers of various clerical authorities. Topics such as the conditions for valid congregational prayer, the rules of Khums on assets and food items, and the purity of well water in case of contamination were discussed and answered.
