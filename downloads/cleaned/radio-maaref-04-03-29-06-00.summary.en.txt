This program critically examines the arguments of Fakhr Razi and Shokani regarding the validity of consensus (Ijma) based on the Quranic verse "You are the best nation." The first argument posits that since God describes the Ummah as enjoining good and forbidding evil, their consensus on any matter inherently makes it good and valid. However, the primary objection is that the verse cannot refer to all Muslims, as the majority are sinners and do not possess this ideal trait. Therefore, the "Ummah" in this context refers to a specific group of infallibles or perfect followers, not the general Muslim population. The second argument, which compares the Ummah to the people of Moses, faces the same issue that the verse defines the criteria for excellence rather than establishing the inherent authority of consensus. Crucially, the verse functions as a condition or cause for the Ummah's excellence, rather than presenting consensus as an independent source of Sharia. Finally, a linguistic analysis of the term "Ummah" reveals that it signifies loyal followers of an Imam, implying it does not necessarily include all Muslims or even all scholars.
