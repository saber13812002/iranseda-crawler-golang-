The program begins by exploring the historical and literary tradition of "Arba'in writing," which dates back to the second century AH and evolved from narrations on the virtues of the Ahl al-Bayt to theological and ethical discussions. Imam Khomeini's book "Forty Hadiths," completed in 1979, is highlighted as a prominent contemporary work containing 33 ethical and 7 theological hadiths. The book's style involves translation, lexical explanation, and detailed analysis with a preaching and awakening approach. The program then shifts to the memoirs of Behrouz Emadi, a prisoner of war from the Iran-Iraq War, who describes the hardships, torture, and starvation in the camps. Emadi recounts the prisoners' efforts to memorize the Quran and maintain fasting, as well as their resistance against staged performances for foreign journalists, which resulted in their punishment. He emphasizes the spirit of solidarity, unity, and moral integrity among the prisoners in the face of the enemy, noting their deep attachment to the Ahl al-Bayt. Finally, the book "Just Cry" about the life of martyr Mohammad Mamarian's mother is introduced, and the program concludes by inviting listener feedback.
