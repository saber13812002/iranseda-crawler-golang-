In this radio program commemorating the birth of Imam Hadi, Ayatollah Javadi Amoli interprets verses from Surah Al-Ma'idah to emphasize the necessity of justice in legislation, execution, judgment, testimony, and oaths. He distinguishes between "juridical justice," which involves performing obligatory acts and avoiding prohibitions, and "ethical justice," which requires balancing the soul's faculties to avoid extremes. The lecture clarifies that judges and witnesses must be just, and it is obligatory for a just witness to testify in a court of justice, whereas an oath-taker need not be ethically perfect but must swear truthfully. It is explained that prophets will appear as witnesses to their followers' deeds on the Day of Judgment, where God will question them about the people's response to their calls. This process illustrates that the world is a container for recording deeds, while the Day of Judgment is the venue for final testimony and accountability. Finally, it is noted that absolute and comprehensive knowledge belongs solely to God, as the prophets, despite their role as witnesses, do not possess God's intrinsic and absolute knowledge of the unseen.
