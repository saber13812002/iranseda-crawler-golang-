This radio program discussed the legal and jurisprudential challenges of providing maintenance for the elderly, focusing on Article 1234 of the Civil Code.
The guests emphasized that the limited definition of maintenance in this article (housing, clothing, and food) implies an exhaustive list, failing to cover vital needs like medical treatment and nursing care.
Citing the practice of the Muslim community and fatwas from religious authorities, they argued that maintenance should encompass all customary and necessary needs, not just those explicitly mentioned in the law.
Dr. Ahansafari highlighted the ethical perspectives of prominent jurists like Shahab al-Jawahir, stressing the need for a humanistic view of illness and aging as inevitable aspects of life.
A notable point raised was that parents rarely file abandonment lawsuits against their children, despite having this legal right, often due to their own compassion.
Ultimately, it was suggested that the Civil Code needs revision to define maintenance as illustrative rather than exhaustive, ensuring adequate support for vulnerable elderly individuals.
It was also noted that if children lack financial capacity, the responsibility for supporting the elderly shifts to society and the government.
