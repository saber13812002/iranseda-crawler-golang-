The text begins by examining the relationship between reason and revelation, arguing that reason is crucial for understanding religious principles and distinguishing miracles from occult sciences, yet it relies on revelation for specific details.
The discussion then shifts to the concept of uprising during the occultation of the Twelfth Imam, interpreting narrations that declare such uprisings invalid.
The author clarifies that these narrations refer to personal uprisings or calls to self-leadership, rather than movements that invite people to the authority of the Marja or the Imam.
The governance of Imam Ali (AS) is presented as a successful model, superior to the Prophet's era in terms of implementing religious laws.
The text analyzes the managerial differences between Ali's (AS) capable administrators, such as Malik Ashtar and Kumayl, and less effective officials.
In the third section, the absolute authority of the Faqih in worldly affairs and human-made laws is explained.
A key point is that a Faqih must possess the art of governance and management in addition to jurisprudential knowledge, which distinguishes a true leader from a mere scholar.
Finally, the process of selecting the leader by the Assembly of Experts is described as introducing a qualified individual to the people, rather than a direct popular vote.
