The first section emphasizes the necessity of internal struggle against the ego and following God's path rather than personal desires.
Ayatollah Makarem Shirazi, citing the Quranic verse on seeking means to God, identifies knowledge, worship, noble ethics, and spiritual poverty as the four pillars of divine connection.
Imam Ali (AS) is presented as the ultimate model and the true means for connecting with God, urging followers to emulate his conduct.
The story of a Muslim's resistance against Hujjaj's torture to preserve his Shia faith is highlighted as a symbol of steadfast loyalty to the Imamate.
The second part shifts to a fiqh discussion on the principle of Istihsab regarding the precedence and delay of events.
In the first scenario, uncertainty about when an event occurred is resolved by assuming its non-occurrence in the earlier time and its occurrence in the later time.
In the second scenario, the relationship between two events is analyzed through various jurisprudential arguments, such as hidden intermediaries and concomitance.
The conclusion stresses the importance of combining self-discipline with following the infallibles' example to achieve divine proximity and understand legal rulings.
