During the Umayyad Caliphate, Islamic society suffered a profound collapse in intellectual, moral, and religious standards, with the public displaying indifference or hostility toward the Ahl al-Bayt due to negative propaganda. In this repressive environment, Imam Sajjad (AS) employed the strategy of taqiyya and avoided direct confrontation, instead using innovative methods such as training freed slaves and sending loyal companions to various regions to preserve and expand the concept of Wilayah. His most significant legacy is the Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya, which utilized the language of prayer to revive religious knowledge and ethics, thereby elevating the spiritual level of the community. By continuously commemorating Imam Hussein (AS) and engaging in political weeping, Imam Sajjad (AS) kept the memory of Karbala alive and laid the groundwork for the open emergence of Shia identity during the era of Imam Baqir (AS). These efforts demonstrate that Imam Sajjad (AS) was not a passive figure but the greatest mujahid in the darkness of that period. Following this, a narrative from the book "Sayyid Man Husayni" was broadcast, depicting the spiritual journey of a teacher to Karbala and the profound impact of pilgrimage on his soul. This story illustrates that Karbala remains a focal point of love and spirituality for Muslims, where every pilgrim seeks a role model to become truly "Karbalaan."
