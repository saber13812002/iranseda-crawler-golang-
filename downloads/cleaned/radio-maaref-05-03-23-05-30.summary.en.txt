In the radio program "Makarem-e Khoban," Ayatollah Mottahari used a narration from the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) to illustrate the worthlessness of the world in the eyes of God. He emphasized that if the world were worth even a mosquito's wing to God, not even a sip of water would be given to disbelievers. This statement highlights that worldly blessings are merely tools for testing, while true value lies in the eternal matters of the afterlife. The program then transitioned to "Amir Janha," focusing on the virtues of Imam Ali (AS) and his strict avoidance of oppression. Hujjat al-Islam Hosseini, relying on Nahj al-Balagha, stressed that Imam Ali's justice and anti-oppression stance serve as a model for all Shia. He recounted the story of the Imam refusing to give his brother Aqil more than his rightful share from the public treasury, demonstrating his integrity even toward relatives. Furthermore, Imam Ali (AS) rejected a gift intended to corrupt him, declaring he would not violate the rights of even a mosquito, even if offered the wealth of the entire world. Ultimately, the program's core message centered on the necessity of practicing justice, avoiding tyranny, and standing firm against oppressors in the lives of Shias.
