The program identifies militarism as a key indicator of great civilizations, emphasizing that it must serve justice and humanity rather than oppression. Citing Nahj al-Balagha and the letter to Malik Ashtar, the Islamic army is defined as "Jund Allah," a force dedicated to protecting religion, national security, and the dignity of sovereignty. This army is distinguished from colonial forces by its ethical foundation, discipline, and commitment to international law, acting as a liberator rather than a tool of subjugation. It functions as an integral part of a social network, interdependent with institutions like commerce and the judiciary to ensure societal stability. The discussion highlights that a faithful army remains resilient even without logistical support due to its strong ideological and popular base. The broadcast concludes with a narrative from the book "Ocean of the East," where the protagonist, upon hearing of Imam Reza's martyrdom in Nishapur, realizes that true salvation and the "water of life" lie in accepting the Imam's authority.
