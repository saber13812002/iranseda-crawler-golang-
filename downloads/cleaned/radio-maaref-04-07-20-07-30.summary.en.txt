This radio program begins by highlighting a recent study indicating that daily consumption of diet sodas increases the risk of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease by up to sixty percent, even more than regular sodas. Dental experts also emphasized that failing to use dental floss and bacterial buildup on the tongue are primary causes of bad breath that brushing alone cannot resolve. A significant portion of the discussion, coinciding with the commemoration of Hafez Shirazi, explored his profound connection to the Quran and how his poetic style was influenced by the Quran's eloquence. Subsequently, in the "Ganj-e Sa'adat" segment, Hujjat al-Islam Fallah analyzed Quranic verses regarding "ta'en" (sarcasm) and "shamat" (mockery), stressing that criticism which exceeds its limits and causes harm to others is a moral sin. Citing narrations from the Ahl al-Bayt, he noted that speech and tone are considered part of one's actions and that public or repetitive criticism should be avoided. The key takeaway was that while reprimand may sometimes be necessary for education, any mockery intended to hurt or done out of habit is forbidden, and one must control their thoughts to prevent such behavior.
