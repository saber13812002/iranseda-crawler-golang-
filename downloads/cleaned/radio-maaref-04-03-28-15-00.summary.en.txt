In this radio program, experts discussed the priority of women's rights in law while examining the status of international law and humanitarian rights during wartime. They argued that although full compliance with these rights is difficult during conflict, with often only about 20 percent being observed, even this partial adherence is vital for reducing violence and saving civilian lives. Dr. Ghaemi compared the crimes of the Zionist regime to Saddam Hussein's invasion of Iran, emphasizing the necessity of legal pursuit and prosecution of war criminals by the International Criminal Court. Dr. Tashkari referenced the jurisprudential text "Bab Jihad," demonstrating that Islam defined principles such as the distinction between combatants and non-combatants and the observance of ethics in war before international conventions were established. He noted that Islamic teachings from the early Islamic period and the Crusades laid the foundation for humanitarian rules in international relations. Ultimately, both experts believe that Islamic jurisprudence pioneered the formulation of ethical rules of war, and modern international law is largely indebted to this intellectual heritage.
