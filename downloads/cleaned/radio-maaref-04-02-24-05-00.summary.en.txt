This radio program discusses the different levels of "khawf" (fear) of God in Islamic theology.
It explains that while fear of hellfire is necessary for maintaining faith and avoiding sin, the fear of being deprived of divine proximity and God's pleasure is a higher station reserved for God's saints.
The speaker cites Quranic verses to argue that God's indifference on the Day of Judgment is the worst punishment for disbelievers, noting that saints, despite their infallibility, fear missing out on these spiritual states.
The discussion then shifts to the jurisprudence of Hajj and Umrah, specifically addressing what becomes permissible after the act of "taqsir" (shortening hair).
The majority of jurists hold that all prohibitions of Ihram are lifted except for women and perfume, although some scholars from both Sunni and Shia schools have accepted that perfume also becomes permissible after taqsir.
The segment concludes by referencing various narrations on this topic and emphasizing the need for a detailed examination of jurisprudential evidence to reach a final consensus.
