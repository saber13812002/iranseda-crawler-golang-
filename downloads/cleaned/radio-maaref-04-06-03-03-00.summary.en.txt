The program begins by critically analyzing Stephen Hawking's "no-boundary" theory of time, which faces significant scientific, philosophical, and theological challenges. Critics argue that the theory lacks a solid scientific foundation and that the concept of "imaginary time" at the Big Bang is a fallacy contradicting both philosophical analysis and human intuition. Logical inconsistencies in defining the sequence of imaginary and real time have been highlighted by researchers such as Florian Forestier. In the second part, the program discusses the jurisprudential obligations of doctors in saving patients' lives, classifying it as a collective obligation (wajib kifa'i). While the primary responsibility lies with medical professionals, it shifts to relatives and the general public if professional help is unavailable. Finally, the show addresses listeners' religious questions, covering prayer and fasting rules for migrants, wudu procedures for those with broken bones, and the permissibility of giving alms to Sayyids.
