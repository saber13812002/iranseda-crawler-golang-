The program began with congratulations for Nurses' Day and well-wishes for aid caravans, followed by a lecture by the late Hojjat al-Islam Erfan on the occasion of Fatimiyyah.
Erfan refuted feminist claims that Islam diminishes women's status, highlighting the prominent roles of early Muslim women like Khadijah (SA) and Hazrat Masoumeh (SA) in faith, martyrdom, and migration.
He argued that Islamic history demonstrates women achieved spiritual and social heights equal to or surpassing those of men, countering modern narratives of female oppression.
The speaker emphasized the spiritual greatness of Hazrat Masoumeh (SA), citing stories of great scholars like Mulla Sadra and Ayatollah Najafi Marashi who sought her intercession to resolve philosophical and worldly issues.
He also discussed the political and safe-haven role of Hazrat Masoumeh's shrine in Iranian history, noting it served as a recognized sanctuary for political refugees in various eras.
The lecture concluded with a prayer for the fulfillment of Shia needs through Hazrat Masoumeh's intercession and the destruction of Islam's enemies.
