In this program, Professor Taleh Javadi interprets verses 163 to 166 of Surah An-Nisa, emphasizing the principle of universal prophethood. He argues that no nation has ever been left without a divine guide, supported by both rational and scriptural evidence. God's guidance may manifest through the appointment of a prophet, the preservation of scripture and tradition, or the presence of infallible successors. The verse stating that prophets are sent in the language of their people does not limit their mission to a specific tribe but highlights their cultural familiarity and the universal nature of their call. The repeated sending of prophets is essential to prevent human objection on the Day of Judgment and to guide people through good news and warnings. The subtle distinction between "obligatory upon God" and "necessary from God" is clarified to show that God acts out of His pure goodness rather than external compulsion. Finally, Fakhr Razi's interpretation of the verse regarding divine accountability is critiqued, reaffirming the necessity of a divine argument for establishing justice and human salvation.
