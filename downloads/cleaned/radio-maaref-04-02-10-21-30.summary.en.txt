This program analyzes verses 90 to 93 of Surah Al-Ma'idah to discuss the Islamic rulings on alcohol, gambling, and the pre-Islamic practice of Azlam.
The commentator refutes the view of Al-Manar regarding the gradual prohibition of alcohol, arguing that the Quran declared its absolute illegality from the beginning using terms like "abomination" and "great sin."
The concept of gradualism in the Quran refers to the transition from Meccan to Medinan revelations and the clarification of details, rather than a step-by-step change in the ruling itself.
Gambling is described as "Maysir" because it involves acquiring wealth easily without labor, offering a false benefit that disrupts a person's relationship with God and society.
Any game involving an element of chance, whether using traditional or modern tools, falls under the prohibition of Maysir.
The term "Azlam" in these verses refers specifically to the sticks used for gambling and is fundamentally different from the Islamic practice of Istikhara.
Istikhara differs from the pre-Islamic practice of seeking knowledge of the unseen and is based on rational thought, spiritual inspiration, or consultation with experts.
Ultimately, abstaining from alcohol and gambling is essential to preserve both divine and human relationships, as these substances impair reason and social conduct.
