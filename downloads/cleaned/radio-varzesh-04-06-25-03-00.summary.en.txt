Ali Qardashi, the president of the Iranian Table Tennis Federation, reflects on his tenure and a pivotal 1999 trip to North Korea. He was impressed by the high technical level of North Korean players despite their lack of resources, even noting their use of patched rackets. Upon returning to Iran, he facilitated the arrival of North Korean coaches and players, which significantly elevated the sport's standard in the country. Qardashi also discusses the introduction of foreign players to the Iranian league, a move that helped domestic teams improve through high-level training. He recounts the 2014 election where he won with 35 out of 39 valid votes, highlighting the collaborative efforts of his campaign team. After serving for ten years, Qardashi states he has no intention of running for a fourth term, believing that capable successors have been nurtured within the federation. Despite ongoing sanctions and financial challenges, he remains optimistic about the sport's future, predicting potential Olympic and world medal successes in the coming years.
