Abbas Aghachaki discusses the impact of Iran's four-year ban on international competitions, which paradoxically led to the development of stronger youth teams. He highlights the 2012 Asian Youth Championship victory and the subsequent qualification for the World Championship, marking a significant leap for Iranian wheelchair basketball. Aghachaki emphasizes that players' physical dedication and the improvement of wheelchair technology were crucial in overcoming resource gaps against more advanced teams. He also credits his role in changing Iran's geographic zone in the global federation, which facilitated player transfers to European leagues and enhanced technical levels. Simultaneously coaching national teams from Malaysia, Thailand, and Afghanistan on a fly-in basis helped these nations improve their Asian rankings. He takes pride in receiving Afghanistan's National Hero award and the gratitude of the disabled community for his developmental efforts. Aghachaki also reflects on his 24-year continuous commitment to the Do Khani club and the establishment of basketball academies that produced stars like Sina Vahidi. Finally, he expresses hope for the continued growth of Iranian basketball and the support of young players, underscoring his lifelong dedication to the sport.
