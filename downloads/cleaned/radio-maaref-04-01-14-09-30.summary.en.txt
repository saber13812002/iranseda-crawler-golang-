The program argues that negotiating with hegemonic powers like the United States is a form of shameful surrender, promoted by enemies as part of a cognitive war. The speaker distinguishes between indefensible ideologies like Zionism and Islam, which is rooted in justice and support for the oppressed, emphasizing the need to preserve Islamic identity. The issue of hijab is presented as a symbol of identity and resistance against the West's de-Islamization efforts, which aim to weaken the Islamic Republic. The second part of the show honors the life and service of Ayatollah Hashemi Olya, the founder of the Imam Mahdi Seminary in Tehran. Despite severe pressure from the SAVAK and harsh pre-revolutionary conditions, he maintained a scientific and educational stronghold in the Shamsirpol district through relentless dedication. Students recall his rigorous teaching style, mastery of Arabic literature, and his high moral character and humility as a model of a devout scholar. The seminary not only supported the front lines during the war by sending fighters and supplies but also cultivated committed generations by focusing on moral education. His legacy is defined by his selfless devotion to God, his role as a revolutionary figure, and his enduring impact on the Islamic scholarly community.
