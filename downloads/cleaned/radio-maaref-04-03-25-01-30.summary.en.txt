In his Ghadir sermon, Imam Ali used allegorical references to biblical figures to assert his authority while maintaining social stability and teaching the rituals of Hajj. The Prophet's marriage to Fatima Zahra is highlighted as the sole union that definitively proves the legitimacy of both the husband and the children, serving as a primary practical proof of Ali's Imamate. The scholar Sayyid Murtadha cited Ali's exclusive brotherhood and status as the Prophet's son-in-law as key evidences, a view echoed by prominent Arab poets. Fatima Zahra awakened the community's conscience by questioning their neglect of the Ghadir pledge, arguing that this betrayal caused humanity to remain stuck in basic needs rather than achieving higher spiritual and social progress. Reconnecting with the legacy of Ghadir and honoring Ali's Imamate is presented as a necessary prerequisite for preparing society for the Mahdi's appearance. The discussion concludes with a prayer for unity among Shias, respect for the Ahl al-Bayt, and divine assistance in alleviating social hardships.
