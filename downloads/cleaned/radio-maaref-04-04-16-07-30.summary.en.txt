The program began by highlighting the deep bond between the Iranian people and the culture of the Wilayah, emphasizing the public's strong attachment to the Imam's representative.
The main discussion focused on the conditions for the acceptance of charity (Infaq) in Islam, stressing the necessity of faith, monotheism, and obedience to religious laws.
Citing Quranic verses, the instructor explained that the charity of disbelievers, sinners, and those who neglect prayer is not accepted by God, regardless of its apparent benevolence.
Prayer was identified as the central pillar of religion; its absence invalidates other religious acts, including charity, stripping them of their monotheistic value.
Factors such as showing off, ingratitude, causing harm, and oppression were listed as major obstacles that prevent charity from being accepted and may even lead to divine punishment.
Ultimately, it was concluded that valid charity must stem from genuine faith and prayer, free from any non-monotheistic motives, to be worthy of God's approval.
