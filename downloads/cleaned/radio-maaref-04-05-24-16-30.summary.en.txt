This radio program begins by discussing a narrative from Ibn Hisham's Sirah regarding the Prophet Muhammad's ethical conduct during the siege of Khaybar, where he returned entrusted sheep to their Jewish owners despite having the power to claim them as war spoils. The discussion then shifts to a listener's question about Sheikh Tusi and the establishment of the Najaf seminary. Sheikh Tusi is introduced as one of the greatest Shia scholars of the fifth century AH, known for significant works such as "Tibyan" and "Rijal." He initially studied under Sheikh Mufid in Baghdad and eventually assumed the role of the Shia Marja after the deaths of his teacher and successor. Following the fall of the Buyid dynasty and the rise of the Seljuks, religious tensions in Baghdad's Karkh district led to the burning of Sheikh Tusi's library and teaching platform. This event prompted his migration to Najaf in 448 AH, where his presence laid the foundation for the city's renowned seminary. The program concludes by encouraging Iranian pilgrims to visit the graves of Shia scholars in Iraq to deepen their understanding of this rich historical heritage.
