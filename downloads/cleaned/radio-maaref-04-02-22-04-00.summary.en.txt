The radio program began by outlining the thirteen obligatory rituals of Umrah al-Tamattu, from entering the state of Ihram to stoning the Jamarat.
It then addressed a fatwa by Ayatollah Khamenei regarding the purchase of dissolved gold in the virtual space, ruling that such transactions are invalid and the income haram if proper legal conditions are not met.
Other jurisprudential questions were answered, including the obligation of greeting in digital spaces and the conditions under which a driver's prayer is shortened due to frequent travel.
The program clarified that paying "Radd al-Mazalim" does not replace the obligation to repay debts to government agencies, and reciting the Karbala prayer in Qunut is not permissible.
The second segment, titled "Parsaiyan," focused on Islamic teachings regarding personal grooming and cleanliness in clothing.
Practical examples of Imam Khomeini and Ayatollah Beheshti's meticulous attention to their attire were shared to encourage listeners to maintain high standards of appearance.
Finally, the "Rawaq-e Ma'rifat" segment initiated a discussion on the theological foundations of classical and quantum physics.
This part examined principles such as realism and determinism in modern physics and their challenges when viewed through the lens of Islamic wisdom.
