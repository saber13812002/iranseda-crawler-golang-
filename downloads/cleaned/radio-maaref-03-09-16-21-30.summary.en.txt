In this radio program, the instructor of the Mahdism course critically examines the narrations regarding the "Yamani" and the alleged obligation to follow him. By applying the science of Rijal (narrator evaluation) and detailed logical analysis, the instructor argues that the narrations used to prove the Yamani's legitimacy contain significant chain-of-transmission defects and cannot be relied upon with certainty. Furthermore, the instructor demonstrates that the temporal and spatial indicators mentioned in the texts contradict current claims, particularly regarding the timeline between the emergence of Saffani and the Imam of the Age. It is emphasized that Shia jurists have historically derived the rulings of the occultation period from general jurisprudential principles rather than these specific narrations, and none of the authoritative fiqh books mandate obedience to the Yamani. The "Narration of the Will" is also analyzed, revealing that it does not define a role for the Imam's son during the occultation that would justify allegiance to a specific individual. Ultimately, the instructor stresses the necessity of a rigorous, scientific approach to Mahdist narrations to prevent the exploitation of academic blind spots for promoting false beliefs. The program concludes with a call for more specialized education in seminaries to ensure a deeper understanding of these complex theological issues.
