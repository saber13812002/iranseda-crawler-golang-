This radio program examined the relationship between women's and family jurisprudence and social sciences, highlighting their necessity for realizing Islamic civilization. Experts emphasized that jurists must utilize social science data to understand modern social complexities, but this engagement requires a critical awareness of the power dynamics and Western foundations underlying these disciplines to avoid blind imitation and secularization. It was stressed that understanding social and local transformations cannot rely solely on classical philosophical texts but demands a dynamic analysis of contemporary realities. Women's and family jurisprudence, as the fundamental cell of Islamic civilization, plays a guiding and system-building role, meaning that merely stating obligatory rulings is insufficient for resolving social issues. Consequently, jurists must move beyond the stage of discovering rulings to actively participate in designing social and legal systems. Ultimately, without uncovering a jurisprudential model for women and family and deeply understanding religious knowledge beyond mere fiqh, achieving jurisprudential civilization-building and addressing the challenges of the modern world remains impossible.
