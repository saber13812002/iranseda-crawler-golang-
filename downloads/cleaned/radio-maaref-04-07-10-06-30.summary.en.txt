The program begins with a jurisprudential analysis of the "Husn Qubh" theory, arguing that this rule is inherently rational rather than innate or natural.
It critiques the view of the late Hakim Kampani, who believed reason lacks the power to legislate and thus considered the rule non-rational.
Opponents argue that if reason does not legislate, the rule must be attributed to nature, but this contradicts the theological foundations of the Imami school.
The second part of the show opens with daily observances, including the birth of Imam Hassan (PBUH) and National Elites Day, accompanied by Quranic recitations.
The main discussion focuses on defining an elite as someone who has a tangible positive impact on science, technology, or culture, not just someone with a degree or position.
The central question posed to the audience is what support they would provide if they were an official and an elite person approached them with an innovative idea.
The historical context of the day is reviewed, referencing the election of Ayatollah Khamenei to the presidency and other significant historical events.
Finally, the program concludes by emphasizing the importance of identifying and supporting true elites for national progress and inviting listeners to answer the day's question.
