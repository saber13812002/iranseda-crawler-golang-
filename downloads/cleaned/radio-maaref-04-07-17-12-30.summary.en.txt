This program examines the evidence for the finality of Prophet Muhammad's prophethood, based on the teachings of the late Ayatollah Na'imabadi. The first argument is the comprehensiveness of Islam, which covers all political, legal, and ethical dimensions, thereby eliminating the need for a new religion. The second argument is the completeness of the Quran, which validates its divine origin through scientific and natural evidence alongside its legal rulings. The primary evidence, however, is the explicit Quranic verse in Surah Al-Ahzab that defines the Prophet as the "Seal of the Prophets" while rejecting the pre-Islamic custom of adoption. The term "Seal" here signifies the definitive end of the prophetic era, meaning the direct channel of heavenly news to humanity was closed with his arrival. Historically, no previous prophet claimed to be the last; instead, figures like Moses and Jesus foretold the coming of a final messenger. Consequently, any claim of prophethood after Muhammad, such as those by Ahmad Qadiani or Ali-Mohammad Shirazi, is considered a lie and a deception. The only legitimate successors to the Prophet are the twelve Imams, who are tasked with preserving and interpreting the faith.
