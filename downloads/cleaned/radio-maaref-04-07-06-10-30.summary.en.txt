In the radio program "Shifa," Dr. Hassan Akbari emphasized the moderate consumption of dates based on Islamic traditional medicine, noting that while dates are inherently warm in nature, their intake should be limited in non-native regions to prevent adverse effects.
He highlighted that fresh dates are a better option for individuals seeking to increase their body's moisture compared to dried dates.
In another segment, Hojjat al-Islam Rouhollah Rasoul, citing the Holy Quran, criticized pre-Islamic views on the gender of children, stressing that both sons and daughters are divine blessings and should not be negatively valued based on gender.
He condemned the behavior of some Arab tribes during the pre-Islamic era, who grieved upon hearing of a daughter's birth, and called for a divine and grateful perspective on offspring.
The program also marked the first anniversary of the martyrdom of Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, describing him as a symbol of resistance and a wise political leader whose strategies serve as a model for future generations.
The final part of the show focused on criticizing international structures like the United Nations, which, despite human rights claims, operate under the influence of major powers and fail to protect the rights of oppressed nations.
These criticisms emphasized that such organizations are often tools used to manipulate nations and must be faced with awareness and caution.
The program concluded by inviting listeners to share their feedback and expressing gratitude for their continued support.
