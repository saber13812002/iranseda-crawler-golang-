Iran's presence in Syria was fully legitimate, based on domestic and international laws as well as a formal request from the Syrian government.
The primary reason for this deployment was to counter the terrorist threat of ISIS, which had occupied large parts of Iraq and Syria, directly endangering Iran's borders and national security.
Iranian forces adopted an advisory role by training and organizing local youth and establishing a "Resistance NATO" to neutralize the threat and protect holy shrines.
The Supreme Leader emphasized that this was a defensive measure; without it, the war would have spilled over into Iranian territory, severely compromising national security.
The recent collapse of the Syrian regime reflects internal erosion and the betrayal of certain military commanders rather than a failure of the resistance strategy.
Despite these challenges, the experience of fighting ISIS remains a valuable asset for regional resistance forces and Iran.
It is hoped that despite political changes, the resistance movement in Syria will remain intact and continue to shape regional dynamics in its favor.
