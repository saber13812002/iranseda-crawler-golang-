In this program, Professor Sabei examines Imam Khomeini's critique of the theological views of Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari. The Imam refuted the principle of "divine habit" by asserting the existence of necessary causal relations in nature, comparing al-Ash'ari's denial of causality to David Hume's empiricist stance. By invoking the rule that "what is not necessary does not exist," the Imam rejected the separation of cause and effect and argued that absolute distinction between Creator and creation contradicts the emanation of the latter from the former. Regarding the vision of God, the Imam rejected al-Ash'ari's view of a sensory and material vision, arguing that the absolute essence of God is incomprehensible. He posited that what mystics perceive are the manifestations of divine names, not the divine essence itself. The Imam provided a mystical interpretation of Quranic verses such as "removing sandals" and "manifestation on the mountain," emphasizing the necessity of annihilation (fana) for spiritual perception. Finally, the Imam's critique of al-Ash'ari and Fakhr al-Razi was presented as an effort to undermine the Sharia and promote a corrupted form of monotheism.
