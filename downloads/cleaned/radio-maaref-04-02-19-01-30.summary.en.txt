In this program, Professor Mehdi Pishvay discusses the life and ethics of Imam Reza (PBUH) during his exile from Medina to Khorasan, relying on historical narratives. He references the book "Historical Geography of Imam Reza's Migration" to describe the journey and the widespread public reception of the Imam, while noting the strict security surveillance imposed by the Abbasid regime. Through his humble actions, such as eating with slaves and workers, the Imam demonstrated human equality and service to the people, effectively turning government spies into his loyal companions. He defined the criterion for human superiority as piety and righteous action, emphasizing good morals and the concealment of good deeds to prevent hypocrisy. The lecture also outlined five key indicators for true Shias: honesty in business, trustworthiness, avoiding excessive talk, refraining from disputes, and maintaining family ties. Furthermore, Imam Reza (PBUH) stressed the balance between religion and the world, arguing that a society neglecting economic and social planning under the guise of spirituality does not meet the Islamic standard.
