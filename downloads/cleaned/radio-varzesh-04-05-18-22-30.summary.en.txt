In this interview, Ali Reza Haghjati, a prominent and veteran Zourkhaneh master, emphasizes the importance of preserving authenticity in Zourkhaneh music and avoiding blind imitation. He argues that the new generation, despite having access to the digital space, must study reliable sources diligently to master the art of "Morshed" and become truly impactful. Haghjati explains that Zourkhaneh music has a distinct identity and should not borrow styles from other genres, as its audience seeks a specific cultural and historical experience. He also outlines the structure of the Zourkhaneh Sports Federation and the role of the Morshed Association in organizing, training, and judging masters. Despite financial challenges and economic constraints, he stresses the need for greater effort to improve training standards and preserve this cultural heritage. Haghjati shares his memories of listening to Master Moradi as a model for artistic and moral growth, highlighting the Zourkhaneh's role in ethical education and spiritual purification. Finally, he mentions that his sons and daughters continue to work in the field of Morshed and Zourkhaneh music, expressing his deep love for this art and the people of Yazd.
