The program begins by recounting memories of the Iran-Iraq war, highlighting how the resistance of fighters and the spiritual support of Imam Hussein played a decisive role despite the enemy's superior weaponry. The main discussion focuses on the universal nature of the message of Ashura and its capacity to be understood by all nations, including non-Muslims. Western historical and literary figures such as Edward Gibbon and René Joseph have described Imam Hussein as a symbol of absolute sacrifice and the future leader of humanity. These figures emphasize that Hussein's movement was a combination of pure reason and love, rather than an emotional or political decision. The core message is that one must stand firm on their ideals and avoid compromising with oppression, even at the cost of one's life. The mourning rituals of Ashura have preserved the identity of Shias over centuries and prevented their submission to oppressive governments. Finally, by referencing the martyrdom of students in missile attacks, it is emphasized that such sacrifices are rooted in the teachings of Ashura.
