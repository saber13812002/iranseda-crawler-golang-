In this radio program, the instructor examines the chain of narration for a report by Abdullah ibn Fadl Hashimi regarding the reasons for the Imam's occultation. Despite ambiguities in certain narrators, such as Ahmad ibn Abdullah Madaini, the report's validity is accepted due to its strong text and reliance on divine wisdom. The text emphasizes that the Imam's occultation is based on a wisdom that, like the occultations of predecessors, will become clear after his reappearance. To prove the feasibility of occultation, historical examples from prophets like Edris, Noah, and Abraham are cited from the book Kamil al-Din. The occultation of Prophet Edris is highlighted as a prominent case, characterized by the pressure of tyrants and the Shia's hope for relief until his eventual emergence. These analyses demonstrate that the occultation of the divine proof is a recurring phenomenon in history rooted in divine wisdom. The ultimate goal of this discussion is to clarify that the Imam's occultation, like previous ones, is founded on wisdom, and his awaiters must maintain patience and hope for relief.
