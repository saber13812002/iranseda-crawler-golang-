This text analyzes verse 120 of Surah Al-Baqarah and the story of Prophet Noah, highlighting two main points. First, the acquisition of divine knowledge and etiquette by prophets is gradual; thus, early actions that may seem inconsistent with ultimate perfection are due to their intermediate stage at that time. Second, there is a crucial distinction between "inquiry for knowledge" (istifham), which aligns with monotheistic etiquette, and "requesting or objecting," which is inappropriate after a divine promise is fulfilled. In the incident of Noah's son drowning, his question to God was solely to understand divine wisdom, not to request salvation or protest the outcome. By acknowledging God's promise as true and God as the Supreme Ruler, Noah demonstrated that he had not deviated from proper etiquette. God’s subsequent admonition to Noah was a warning against repeating such inquiries rooted in ignorance, rather than a reprimand for a sin. This interaction illustrates that prophets learn hidden knowledge gradually from God and align their conduct with their current spiritual stage. Ultimately, maintaining perfect etiquette in the face of divine decree, even in the most difficult circumstances, is a defining characteristic of the prophets.
