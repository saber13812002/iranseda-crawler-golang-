This radio program critically examines the Sunni argument for the validity of consensus (Ijma) based on the Quranic verse describing the Muslim community as a "middle nation."
Shia scholars argue that "middle" here does not imply simple justice, but rather signifies that the Prophet testifies to the community and the community testifies to the Prophet.
This testimony, which occurs on the Day of Judgment, requires infallibility and comprehensive knowledge of both external and internal truths, attributes possessed only by the fourteen Infallibles.
The primary critique is that even if the community were considered just or infallible in the afterlife, this does not validate their worldly consensus or grant them the authority to legislate new laws.
Even assuming infallibility, it only implies reporting existing realities, not the independent creation of new religious rulings or interpretations.
Notable commentators, including the Sunni scholar Alusi, conclude that the verse merely highlights the superiority of the Islamic community over others, without establishing the legal authority of consensus.
Ultimately, since core religious principles are already established by the Quran and authentic narrations, there is no necessity to rely on consensus to prove Sharia rulings.
