In the radio program "Zamzame Ahkam," Hojjat-ol-Islam Vahidpour emphasized the necessity of respecting others' rights during collective pilgrimages such as Arbaeen. Citing the jurisprudential rule of "Sabqah," he deemed cutting in line or encroaching on others' space impermissible, even within the Haram or during congregational prayers. Vahidpour advised pilgrims to sit quietly and maintain a spiritual connection with Imam Hussein (AS) through prayer rather than disturbing others to reach the shrine. In the Q&A section, Ayatollah Makarem Shirazi's fatwa declaring barley water absolutely haram and the obligation to gently forbid evil were discussed. Additionally, according to Ayatollah Sistani's ruling, the Qibla direction in unknown locations should be determined using signs like mosque mihrabs or believers' graves. While some weak narrations discourage visiting graves on Friday nights, it is generally considered permissible if there is no certainty regarding the prohibition. The program concluded with an announcement about a specialized session for Quran reciters and the playback of religious music and poetry.
