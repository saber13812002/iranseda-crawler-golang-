In this discussion, the expert emphasizes that the snapback mechanism in the JCPOA implies the revival of UN Security Council sanctions against Iran's nuclear and missile programs, rather than being a temporary threat. He explains that the current suspension by European powers is merely a tactic to buy time for diplomatic coordination, with no guarantee of permanent cancellation. The expert warns that Iran remains under the obligations of Chapter VII of the UN Charter and should not halt its military programs out of fear of the mechanism's activation. Economically, he argues that market fluctuations caused by these threats are largely psychological, and the government must insulate the domestic economy from external shocks by reducing dollar dependency and expanding bilateral trade. He also stresses the importance of strengthening national spirit and supporting domestic production to reduce vulnerability to external pressures. Finally, highlighting the resilience of the youth and the private sector, he calls for activating economic diplomacy and building trade networks with neighboring countries and BRICS nations.
