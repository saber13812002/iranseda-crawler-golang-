In this episode of the radio program "Ganj-e Sa'adat," Professor Mohammad Hadi Fallah explored the nature of anger and rage based on the narrations of Imam Ali and Imam Sadiq (PBUH). He described anger as an "illegitimate offspring" and the "key to all evil," emphasizing that it leads to destruction rather than blessing or virtue. From an ethical perspective, the professor noted that harboring anger toward someone with free will is considered a sign of lowliness, and associating with angry individuals is strictly prohibited. Using the metaphor of "fire in the hearts," he explained that the root of anger lies in the ego-driven soul (nafs), which can consume a person's entire being if left unchecked. He highlighted that the soul's two primary powers are anger and desire, and failing to monitor them can transform a person into a slave of rage or lust. The speaker illustrated the intensity of this internal fire by reciting poetry from a Mazandaran poet, urging listeners to seriously avoid this psychological and moral harm. Finally, the discussion expanded to the concept of "enjoining good and forbidding evil," stressing the importance of understanding the specific conditions and audiences involved in this religious duty.
