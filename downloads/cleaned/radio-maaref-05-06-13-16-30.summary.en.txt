In the radio program "Historical Trivia," Dr. Mohammad Reza Jafari examined the instructive fate of Ubaydullah ibn Har al-Ju'fi. This figure, who had an Uthmanite background and fought against Imam Ali in the battles of Siffin and Jamal, encountered Imam Hussein (PBUH) on his way to Kufa. Despite acknowledging the virtue of martyrdom in the Imam's company, Ubaydullah refused to join due to his fear of death, offering only his fast horse as a gift, which the Imam respectfully declined. After Karbala, he participated in Mukhtar's uprising and later joined Mus'ab ibn Zayyid, eventually drowning himself in the Euphrates in 68 AH. In the second part, the program addressed the case of Zuhayr ibn Qays al-Bajali, who, despite his Uthmanite origins, was martyred alongside Imam Hussein on the day of Ashura. When questioned by his enemies about his presence, Zuhayr affirmed that although his past was Uthmanite, he had chosen the path of guidance. These two examples highlight the contrast between the fear of death and the conscious choice of truth in early Islamic history.
