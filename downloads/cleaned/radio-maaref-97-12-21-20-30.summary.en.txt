The radio program "Shifa," featuring Dr. Pourhosseini, explored traditional Islamic medicine strategies for managing fear and heart weakness. The discussion emphasized that excessive medication can be harmful and that chronic fears should be addressed early in childhood. Honey was recommended as a Quranic remedy to warm the heart and reduce sleep terrors in children, provided it is natural and not adulterated. The fruit Amla was highlighted for its specific benefits in strengthening the heart and alleviating panic, with consumption in jam or milk-based forms suggested. Saffron was presented as a specialized heart tonic to boost animal spirit and courage, though overuse was cautioned against due to its hot and dry nature, which can cause bile-related imbalances. In response to a listener's query, the doctor advised against attempting to change one's innate temperament, stating that maintaining balance within one's natural constitution is the key to health. The program concluded by honoring the Martyrs' Foundation, emphasizing the spiritual value of martyrdom and the example of Imam Hussein (AS) in steadfastness and sacrifice.
