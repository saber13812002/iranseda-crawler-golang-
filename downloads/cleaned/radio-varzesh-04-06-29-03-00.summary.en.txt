This discussion highlights the historical significance of local dirt pitches in Tehran for discovering and nurturing football talent, emphasizing the pivotal role of figures like Hassan Habib in transforming these spaces into professional hubs. The guest stresses the shift of football from a local, emotional phenomenon to an economic business, noting the lack of truly private teams with genuine competitive motivation. He also points out the structural gap between youth academies and senior teams, criticizing the lack of cohesive management and communication between these two sectors. In his view, the primary reason for national teams' underperformance compared to successful youth teams is the weakness in grassroots education and the shortage of experienced, knowledgeable coaches. The guest emphasizes the necessity of utilizing veterans and experts to reform the educational system and create a coherent pipeline from the under-10 level to the national team. He believes that without fundamental reforms in coaching and club management, achieving Iran's rightful place in Asian football will remain difficult. Finally, respecting past experiences and leveraging expert knowledge are identified as the keys to rescuing football from its current state.
