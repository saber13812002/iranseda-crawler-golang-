This radio program examined the evolution of family law in Iran following the Islamic Revolution. Prior to the revolution, family law was primarily codified in the 1935 Civil Code based on Islamic jurisprudence, though the Pahlavi era saw a shift toward Western legal models. After the revolution, laws were revised to align with Sharia principles, leading to the amendment or repeal of provisions that conflicted with Islamic jurisprudence. However, new social challenges, such as inflation and changing family structures, necessitated a re-evaluation of certain mainstream jurisprudential views. Legislators utilized internal jurisprudential resources to address these issues while aiming to preserve family stability and ensure equity between spouses. For instance, the wife's inheritance share from her husband was adjusted to reflect contemporary economic and social realities. Additionally, practical solutions, such as granting guardianship to mothers after a father's death, were implemented without contradicting Islamic law. The discussion concluded that the Islamic Revolution has successfully balanced the protection of women's rights with the preservation of the family unit through a dynamic application of jurisprudence.
