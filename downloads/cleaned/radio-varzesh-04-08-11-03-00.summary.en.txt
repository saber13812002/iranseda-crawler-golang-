Dr. Zahra Nateghi outlines her pivotal role in establishing and developing Wushu in Iran starting from the 1990s. She highlights the crucial step of inviting the first Chinese coaches to enhance technical standards and conduct training camps nationwide, which led to the formation of the first national team in 2001. Following Wushu's separation from the sports associations to become an independent federation, Nateghi served as the vice president for the women's section. She also details her experience in launching new sports like women's rugby and cricket, where she personally learned the disciplines to overcome cultural and administrative barriers. Despite managerial conflicts that caused her temporary departure from the Wushu federation, her return after 15 years was warmly welcomed by the sports community. Nateghi emphasizes that women's sports contribute not only to athletic progress but also to raising a healthy generation and strengthening families. She concludes by expressing her desire to mentor future managers and share her 32 years of experience in serving women's sports.
