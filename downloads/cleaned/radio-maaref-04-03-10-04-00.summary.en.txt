In the Zammame Ahkam program, various religious questions were addressed with detailed rulings. Regarding the expiation for missed fasts, it was emphasized that 22.5 kilograms of food, such as wheat, rice, or bread, must be provided physically to the poor rather than just money. Concerning the punishment of a child, if serious physical or psychological harm is inflicted, paying blood money is required, and mere forgiveness is insufficient. Regarding the makeup of prayers and fasts, if the eldest child was a minor or insane at the time of the parents' death, the obligation to make up these acts falls on them once they reach maturity and sanity, not on other siblings. In terms of Khums, fabric purchased for making a mattress but left unused for a year becomes subject to Khums. Non-reciprocal livelihood assistance is not subject to Khums, whereas profits from selling goods are. In the Nahj al-Balagha section, the concept of "Haqq" (truth) was defined as stability, while "Batil" (falsehood) was described as instability. Imam Ali (AS) advised that one should rely on direct observation and investigation rather than mere hearsay to distinguish between truth and falsehood.
