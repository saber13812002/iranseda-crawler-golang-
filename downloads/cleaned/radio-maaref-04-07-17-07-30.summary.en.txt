The radio program begins by highlighting the discovery of the earliest known commercial complaint letter from the Assyrian period, illustrating the historical significance of correspondence in resolving disputes. It emphasizes the role of letters in shaping history and advocates for reviving the culture of letter-writing to maintain emotional bonds in the digital age. The main segment focuses on the ethical issue of verbal abuse and its remedies, drawing upon Quranic verses and the traditions of the Infallibles. The guest scholar identifies verbal abuse as a compounded injustice and recommends seeking forgiveness, avoiding repetition, and relying on the Ahl al-Bayt as primary solutions. A narrative involving the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) and a verbally abusive woman is shared to demonstrate the human capacity for spiritual healing and reform. The discussion concludes by stressing that applying religious teachings and seeking refuge in sacred traditions are essential for overcoming moral flaws and enhancing the community's mental well-being.
