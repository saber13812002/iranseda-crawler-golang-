The program critically examines the hadith regarding the "consensus of the Ummah on error," demonstrating that these narrations lack valid chains of transmission in both Sunni and Shia sources. In Shia literature, the report appears only in weak or mursal (unconnected) forms, while Sheikh Saduq's version relies on unknown narrators. Imam Ali's (AS) response in these texts challenges the minor premise of the argument—specifically the actualization of consensus—rather than the major premise, arguing that a gathering without the Prophet's true followers does not constitute a valid Ummah consensus. Analysts argue that refuting the minor premise is a stronger logical defense in a debate, a point also supported by Ayatollah Sobhani. A comparative analysis reveals that the core narration in Sahih Muslim and the Sunan books omits the consensus element, suggesting that the addition of "consensus" is a later, unreliable interpolation. Consequently, these narrations are deemed invalid both in terms of their chains of transmission and their logical implications. The broadcast concludes by introducing the subsequent segments of the radio program, including news and other religious shows.
