The program begins by announcing nationwide celebrations for the Prophet's birthday, emphasizing the theme of national unity and love for the Ahl al-Bayt.
The speaker then explores the secret of the Prophet's success, identifying it as his exemplary ethics and human conduct, as highlighted in Nahj al-Balagha.
Despite being sent to a society that was largely illiterate and plagued by conflict, the Prophet guided people to salvation through his personal example in worship, charity, and jihad.
A central point of the discussion is that the peak of the Prophet's ethics was found within his home, where he treated his family with kindness and avoided harsh criticism.
The speaker warns that a discrepancy between public politeness and private bad temper is a form of hypocrisy that can lead to psychological distress and the need for medication.
By citing the Prophet's practice of saying "Alhamdulillah" in all circumstances, the speaker encourages the audience to appreciate their blessings and maintain a positive perspective on life's challenges.
The Prophet is portrayed as a relatable, ordinary human being whose domestic life serves as a complete model for healthy family and social relationships.
