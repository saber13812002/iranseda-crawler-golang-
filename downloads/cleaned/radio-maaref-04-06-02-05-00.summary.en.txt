Imam Ali posits that even without the fear of hell or the hope of paradise, noble ethics remain essential for human dignity and success. These virtues are rooted in the inherent dignity of humanity, distinguishing humans from animals, and humans are naturally inclined toward goodness. However, this innate potential requires cultivation; just as rain is needed for the earth to flourish, humans need moral education to bring their potential to fruition. A crucial principle is that one must first cultivate oneself before attempting to educate others. While jurisprudence and law primarily focus on external actions and legal rulings, ethics delves deeper into the internal state of the individual. The Quran emphasizes that God judges not only actions but also hidden intentions, a dimension often absent in legal frameworks. Thus, ethics transcends legal boundaries by encompassing the purity of intent and internal spiritual health. This internal dimension is also recognized in psychology as vital for individual and social well-being.
