The program refutes the Shia claim that Ali (AS) was born in the Kaaba, arguing that the structure was a temple for idols.
The primary evidence is the absence of the three major idols, Lat, Uzza, and Manat, from the Kaaba, as they were located in Taif, Qudayd, and Wadi Nahla.
Another argument is that early Muslims prayed towards the Kaaba, which would be contradictory if it were considered a house of idols.
The second segment discusses the marriage of Imam Musa al-Kazim (AS) to Shahrbanu, labeling it a famous but historically flawed narrative.
The main criticism is that the Sasanian royal family had already fled Madain before the Muslim conquest, making their capture impossible.
The program then explains the fundamental differences between Akhbari and Usuli schools of thought in Shia jurisprudence.
Akhbaris, led by Muhammad Amin al-Isfahani, rejected the authority of the Quran's apparent meanings and the role of reason in legal deduction.
Finally, the efforts of Sheikh Murtada al-Ansari to codify Usul al-Fiqh and affirm the role of reason led to the decline of the Akhbari school.
