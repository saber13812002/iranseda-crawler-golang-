In this Radio Ma'aref program, the theological discussion focuses on the rule of "repelling probable harm" and whether reason mandates preventing harm to others. The speaker analyzes the views of the Mu'tazila and Imamiyya, concluding that most Mu'tazilite theologians limit the rule to repelling harm from oneself, considering harm to others merely recommended. Evidence such as the specific phrasing "repelling harm from oneself," arguments regarding enjoining good and forbidding evil, and explicit statements by scholars like Qadi Abd al-Jabbar and Mulla Hameed Khwazmi support this perspective. Even when harm to others is linked to self-harm, reason only mandates action due to the damage inflicted upon the individual. Sheikh Tusi also rejected the rational obligation of repelling harm to others in some works, attributing it instead to the principle of divine grace. Ultimately, the available evidence indicates a near-consensus among theologians that repelling pure harm to others is not rationally obligatory.
