The program begins by praising the extraordinary generosity and spirituality of Ayatollah Shafie, who served the needy by distributing gifts and establishing free bakeries and butcheries. The second section emphasizes the importance of maintaining decorum in mourning gatherings for the Sayyids and martyrs, condemning vulgar movements and the alteration of sacred names as unacceptable. Organizers bear the primary responsibility for upholding these religious boundaries to prevent violations of Islamic etiquette. In the legal rulings segment, the expiation for intentionally breaking a fast must be provided as food to sixty poor individuals, rather than just cash, unless it is guaranteed to be converted into food. A forgotten prostration after prayer must be compensated with specific recitations and two prostrations of forgetfulness. Travelers who intend to stay for ten days do not need to renew their intention until they leave the city, and doubt in the Tashahhud does not invalidate prayer if it is not obsessive. Finally, while a husband is legally permitted to reclaim his wife's dowry, it is ethically discouraged, and the sale of hair without extensions or harm is considered permissible.
