The text highlights the fundamental difference between imperialist armies and the revolutionary Islamic army, noting that while peacetime appearances can be deceptive, ideological and political quality determines success in wartime.
Drawing on the experience of the Iran-Iraq war, the author illustrates how the Ba'athist army collapsed once material support ceased, whereas Iranian forces, driven by faith and ideological training, resisted despite severe resource shortages.
Relying on Nahj al-Balagha and the letter to Malik Ashtar, the Islamic army is defined as "God's soldiers," whose purpose is to safeguard security, religious dignity, and serve the nation, rather than to oppress or seek war.
In this perspective, the army is a pillar of the social network, maintaining a reciprocal relationship with other societal institutions, and cannot be sustained without economic and social stability.
The key distinction of a believer's army is its resilience in the absence of adequate logistics, as its motivation stems from spiritual and ethical beliefs rather than mere material interests.
Discipline, order, and mutual respect between commanders and soldiers are core principles of the Islamic army that must prevail in both battlefields and barracks.
The designation of "Army Day" by Imam Khomeini symbolized the acceptance of the army's Islamic and popular identity and affirmed its role in preserving the revolution.
Ultimately, the army, relying on its Muslim and faithful base, successfully resisted internal and external plots aimed at creating disruption, thereby maintaining its integrity.
