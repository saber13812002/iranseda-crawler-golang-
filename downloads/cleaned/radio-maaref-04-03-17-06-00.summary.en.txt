The program begins by interpreting the verse on testimony in Surah Al-Baqarah, citing the occasion of its revelation regarding two Christians who allegedly concealed a Muslim's property. The commentator explains that witnessing a will, particularly when signs of death appear, is essential for preserving rights and preventing future disputes. A key point is the priority of two just Muslim witnesses, but in their absence, two just witnesses from the People of the Book are also accepted. This acceptance highlights Islam's universal nature and the shared importance of trust and justice across religions. If the heirs doubt the integrity of these non-Muslim witnesses, they must detain them after prayer and require them to swear an oath to God that they have not acted unjustly. This oath must include a commitment to not conceal testimony or sell truth for money, even if the deceased is a close relative. Finally, it is emphasized that while non-Muslim testimony is valid in financial matters, they cannot serve as legal guardians for a Muslim child.
