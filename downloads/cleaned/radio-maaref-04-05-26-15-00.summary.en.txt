This segment of the program examines the jurisprudential and legal framework for supporting the elderly, focusing on identifying the primary obligated parties.
The obligated parties are categorized into four main groups: children, spouses, citizens (believers), and the government, each with distinct responsibilities.
Regarding children, the rule of "the nearest then the nearest" applies, meaning grandchildren are only responsible if their parents are absent.
A significant point raised is the jurisprudential priority of a spouse's maintenance over that of parents, though the main challenge lies in defining the boundary between necessary and luxurious expenses for a spouse.
The Islamic government has broader duties toward the elderly in the modern era, yet weak law enforcement and limited financial resources in support institutions remain critical challenges.
From a jurisprudential perspective, children's obligations are not limited to financial needs; the spiritual and emotional dimensions of parents' lives are also addressed under the concept of "I'afaf."
Jurisprudential disagreements regarding the scope of these obligations highlight legal gaps in current laws, indicating a need to revise the support system.
Finally, the necessity of analyzing legal institutions such as maintenance and endowments to ensure the economic and psychological security of the elderly in old age was emphasized.
