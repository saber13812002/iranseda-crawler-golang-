Ayatollah Javadi Amoli, in his tafsir of Surah Al-Ma'idah, categorizes the concept of "questioning" in the Quran into four types: request, reproach, seeking clarification, and inquiry. Citing the stories of Noah and Moses, he emphasizes that questions mixed with objection or mockery are prohibited, even when asked by prophets. Conversely, inquisitive questioning for the sake of gaining knowledge is highly praised, provided the asker possesses a "wise heart" and a "questioning tongue." The verse "Do not ask about things" serves as a prohibition against inquiries whose answers might lead to humiliation, confusion, or the revelation of divine forgiveness. The Prophet is tasked with clarification, while believers are encouraged to ask constructive questions and engage in deep reflection. Finally, the scholar clarifies that the requests of Moses' people and the disciples for miracles were of the "request" type, distinct from the general prohibition in Surah Al-Ma'idah, which targets harmful inquisitive questions.
