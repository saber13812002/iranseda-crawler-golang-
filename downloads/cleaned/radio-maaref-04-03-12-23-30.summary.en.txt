In this radio program, a family expert emphasizes the importance of children's ability to defend their rights and their verbal power. He argues that the school environment alone is insufficient for social growth, and parents must provide complementary, managed settings outside of school. A key strategy is reducing a child's heavy dependence on parents by ensuring they sleep separately from a young age. Additionally, it is recommended to assign simple responsibilities, such as shopping, to children around the age of five to boost their independence and self-confidence. Leaving children with trusted relatives for overnight stays can also help decrease dependency and enhance social skills. Ultimately, children who are overly attached to their parents and lack playmates are more vulnerable in new environments and may struggle to stand up for themselves.
