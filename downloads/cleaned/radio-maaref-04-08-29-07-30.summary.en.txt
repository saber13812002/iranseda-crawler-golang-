In the first segment, listeners shared how engaging with their "inner child" helps maintain joy and intimacy within large, multi-generational families.
The host, citing Quranic verses and traditions, defined the inner child not merely as a psychological concept but as a divine truth representing the inherent gentleness of the human soul.
He identified three signs of a living inner child: delight in good deeds, a soft heart, and selfless service to others without seeking recognition.
In the second segment, the guest scholar analyzed "mockery" as a major verbal vice and sin based on Quranic and hadith sources.
The scholar emphasized that mocking sacred rituals, prayer, or prophets is essentially mocking oneself and the entire cosmic order, rooted in ignorance and lack of reason.
The Quran not only forbids mockery but also warns that sitting with or remaining silent in the face of those who mock sacred matters leads to sharing in their sin and being gathered with hypocrites and disbelievers.
The discussion concludes that preserving the kindness and simplicity of the inner child, while strictly avoiding mockery, is the key to remaining in divine mercy and achieving social peace.
