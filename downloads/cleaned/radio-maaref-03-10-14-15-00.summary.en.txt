The analyst argues that internal factors, particularly social crises, were more decisive than external forces in the collapse of Bashar al-Assad's regime. He emphasizes that the civil war and the government's failure to address social needs, especially the neglect of loyalist martyrs' families, created deep societal fractures. Ideological shifts following the Soviet Union's collapse and the 2014 constitutional amendments further activated these social fault lines. Concurrently, severe economic crises caused by war, sanctions, and the looting of oil resources by Turkey and the US drastically reduced Syria's GDP. Iran had previously warned about these social challenges through a letter from the Supreme Leader to General Soleimani, advocating for their resolution. However, ignoring these warnings and turning Syria into a battleground for regional powers led to the weakening of the country's military and economic foundations. Ultimately, the combination of social and economic crises paved the way for the central government's fall and the entry of external actors.
