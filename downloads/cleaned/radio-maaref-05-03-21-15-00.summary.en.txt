The text begins by critiquing the philosophical foundations of human rights in the West, arguing that these concepts have lost their divine and rational roots after secularization. The speaker emphasizes that in the Islamic world, human rights have a theocentric and ancient foundation that transcends the contractual and empiricist definitions of the West. He stresses the necessity of returning to Quranic verses and hadiths to gain a deeper understanding of justice and both material and spiritual human rights. Disagreements regarding the definition of human rights stem from fundamental differences in anthropology and epistemological sources among various schools of thought. The text also highlights the practical contradictions in the West's implementation of human rights and its instrumental use in foreign policy. Subsequently, a brief section provides simple tips for reducing water consumption in the kitchen. The text concludes with an introduction to radio programs and an invitation for audience participation.
