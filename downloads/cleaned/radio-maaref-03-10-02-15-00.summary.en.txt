This radio program examined the legal and jurisprudential frameworks for addressing crimes committed by artificial intelligence systems. From a legal perspective, punishments such as termination of existence (permanent shutdown), confinement (access restriction), free public service, and financial penalties were proposed based on general Islamic penal principles. Additionally, mitigation and deferred sentencing mechanisms were discussed as corrective tools for AI. From an Islamic jurisprudential standpoint, "Hudud" punishments, which are strictly reserved for adult humans, cannot be applied to AI. Conversely, "Ta'zir" punishments, aimed at rehabilitation and deterrence, could potentially be adapted to this new phenomenon through precise subject identification. The guests emphasized that the nature of AI must be correctly understood as either an independent entity or merely a tool before assigning liability. Finally, the necessity of drafting specific, independent laws for this emerging technology, rather than reducing it to simple instruments, was highlighted.
