The program emphasizes the Quranic and hadith-based importance of befriending prophets, the truthful, martyrs, and the righteous to be gathered with them in the afterlife.
Believers are urged to pursue piety and associate with the "Sadiqin," who represent the truth and the Ahl al-Bayt.
Imam Ali advises prioritizing intellect and honesty over superficial claims when choosing friends or spouses, while strictly avoiding those lacking both wisdom and nobility.
A believer is described as a "critic" who discerns truth from falsehood in speech, much like a coin assayer distinguishes genuine from counterfeit currency.
The second section examines the jurisprudential concept of "reported consensus" (Ijma Manqul) as a valid proof if single reports are accepted.
Sheikh Ansari clarifies that consensus cited in classical texts often signifies the existence of a specific proof or an usuli principle, such as "mughtazi wa mani'" (necessity and obstacle).
For instance, the rulings of Sayyid Murtada and Sheikh Mufid on purifying impurity with non-water substances were based on this principle rather than literal consensus.
Thus, the term "consensus" in fiqh literature sometimes refers to actual agreement and other times to reliance on a specific underlying rule or evidence.
