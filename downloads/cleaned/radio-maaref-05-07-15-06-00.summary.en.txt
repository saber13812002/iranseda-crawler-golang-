In this exegesis, the concept of "days" in the six-day creation verse does not refer to ordinary days or solar and lunar calendars, as the Earth's and Sun's movements that define these calendars did not yet exist during creation. God's actions are transcendent of time and space, occurring in an instantaneous, timeless moment, though they are described progressively for human comprehension. Therefore, the six days of creation symbolize historical epochs or stages of existence rather than 24-hour periods. Similarly, the Day of Judgment is a time when the current order of the universe is dismantled, rendering time and space inapplicable and unmeasurable by existing calendars. Narrations attributing creation to specific weekdays of the week are not scientifically or textually reliable and should not be accepted as absolute truth. Ultimately, the precise order of the universe reflects divine wisdom, arranged specifically for the emergence of humanity as God's vicegerent.
