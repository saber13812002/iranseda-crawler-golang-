This program analyzes the difference between formal Islam and true faith by interpreting Surah Ma'un.
According to the Quran, genuine faith must be accompanied by actions such as caring for orphans, feeding the hungry, and praying with full presence of mind.
Praying merely for show without social impact is considered a sign of practical disbelief and spiritual emptiness.
The concept of "Ma'un" in this surah refers to charity and social assistance, and withholding it indicates a lack of true piety.
While critics sometimes view Islam as merely a collection of sermons, the response is that Islam is a practical and governmental system.
In the Islamic system, ownership is a right but not unlimited, and the wealthy are obligated to use their wealth to meet societal needs.
Imam Sadiq (AS) labels substance abusers as foolish, emphasizing that public assets should not be placed in the hands of corrupt individuals.
The final conclusion is that true piety requires linking inner faith with social and economic responsibilities.
