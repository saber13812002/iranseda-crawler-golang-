Visiting Imam Reza (AS) must be accompanied by the two auxiliaries of knowledge and submission to be accepted by God, similar to the martyrdom at Badr. The primary criterion for the acceptance of this pilgrimage is a genuine behavioral transformation and the cessation of sins after returning from the shrine. Just as drinking water is only effective if it quenches thirst, a visit without a real change in character is futile. To achieve this goal, the authorities of the Qom Holy Shrine have organized coherent programs, including knowledge and educational circles. A key point in education is providing practical human role models, as people are more influenced by seeing real examples than by merely hearing advice. The story of an old man who became mute after selling his servant to a merchant's wife and was eventually guided to Khorasan through a dream illustrates this path. This narrative demonstrates that even in the most difficult circumstances, hope for divine guidance and following spiritual models can be a solution. Ultimately, the pilgrimage should serve as a turning point for the simultaneous elevation of both knowledge and action, allowing the visitor to return with a renewed identity.
