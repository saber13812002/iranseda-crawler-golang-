The radio program begins by emphasizing the necessity of piety and possessing the "habit of justice" to achieve spiritual authority over hearts.
Key components of this habit include observing religious duties, avoiding sins, and specifically refraining from backbiting and slander.
Citing religious narrations, backbiting is compared to eating dead flesh and is considered a grave sin.
The discussion then shifts to the jurisprudential issue of "afraz" (partition) in undivided (mushaa) waqf properties.
The instructor explains that while a superficial division among living co-owners is permissible, it does not constitute a permanent, legal separation.
To create a definitive and permanent division, the undivided property must be converted into separate, distinct shares.
Finally, the distinction is clarified between a temporary, apparent division valid only during the owners' lifetimes and a substantive division that results in exclusive ownership.
