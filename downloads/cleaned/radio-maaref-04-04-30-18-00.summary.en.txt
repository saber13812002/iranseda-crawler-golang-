In the radio program "Ma'rif," Hujjat al-Islam Fallah, relying on the Quran and narrations, identified stinginess as the root of many moral vices and theological challenges. He defined stinginess not merely as financial miserliness but also as neglecting religious obligations, withholding zakat and khums, and refusing to help others. According to narrations, a truly stingy person is one who is lazy in fulfilling divine duties yet spends lavishly on haram matters and extravagance. Imam Ali (AS) described abandoning obligatory acts as the ugliest form of stinginess, while Imam Sadiq (AS) cited earning haram wealth and spending it on non-righteous causes as key manifestations of this vice. In the second segment, Professor Bagherian analyzed Quranic verses, stating that extravagance causes the loss of God's love, whereas balanced charity is a sign of God's special servants. He emphasized that utilizing divine blessings must remain within the limits of moderation, as God does not love those who are extravagant. The overall conclusion of the discussion is the necessity of avoiding stinginess and extravagance, moving towards charity and piety to attain God's pleasure and spiritual peace.
