Major General Sadegh Shirzad, a 91-year-old sports veteran, emphasized the vital role of walking and mass sports in maintaining health at an advanced age. He shared his personal story of a severe leg fracture and his achievement of becoming the world's third-best walker in Canada, highlighting the recognition he received from Amir Majdara. Shirzad identified the lack of dedicated sports spaces in parks, which are often occupied by non-sporting activities, as a major obstacle to mass sports in Iran. He recalled the golden era of mass sports under Amir Majdara, who was instrumental in training instructors and promoting a culture of walking across the country. In track and field, Shirzad discussed his experiences as an international referee and national team coach, citing successes in Asian Games and collaborations with investors like Ali Moradian to build infrastructure. He argued that saving Iran's track and field requires stronger ties between sports and industry, along with better support for coaches and investors. Finally, Shirzad mentioned his son's resignation from the Housing Organization due to unprofessional interference, stressing the need for expertise in sports management.
