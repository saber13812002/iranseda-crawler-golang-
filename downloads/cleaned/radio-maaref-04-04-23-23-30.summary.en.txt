In this radio program, a child development expert emphasized the importance of attending to the pre-verbal needs of infants under one year old to prevent screaming from becoming a primary communication tool. The expert advised parents to anticipate their child's desires and respond proactively to avoid escalating frustration into habitual outbursts. Addressing a listener's concern about a two-year-old using inappropriate language, the "extinction" method, or deliberate ignoring, was recommended as the most effective strategy to reduce such behavior. The key insight is that strong emotional reactions or repeated scolding reinforce bad habits, whereas silence and non-attention lead to their eventual fading. The second part of the discussion addressed conflicts between a child and their grandmother, suggesting that the mother should maintain the primary disciplinary role to allow the grandmother to focus on emotional support. This clear division of roles helps prevent family tension and confusion for the child. The program concluded with religious prayers and condolences for the mourning period of the Ahl al-Bayt, reiterating the vital role of active parental involvement in child-rearing.
