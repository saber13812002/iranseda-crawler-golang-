This text combines two historical narratives: the martyrdom of Sayyid Mohammad Hadi Nasrallah during the Ansar al-Aqsa operation and the war memories of Hassan Nouri. Following Hadi's death, Israel used his body as a bargaining chip in prisoner exchanges, eventually returning it to Hezbollah in exchange for 40 Lebanese martyrs and six prisoners. Despite his profound grief, Sayyid Hassan Nasrallah delivered a speech on the anniversary of his son's martyrdom, consciously hiding his tears to avoid fueling Israeli psychological warfare. He also refuted Saudi rumors claiming Hadi died in a Beirut nightclub, defending the honor of the resistance. The second part details Hassan Nouri's captivity after the Fath al-Mubin operation, where he was taken by Ba'athist forces. He describes the harsh conditions in Iraq's intelligence prison, including starvation, lice, and humiliation, while highlighting the moral support provided by an Iranian translator named Karim Alwani who helped maintain his spirit.
