This segment of the discussion clarifies the fundamental distinction between "incidence" (hulul) and "emanative standing" (qiyam suduri) in sensory and imaginative perceptions.
The soul, acting as a creator, generates perceptual forms that stand to it through emanation rather than physically residing within it.
This differentiation resolves several objections based on the assumption that forms inhabit the soul, such as the claim that forms are substantial or that the soul becomes a material substrate.
If forms were to inhabit the soul, the soul would transform into the specific nature of the form, such as becoming a horse or heat, which contradicts the reality of perception.
By accepting emanative standing, the soul remains distinct, similar to the Necessary Being, creating forms without being qualified by their external attributes.
This argument demonstrates that essences are realized in the soul as effects dependent on their cause, without altering the soul's essence.
Ultimately, this analysis is accepted for sensory and imaginative perceptions, while the discussion regarding intellectual forms is deferred to a later stage.
