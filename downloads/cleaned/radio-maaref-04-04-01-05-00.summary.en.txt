This radio program explores the philosophical and ethical debate over whether silence or speech should be considered the fundamental principle of human conduct. Citing a narration from Imam Sadiq, the speaker argues that speech is the default state, as it is essential for education, enjoining good, and forbidding evil, rather than advocating for absolute silence. However, the speaker emphasizes that speech must be controlled and responsible, likening it to a car's brake system to prevent reckless talk, slander, and unfounded accusations. The discussion highlights the importance of maintaining proper hijab and modest dress for both men and women as a religious and social obligation, criticizing inappropriate public behavior. The speaker also addresses social sins such as backbiting and the need for precision and evidence in political discourse to avoid baseless analysis. The program concludes with prayers for the country's protection from internal and external enemies and a call for the integration of faith and politics in a Muslim's life. Finally, the show transitions to a preview of a jurisprudence lecture discussing the Quran's self-sufficiency and the concept of semantic distortion.
