This radio program emphasizes the emotional and spiritual significance of mourning Imam Hussein during the months of Muharram and Safar. The speaker argues that repentance in Islamic teachings transcends rational logic, being rooted instead in divine mercy. The story of the poet "Moghebel," who was cured of leprosy after repenting upon hearing mourning chants, serves as a symbol of how mourning awakens the soul. Mourning not only preserves the collective identity of Shias but also breaks hardened hearts, paving the way for a return to the path of truth and the acquisition of religious knowledge. Citing Rumi and Imam Khomeini, the speaker illustrates that breaking the heart is a prerequisite for receiving divine light. Finally, by reading a narrative from the book "Najwan Hassan" about caring for the wounded, the program highlights the deep connection between the suffering of Karbala and human empathy within the Shia community.
