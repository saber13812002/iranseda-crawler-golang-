In this program, Professor Pishbahi first correlated the Quranic trees of Shajarat al-Tayyibah and al-Khabithah with the Ahl al-Bayt and the Umayyad dynasty, respectively. He then noted that remnants of the Zilkhana dam still exist in the Caucasus region. The conquest of Iran by Muslims during the caliphate of Umar ibn al-Khattab took place over a decade through battles like Qadisiyyah and Nahavand, characterized as an Islamic rather than ethnic struggle. The segment also distinguished between Nasnas and Jinn, while discussing the natural death of Amr ibn al-As, Yazid's poisoning, and the alleged assassination of Aisha by Muawiyah. Ali (AS) was identified as the first male convert to Islam, and the root of sectarian divisions was traced back to the dispute over succession and the arbitration of Siffin. The Sahifa of Zahra (SA) was described as a collection of future events revealed to Ali (AS) through divine inspiration. Finally, the program criticized incorrect views on the expectation of the Mahdi, such as indifference to global affairs or encouraging sin to hasten his arrival.
