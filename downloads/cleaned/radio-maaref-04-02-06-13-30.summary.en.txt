In the radio program "Andisheha," Dr. Mohammad Jafari analyzed the characteristics of secular spirituality. He defined it as a non-religious, this-worldly concept that emerged in post-modernism due to a perceived spiritual void in the West. The primary distinction from religious spirituality lies in its lack of divine foundations and its reliance on personal feelings rather than reason and Sharia. Referring to the term SBNR (Spirituality But Not Religion), the speaker warned that many individuals are unknowingly caught in a syncretism between religious and secular spirituality. He identified the rejection of rationality and truth-seeking in favor of a pragmatic, emotion-driven approach as a major harm of secular spirituality. Furthermore, he interpreted the concept of "Merciful Islam" as an example of secular spirituality within the Islamic context, which isolates mercy from justice and religious laws. Finally, he emphasized that religious spirituality must be grounded in rational inquiry and innate nature to remain immune to the harms of individualistic and non-religious spiritualities.
