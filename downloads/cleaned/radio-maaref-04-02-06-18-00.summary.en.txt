In the radio program "Ganj-e Sa'adat," Professor Mohammad Hadi Falavi analyzed the concepts of "ghadab" and "ghizz," defining them as anger accompanied by harshness and a desire for revenge. He explained that humans are a combination of body and soul, and that innate faculties like desire and anger are essential and sacred for seeking benefits and avoiding harm. However, failing to manage these faculties can lead to deviation and sin, as illustrated by the story of Qabil and Habil. Falavi emphasized that the Quran calls for spiritual elevation by purifying the heart from anger, promising healing and higher moral ranks. In the second segment, a jurisprudential expert discussed the Islamic ruling on bank late-payment penalties and the associated issue of usury. He noted that while there are differing scholarly opinions, some authorities consider these penalties permissible under specific conditions based on the Supreme Leader's fatwa. The program also addressed listener questions regarding medical rulings and the process of following religious authorities.
