The text examines the role of reason in the Quran, distinguishing it from mere conjecture and blind adherence to tradition.
The Quran regards reason as a divine argument but warns that it is insufficient alone for understanding all truths without revelation.
A major cognitive error is substituting guesswork and assumptions for certainty in personal and social decision-making.
Blindly following the customs of ancestors without rational justification is considered a suspension of reason, which the Quran condemns.
On the Day of Judgment, individuals cannot justify their actions or words by blaming collective behavior or imitation of others.
The Quran emphasizes purifying the heart (fuad), which is an existential capacity beyond mere intellect and serves as the source of intuition.
Purifying the heart and intellect leads to a life free from fear and sorrow in this world and salvation in the hereafter.
Thus, Quranic civilization-building requires combining rationality, revelation, and internal purification to achieve certainty and piety.
