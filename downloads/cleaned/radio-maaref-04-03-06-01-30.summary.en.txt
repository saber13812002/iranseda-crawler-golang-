The text begins by criticizing autonomous driving, arguing that a car without human control is unlikely to survive high-speed hazards safely. It then draws a parallel, describing human reason as a lamp that, guided by divine revelation, illuminates the correct path in life. A central theme is the constant awareness of being in God's presence, as divine surveillance extends beyond visible actions to hidden intentions and inner betrayals. By managing desires and recognizing one's inherent poverty, a person who constantly sees themselves before God becomes a righteous individual. The latter part of the text references Imam Javad (AS) and the virtues of generosity, accompanied by prayers seeking divine mercy. It concludes with supplications for remembrance before the Ahl al-Bayt (AS), interspersed with vocal repetitions and music that mark the end of religious recitations.
