This program analyzes Imam Khomeini's leadership as a unique and charismatic force in Shia history, which led enemies to believe the Islamic Republic would collapse after his passing. Professor Rasakhi explains that the Imam implemented critical strategies in his final months, including ending the Iran-Iraq war, abolishing the deputy leadership position, and amending the constitution. These constitutional reforms aimed to structure the system and remove theological ambiguities regarding the leader, ensuring the state did not rely solely on individual charisma. The subsequent leader, Ayatollah Khamenei, is highlighted for his extensive executive, military, and parliamentary experience, distinguishing him as a continuationist leader. Unlike many political systems where the second leader criticizes the first, Iran's current leadership maintains the Imam's path by combining jurisprudential knowledge with crisis management experience. This structural and experience-based approach has ensured the system's resilience against internal and external challenges. The program concludes by emphasizing the current leader's sincere dedication to serving the nation and upholding Islamic values.
