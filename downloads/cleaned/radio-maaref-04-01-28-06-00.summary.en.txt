The principle of "avoiding probable harm" is a fundamental and self-evident rule in Islamic thought, rooted in the Quran and Hadith.
The Quran warns of eternal punishment for denying the truth, urging both believers and disbelievers to investigate the religion to avoid potential harm.
Narrations from Imam Sadiq and Imam Reza emphasize rational caution in addressing religious claims, using metaphors like choosing a safe path in a desert.
This argument parallels Pascal's Wager in Western philosophy, which posits that choosing faith is rational when comparing the outcomes of belief and disbelief.
Islamic theologians, particularly the Mu'tazila, used this principle to establish the "obligation of reflection" as the primary rational duty of humans.
According to this view, neglecting to think and investigate is irrational and forbidden due to the fear of potential hellfire.
Therefore, individuals are obligated to use reason and inquiry to discern the truth of religion, ensuring their safety from potential eternal punishment.
