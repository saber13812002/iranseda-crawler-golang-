In this radio program, jurists discussed the jurisprudential rulings regarding compensation for damages inflicted upon civilians during war. According to Islamic legal principles, the primary responsibility for compensating both physical and financial damages lies with the instigating entity, which is the invading enemy. If it is impossible to recover these costs from the enemy, the Islamic government is not strictly obligated to pay from the public treasury, but the Islamic ruler may choose to do so as a voluntary act based on public interest. Should compensation be successfully recovered from the enemy in the future, any amounts previously paid from the public treasury must be reclaimed from the affected individuals to prevent double payment. The discussion also covered international mechanisms, such as UN Security Council resolutions and peace treaties, for pursuing claims against the aggressor nation. Finally, it was emphasized that the Islamic ruler can manage this crisis by utilizing their authority to establish specific compensation funds or mechanisms.
