In this radio program, the expert argues that the economic and security repercussions of strikes on US bases in Arab countries served as sufficient deterrence, making a full-scale war with these nations unnecessary and counterproductive. He emphasizes that engaging in prolonged conflicts, similar to Russia's experience in Crimea, would create a broad Arab front against Iran and impose a heavy burden on the country. Addressing listener questions, the guest distinguishes between political criticism and legal crimes, explaining that prosecuting former officials for "omission of duty" or policy failures requires precise legal documentation rather than just public demands. He further stresses the importance of maintaining a firm, legally grounded stance regarding the assassination of the former leader and threats against the current one, asserting that pursuing these issues strengthens Iran's negotiating position internationally. The discussion concludes with an acknowledgment of public concerns over inflation, urging officials to work toward economic stability while thanking the audience for their participation.
