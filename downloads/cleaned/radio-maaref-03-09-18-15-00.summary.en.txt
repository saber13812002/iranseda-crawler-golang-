In this radio program, legal and jurisprudential experts examined the justifying factors for crimes in artificial intelligence systems. Dr. Estakhbatiyan, relying on jurisprudential concepts, identified duress and necessity as the primary factors that negate criminal liability, emphasizing that human dignity always takes precedence over the value of any AI system or property. Dr. Andelib distinguished between necessity (an internal factor) and duress (an external factor), using examples like an automated locomotive to illustrate the ethical dilemmas AI faces in emergency decision-making. The discussion also highlighted that strong, independent AI, unlike humans, operates only at the level of "information" and does not reach the level of "knowledge" or deep understanding. Consequently, the criminal liability of weak AI is transferred to its creator or user, whereas strong AI, if accepted as an independent entity, would possess rights and duties similar to humans. Therefore, if such a system commits a crime, it would be subject to punishment or legal excuses just like a human being.
