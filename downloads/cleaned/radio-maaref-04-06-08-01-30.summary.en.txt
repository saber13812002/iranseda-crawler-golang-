This program examines the ethical and managerial traits of Martyrs Rajai, Bahonar, and Raisi as models of accountability.
A key point is their custodial view of power; they considered responsibility not an opportunity for plunder, but a divine trust for pure service to the people.
Simple living and avoiding ceremonial luxuries were prominent traits, demonstrating that true leaders must empathize with the underprivileged so that poverty is not offensive.
Special attention to the deprived and provincial trips to solve people's problems were viewed not as populism, but as a prophetic tradition and religious necessity.
Their history of jihad and enduring severe torture during the Pahlavi regime demonstrated their iron will and religious insight.
Martyr Rajai maintained his spiritual state through prayer and reliance on God, even at the peak of physical suffering.
Finally, the program introduced the book "Ocean of the East" and its narrative about a character named Aman searching for the water of life, touching on esoteric and religious themes.
