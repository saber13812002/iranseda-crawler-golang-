The radio program "Tales of Freedom" featured Mr. Nouri, a former prisoner of war, who recounted his experiences of captivity and resistance during the Iran-Iraq War. He described the harsh conditions of air raids and his transfer to Iraqi camps, including the "Between the Bars" camp, which was notorious for holding young prisoners. The captives were forced to learn Iraqi-style military drills to be paraded in front of Saddam Hussein's portrait for propaganda purposes. Mr. Nouri detailed the food strikes and the fierce resistance of the young prisoners, highlighting the story of Amir Shahpasandi, a 16-year-old who refused to betray his revolutionary ideals despite being tortured with a hot iron. The presence of religious figures in the camps, such as Hajj Abut Al-Abi, played a crucial role in maintaining the prisoners' morale and faith. The most bitter memory for him was the news of Imam Khomeini's martyrdom, which coincided with the announcement of his successor's selection. The return of the freed prisoners to Iran was seen as a symbol of the spirit's victory over the body and the steadfastness of faith against oppression. The program concluded with a book recommendation about martyr Majid Seifi and prayers for the Arbaeen pilgrimage.
