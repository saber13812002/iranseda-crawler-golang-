The text begins with a Quranic interpretation emphasizing that the houses of oppressors will be destroyed, asserting that injustice, whether political or personal, carries immediate and severe worldly consequences.
The author illustrates this by recounting the story of Ayatollah Borujerdi, who publicly acknowledged and compensated for his mistake in dismissing a scholar, demonstrating that even religious leaders must answer for their errors.
The narrative then shifts to the book "Neshan-e Hassan," depicting the turmoil in Medina following Muawiyah's death and Walid bin Uqba's announcement of Yazid's succession at the Prophet's Mosque.
Qasim ibn Hassan, son of Imam Hassan, witnesses the public outrage and, upon learning of Imam Hussein's summons to Dar al-Imara, feels a sense of pride and readiness to defend justice.
The presence of Qasim and his brothers alongside Imam Hussein and Abbas highlights the deep connection of the Ahl al-Bayt's descendants to the ideals of Karbala and their preparedness to stand against Umayyad oppression.
This narrative, blending historical sources with literary imagination, portrays the tense atmosphere in Medina before Karbala, stressing the importance of divine zeal and avoiding harm to the helpless.
Finally, the text focuses on the martyrdom of Imam Abbas and its profound impact on Imam Sajjad, underscoring his dignity and role in preserving the honor of the Ahl al-Bayt during history's darkest moments.
