In the Radio Ma'aref program, Professor Mohammadi addressed a question regarding the number and geography of prophets, citing Sheikh Saduq's narration of 24,000 prophets while noting that only 37 are named in Islamic texts. He rejected the idea that prophethood was limited to the Middle East, pointing to prophets in Africa and East Asia, and attributed the primary focus to the region where the first human civilizations emerged. The professor emphasized that the Quran's purpose in recounting prophetic stories is moral education and providing practical examples for the audience, rather than serving as a historical record. In the second segment, the response to a question about respecting a husband who neglects his wife's rights clarified that while respect is permissible and virtuous, it does not justify ignoring the wife's rights or excusing the husband's misconduct. The speaker stressed that one party's mistake does not grant the other permission to err, and both must adhere to their religious obligations. Finally, the "Culture Street" segment concluded by discussing the pivotal role of youth in society, highlighting the importance of education and self-monitoring for personal and societal progress.
