The radio program "Tales of Freedom" began with the memoirs of war veterans and prisoners of war from the Iran-Iraq conflict. Ali Qazvini recounted the failed attempt to defend Qasr-e Shirin and the subsequent capture of a group of fighters by the enemy on the roads. Ali Alidoost described his experience of captivity in Baghdad, including physical and psychological torture in Iraqi intelligence and his transfer to the Mosul prison via a freight train in the severe winter cold. He detailed the harsh prison conditions and even the torture he endured for reciting the Quran and prayers. Another segment highlighted the touching story of Martyr Habibollah Latifi, who rescued a kid goat from the Halabja chemical attack, forming a deep bond between the animal and his son, Sajjad. The program concluded with a jurisprudential discussion on the necessity of establishing free debate platforms to address new economic and governmental challenges. The broadcast aimed to honor the memory of martyrs and prisoners while conveying lessons of freedom and faith to the audience.
