This segment of the "Mabna" program examines the sixth perspective on the jurisprudential rule of "repelling probable harm," which serves as a middle ground between requiring certainty and accepting any degree of probability. According to this view, supported by scholars such as Qazwini, Sadr, and Imam Khomeini, reason mandates repelling harm even when the probability is less than "ghalaba" (more than fifty percent). The primary criterion here is "aqlai" probability, defined as a level of likelihood that generates fear and attracts rational attention, rather than a fixed mathematical threshold. The speaker then outlines their preferred view through three claims: first, that reason applies to probabilities below fifty percent; second, that extremely weak and insignificant probabilities are excluded; and third, that the severity of the harm influences the required level of probability. The main rational argument for this stance is the verdict of conscience and the praise or blame of reasonable people, who commend caution against potential risks and criticize negligence. Furthermore, accepting this rule is essential for proving the "obligation to investigate religion," as it provides the basis for compelling non-believers to examine religious claims. Finally, while briefly touching upon side discussions regarding free will and innate nature, the speaker emphasizes the independent role of reason in identifying probable harms.
