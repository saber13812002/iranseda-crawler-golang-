The radio program "Tales of Freedom" begins by recounting the memoirs of Brigadier General Mohammad Hossein Ansari, who was captured by the Ba'ath regime in 1979. He describes the secret detention of himself and 56 fellow soldiers in dark pits for a decade, highlighting their total deprivation of light and fresh air during the initial years. The prisoners overcame severe biological challenges through ingenuity, such as creating batteries from pomegranate peels to listen to news and using coded wall-knocking to communicate with adjacent cells. Ansari also details the remarkable self-sacrifice of his comrades amidst brutal torture and the spiritual conversion of some Iraqi officers who became allies to the captives. After 1,019 months of captivity, the group was released as the final batch of prisoners at the Khesrowshahr border and returned to Iran. The second segment of the program shifts to a psychological discussion on the negative impacts of social comparison, vanity, and lack of modesty on mental peace. The host emphasizes that grooming should remain simple and free from self-promotion to prevent anxiety and stress. The broadcast concludes by advocating for maintaining modesty and inner tranquility as essential strategies for reducing both individual and social tensions.
