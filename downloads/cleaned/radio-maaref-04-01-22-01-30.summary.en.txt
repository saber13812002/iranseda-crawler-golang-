The program begins with a philosophical discussion arguing that true humanity is impossible without faith in God and adherence to divine teachings, as selfless service is only sustainable with a religious foundation.
The primary indicator of humanity is "putting yourself in another's place," which means respecting others' rights and avoiding actions that we ourselves would find objectionable.
The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) emphasized that a true human being does not wait for a request for help but proactively approaches others to resolve their problems before they even ask.
It is also noted that criticizing others for faults we ourselves possess is a sign of moral double standards and a lack of genuine humanity.
The second part of the program addresses historical questions regarding why most prophets were sent to the West Asian region.
The explanation is that major prophets appeared in the centers of great civilizations to facilitate the spread of the divine message, although the Quran states that messengers were sent to all nations.
Archaeological evidence and the remnants of monotheistic customs in Eastern and European cultures suggest that the teachings of prophets influenced these regions, even if the specific names were forgotten over time.
Finally, it is recommended that religious researchers use linguistic and archaeological tools to conduct more detailed global studies on these historical and theological topics.
