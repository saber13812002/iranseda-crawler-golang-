In this radio program, the structure of theological rules regarding divine unity and justice is explained, distinguishing them from rules governing human actions. The speaker emphasizes that while rules like 'Lutf' and 'Aslakh' impose obligations on God, the rules of 'repelling probable harm' and 'obligation of gratitude' apply to human behavior. The rule of repelling probable harm is presented as a rational and innate principle accepted even by the Ash'ari school, despite disagreements regarding its specific evidentiary basis. This principle has extensive applications in daily life and is crucial for proving fundamental religious principles, such as the obligation to seek knowledge of God and the necessity of appointing an Imam. In Usul al-Fiqh, this rule is utilized to derive legal rulings and address issues like the prohibition of acting on doubt and the validity of single reports. The discussion also covers theoretical debates concerning the definition of probability, the scope of harm (worldly or otherworldly), and whether the rule applies to actions or omissions. Ultimately, the rule is highlighted as a key tool for establishing rational and religious obligations for humans.
