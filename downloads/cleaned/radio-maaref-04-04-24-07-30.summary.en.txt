In this radio program, following listener messages expressing love for Iran and devotion to the guardianship of the jurist, Hujjat al-Islam Falah Bastan shifts the discussion from the virtue of charity to the vice of stinginess. He defines stinginess as a disease rooted in the ego-driven soul and an excessive, non-theistic attachment to the material world, which stands in direct opposition to the teachings of the Ahl al-Bayt. The speaker emphasizes that the Quran frequently mentions stinginess, linking it to disbelief, polytheism, and hypocrisy. Citing verses from the Surahs of Tawbah, Muhammad, and Al-Imran, he explains that the stingy neglect the divine command to give, thereby diminishing their faith and piety. A crucial point is that stinginess brings no benefit to the individual; instead, it manifests on the Day of Judgment as a fiery yoke with a clear identity around the owner's neck. Ultimately, God is fully aware of all actions and the false assumptions of the stingy, as true ownership of all things belongs to Him.
