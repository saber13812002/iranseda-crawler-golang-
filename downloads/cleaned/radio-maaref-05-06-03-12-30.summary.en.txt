The radio program "Tales of Freedom" began with religious recitations before transitioning to the personal accounts of former prisoners of war. A veteran described the harsh conditions of captivity, including enforced silence, severe beatings, and a critical lack of basic necessities. He highlighted the complex behavior of a Shia Iraqi soldier who, despite participating in the abuse, cried out of remorse and secretly bought supplies for the prisoners from his own salary. The narrative also covered the forced shaving with dull blades and the ingenuity used to distribute limited food supplies with handmade tools. The moment of release was recounted, detailing the skepticism surrounding the negotiations and the emotional encounter with Iranian guards at the border. The speaker expressed the collective grief of the group when they finally saw the shrine of Imam Hussein after three years of captivity. The program concluded with a book recommendation and public service announcements regarding the efficient use of water and electricity.
