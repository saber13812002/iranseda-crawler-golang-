In this radio program, Professor Mohammad Sharoudi addressed various theological and jurisprudential questions from listeners. Regarding the perceived superiority of Sunni Muslims, he argued that majority opinion or the existence of prominent scholars like Imam Shafi'i does not prove absolute truth, as Shia Muslims also rely on their own authentic sources. On the topic of Khums, he clarified that this obligation is established in the Quran and Sunnah and is not limited to war spoils but encompasses various forms of income. He also explained that prophets and Imams do not possess independent knowledge of the unseen; rather, such knowledge is granted by God's permission. In response to a question about illness, he noted that God's will is consistent with natural laws and material causes, so humans should seek medical treatment and prayer. The final segment focused on hijab as a religious and legal necessity, emphasizing that its absence is not merely an ethical issue but has political and security implications. Professor Sharoudi stressed the duty of "enjoining good and forbidding evil" for both the public and officials, urging for cultural awareness and social support to uphold hijab.
