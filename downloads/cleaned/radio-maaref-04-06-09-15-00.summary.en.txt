This radio program distinguished between "criminal policy" as a comprehensive set of preventive strategies and "penal policy" as the final, punishment-oriented stage of crime control.
It was emphasized that elder abuse is not always a criminal offense, with many aspects falling under social and religious norms rather than strict legal definitions.
Dr. Mohammadi highlighted Quranic and hadith teachings that elevate the status of the elderly through respect and reverence, serving as a root-level prevention mechanism.
Dr. Hedayatnia stressed the need to shift focus from purely penal approaches to social, urban, and health-related preventive measures.
He cited practical challenges such as unsafe streets, inadequate home infrastructure, and difficulties in accessing healthcare as examples of systemic abuse.
The discussion noted that Iran lacks a specific, comprehensive law for elder protection, unlike many other countries, forcing reliance on general criminal codes.
Finally, it was suggested that governance and social policies must be tailored to treat the elderly as a vulnerable group requiring special support and accommodation.
