In this radio program, Dr. Mohammadi and Dr. Peyvandi examined the principles governing Islamic criminal policy. A key principle discussed is the presumption of innocence, which dictates that an individual is not criminally liable unless their guilt is definitively proven. This principle applies both during the legislative process and when judges determine criminal liability in court. The role of belief in the afterlife and resurrection in preventing crimes against physical integrity was also highlighted. Belief in eternal punishments, such as eternal damnation for premeditated murder, serves as a strong deterrent against committing such acts. Even if a worldly punishment like retribution is carried out, the afterlife penalty remains unless the individual repents. These discussions illustrate that the Islamic penal system combines both worldly and otherworldly deterrence mechanisms.
