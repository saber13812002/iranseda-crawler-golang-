In this radio program, Professor Jafari analyzes the event of Banu Qurayza in the fifth year of the Hijra, where the tribe breached their non-aggression pact by attacking Muslims from behind. Following the defeat of the Confederates, the Prophet (PBUH) besieged the Banu Qurayza fortress and, based on the Torah and divine command, ordered the execution of their combatant men and the captivity of women and children. This ruling, confirmed by the Quran in Surah Al-Ahzab, was carried out due to the severity of their betrayal compared to previous incidents, with estimates of the executed ranging from 40 to 200 individuals. In the second part, the concept of the Mu'tazila is explained, a sect that emerged in the second century AH based on Wasil ibn Ata's theory of being "in a state between faith and disbelief." The Mu'tazila had both theological roots and political origins, stemming from their withdrawal from internal Muslim conflicts during the era of Imam Ali (AS). Known for their rationalist approach to religious issues, their legacy continues in modern rationalist movements within the Islamic world.
