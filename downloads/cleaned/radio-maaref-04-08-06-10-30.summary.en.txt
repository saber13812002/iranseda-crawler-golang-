In the radio program "Shifa," based on Islamic traditional medicine, the importance of moderate date consumption in non-native regions was emphasized, correcting the misconception that all dates have a cold temperament.
It was advised that in areas where dates are not a local product, daily intake should be limited to three to seven dates to prevent potential health side effects.
In the second segment, Hujjat al-Islam Rasoul, citing the Quran and Nahj al-Balagha, identified keeping promises as the fundamental pillar for societal prosperity and reducing legal disputes.
He warned that broken commitments by officials and merchants are the root cause of many social dissatisfactions, using a real estate fraud case as a practical example.
Finally, a speech was broadcast stressing the necessity of unity between the clergy and universities to counter external powers' plots aimed at creating societal division.
The program aimed to promote a religious lifestyle and social awareness, urging listeners to adhere to their commitments and remain vigilant against divisive tactics.
