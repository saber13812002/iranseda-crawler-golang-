In this program, Ayatollah Javadi Amoli interprets the final verses of Surah Al-Ma'idah to address the issue of the Jurist's Guardianship (Velayat-e Faqih). He argues that freedom in Islam is compatible with divine law and that the Jurist's authority is fundamentally different from guardianship over minors, as the jurist represents the people in implementing religious rulings. The acceptance of the people is necessary for the practical execution of this authority, but it does not create the authority itself, which is derived from the religion. He emphasizes that a stable government requires public awareness and participation; without popular acceptance, the implementation of the Jurist's role is halted. The discussion then shifts to the institution of slavery, asserting that Islam replaced the pre-Islamic system of dominance with the principle of liberation through spiritual purity. Islam did not accept slavery as a permanent institution but gradually abolished it by establishing mutual rights between master and slave and providing extensive mechanisms for emancipation. The ultimate goal of the religion is to transform society from a power-based system to a value-based and liberating one.
