The radio program begins by discussing the experience of holding home Quran sessions in Qazvin, where modern technologies, animations, and interactive games are used to teach children.
A key point emphasized is that memorization and understanding of the Quran should progress together, as engaging audio-visual methods significantly boost children's motivation to learn.
The second part of the program features Hujjat al-Islam Falah, who examines the concept of cursing and foul language from the perspective of the Quran and Hadith.
Citing verse 108 of Surah Al-An'am, the scholar argues that cursing is not only forbidden but is also rooted in polytheism and ignorance, even when directed at the false idols of polytheists.
He cites narrations from the Prophet (PBUH) and Imam Sadiq (AS) to explain that cursing expels a believer from Islam and provokes reciprocal insults from the other party.
Finally, it is recommended that Muslims avoid all forms of cursing, including speaking ill of animals, to prevent falling into the trap of polytheism and ignorance.
By maintaining good ethics and refraining from foul language, the community can be protected from the harmful effects of verbal abuse.
