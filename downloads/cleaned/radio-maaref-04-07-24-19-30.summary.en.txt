In this radio program, Professor Mohammad Hadi Fallah discusses the vices of the tongue, specifically focusing on the concept of "verbal wounds" or harsh speech. He explains that hurting a believer is not merely a social offense but a declaration of war against God, as believers are manifestations of divine majesty and power. According to narrations, unlike physical wounds that heal, verbal wounds are permanent and cause deep, lasting pain that never fully recovers. Imam Baqir (AS) warned that anyone who inflicts such wounds on a believer deserves the worst death and is deprived of all good and salvation. The discussion also highlights that non-verbal cues, such as body language and contemptuous looks, can be equally damaging and constitute a form of verbal assault. Furthermore, the host emphasizes that jokes which break someone's heart or damage their reputation are not permissible and are considered a form of blame. Finally, the professor stresses that the sanctity of religious knowledge is preserved only through action, noting that scholars who do not practice what they preach are themselves subject to criticism by the very knowledge they possess.
