In this program, Hojjat al-Islam Homayoun explains the distinction between "good manners" and "noble virtues" based on Quranic verses and narrations. He defines noble virtues as a higher spiritual rank rooted in inner dignity and overcoming base desires, noting that the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) was sent to perfect these traits. The Quran strongly recommends forgiveness and responding to evil with kindness, which can transform enemies into friends and prevent societal conflicts. In the second segment, Ayatollah Mazarani discusses the legal validity of "famous opinions" (shohrat-e fatvayi) in his Usul al-Fiqh class. He argues that, similar to established consensus, famous opinions are valid because they imply the existence of hidden evidence not accessible to us. He further clarifies that the classical scholars' concept of consensus does not require unanimous agreement from all scholars, but rather relies on the views of prominent jurists and the absence of a clear, opposing consensus.
