In this interview, Alireza Hajjati, a veteran master of the Zourkhaneh and a member of the Iranian Masters Association, emphasizes the necessity for masters to have a deep command of literature, poetic meter, and the authentic rhythms of Zourkhaneh music. He criticizes the blind imitation of classical or mourning styles, arguing that Zourkhaneh music must maintain its distinct identity to align with the sport's cultural and historical background. Hajjati also discusses the organizational structure of the Masters Association and the need for systematic training to address financial constraints in educating the new generation. He recalls that in the past, reaching the status of "Sardam" required years of rigorous practice and formal permission from a master, rather than merely possessing a suitable voice. Finally, highlighting the ethical and spiritual dimensions of the Zourkhaneh, he recited a poem by the late Master Moradi and mentioned that his sons are continuing this tradition.
