In this radio program, legal and jurisprudential experts examined the Islamic and legal frameworks governing large-scale domestic and international investment contracts.
It was emphasized that Islamic jurisprudence effectively addresses the challenges of complex hybrid contracts that combine elements of lease, sale, and agency.
Standard international contracts, such as EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction), are widely used in major industrial projects to minimize legal risks and streamline processes.
Under the EPC model, the contractor assumes full responsibility for the project from design to final delivery, while the client is primarily responsible for financing.
To finance these large-scale projects, utilizing capital market instruments like participation and lease certificates was identified as an effective method for mobilizing public investment.
Experts warned that when signing contracts with foreign entities, it is crucial to ensure technology transfer to domestic personnel to prevent long-term dependency.
Strict adherence to legal and jurisprudential clauses in these agreements is essential to safeguard national interests and prevent potential exploitation.
Ultimately, honoring covenants in international economic relations is not only a religious obligation but a necessity for building trust and ensuring sustainable development.
