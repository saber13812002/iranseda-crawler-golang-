The discussion emphasizes the necessity of transitioning from a purely specialized approach to jurisprudence toward a macro-oriented, civilization-building perspective that integrates social sciences and broader Islamic teachings. The central theme is the role of jurisprudence in fulfilling the responsibilities assigned by the Supreme Leader, particularly regarding the family system, as outlined in his recent messages. A key question arises as to whether current conventional jurisprudence can bear this heavy responsibility or if new capacities within the discipline need to be developed. A primary indicator of the desired jurisprudence is its coherent and deep connection with Islamic ethics and teachings, allowing it to transcend mere legal codification and aim for civilizational goals. It is argued that jurists should not rely solely on jurisprudential narrations but must evaluate them against the spirit of the Quran and broader religious knowledge. This approach, supported by senior clerics, requires that the process of legal deduction considers the macro-directions and objectives of the Islamic system. Ultimately, the conclusion is that civilization-building jurisprudence must possess a comprehensive understanding of religious teachings and social needs to effectively shape social and family systems.
