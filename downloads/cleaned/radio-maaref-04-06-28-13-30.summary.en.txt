The text examines the historical role of Arabic and Persian as the primary carriers of Islamic civilization, arguing that European colonizers severed the link between Muslims and their religious heritage by imposing their own languages and banning local ones. This process aimed to impose Western culture and weaken Islamic identity by mandating the teaching of colonial languages in schools and media. The speaker emphasizes that language is not merely a communication tool but a gateway to culture, values, and worldview, and that its forced erasure destroys cultural roots. The slogan "Neither East nor West" is interpreted as a strategy for Muslims to maintain cultural and religious independence against foreign domination. The text also highlights the need for a smart language policy where foreign languages are taught voluntarily and aligned with national and Islamic interests, rather than through coercion. Finally, a brief discussion on Imam Hassan's peace treaty and Imam Hussein's defensive role in it is presented to demonstrate that the treaty was a political and religious necessity where martyrdom was not a viable option under those specific conditions.
