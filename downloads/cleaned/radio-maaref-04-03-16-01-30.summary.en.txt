The text begins by criticizing blind adherence to unhealthy traditions and emphasizes the Quranic necessity of rationality and individual responsibility.
It then explains the concept of the heart as an existential capacity and moral reference for distinguishing truth and escaping fear and sorrow.
The second part transitions to a dramatic retelling of the novel "Jame'e Dast-o-Pas" set during the 1963 uprising.
Salar, the protagonist, participates in armed clashes in Pishva and Varamin to earn money and return to his family life.
After being wounded, he hides his son Sohrab in a car trunk with the help of Zebih and the driver to save him from security forces.
When Husheng and Habib arrive, Salar manages to prevent his son's arrest through deception and concealment.
Meanwhile, Azar and Sorna wait anxiously at home for Salar's return, highlighting family concerns amidst political tensions.
The narrative illustrates how social crises force families into difficult choices and dangerous deceptions.
