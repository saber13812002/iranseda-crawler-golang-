The text combines two distinct topics, beginning with a theological discussion on detaching from worldly desires in favor of the afterlife.
It cites the Quran and Nahj al-Balagha to emphasize that the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) considered the world's value to be as insignificant as a mosquito's wing.
Worldly possessions are described merely as tests, where the key distinction lies in one's motivation for actions like marriage.
The second part shifts to Islamic jurisprudence regarding the Hajj ritual known as the three-day stay in Mina.
Generally, the five rites of Mecca must be postponed until after leaving Mina, rather than being performed before Arafat.
Exceptions are made for women facing menstruation or postpartum bleeding, as well as the elderly or sick who cannot travel.
Individuals who cannot return to Mecca by the end of Dhul-Hijjah due to caregiving duties or fear of arrest are also exempt.
The segment concludes by noting a historical disagreement between Sheikh Tusi and the majority of jurists regarding the permissibility of performing these rites early.
