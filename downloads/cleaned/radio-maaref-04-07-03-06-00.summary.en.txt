This radio program analyzes the view of Allamah Isfahani, who argues that the principle of "repelling probable harm" is an innate instinct rather than a rational or customary rule. He posits that this behavior is a natural drive for survival found in both humans and animals, lacking any basis in rational law or social convention. Consequently, this principle cannot serve as a "statement" to override the legal maxim of "punishment without explicit prohibition." Therefore, in cases where an action might be forbidden, the principle of caution remains dominant, requiring individuals to refrain from the act. If the principle were rational or customary, it could potentially negate the need for caution, but its classification as an instinct preserves the integrity of legal caution. The program further clarifies that social disapproval of failing to flee is due to a lack of common sense, not the inherent immorality of the act. Ultimately, the discussion concludes that natural instincts cannot be used to derive legal rulings, necessitating reliance on rational and textual evidence instead.
