The radio program "Treasure of Happiness," featuring Hujjat al-Islam Falahati, explored the concept of "Shamat" (criticism or reproach) through the lens of the Quran and Hadith. The speaker emphasized that while criticism is not inherently a vice, it becomes a moral flaw when intended to harm, exceeds its limits, or is done publicly and repetitively. Quranic verses were analyzed regarding the divine reproach awaiting those who prioritize worldly life over the hereafter and reject monotheism. The discussion also highlighted the story of Prophet Jonah and the Quranic emphasis on recognizing individuals by their tone, noting that language is considered an action in Islamic jurisprudence. Hadiths from the Ahl al-Bayt (AS) were cited, stating that publicly criticizing a fellow believer is forbidden, and God may afflict the critic instead of the criticized. A key point was that while the thought of criticizing may arise involuntarily, restraining it and not expressing it is a moral obligation. Finally, it was advised that even if someone deserves criticism, it must be done with proper etiquette and without repetition to avoid falling into moral vice.
