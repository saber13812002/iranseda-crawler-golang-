In this episode of the radio program "Ganj-e Sa'adat," Professor Mohammad Hadi Falavi examines the concept of stinginess from the perspective of Islamic narrations. Citing a hadith from the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH), he categorizes individuals based on their behavior regarding consumption and generosity, defining stinginess as a state where a person neither gives to others nor even cares for themselves. The central argument is that stinginess is not merely a moral flaw but stems from a fundamental doubt in God and damages one's practical monotheism and worship. Imam Ali (AS) described stinginess as a sign of deviation from the path of servitude and obedience to God. Furthermore, Imam Sadiq (AS) emphasized that a stingy person lacks certainty in God's reward and essentially harbors ill will toward the Divine. Finally, stinginess is characterized as a collective of vices that, if allowed to take root, leads a person toward other moral corruptions and places them under the dominion of Satan.
