The program's fiqh section discussed the rules for distributing waqf (endowments) among children and grandchildren, covering concepts like combination, priority, equality, and preference.
It was emphasized that if the waqf document is silent, the default rule is equality among heirs unless the donor's intent clearly indicates otherwise.
A key point noted was that specific distribution structures usually apply only to the first and second generations and do not extend to subsequent ones.
In the second part, the instructor stressed the necessity of tawassul (seeking intercession) through the Ahl al-Bayt, especially for youth, as a condition for the acceptance of worship.
He categorized tawassul into three levels: seeking it solely for personal needs, a combined approach for oneself and the Imam, and the highest level based on pure love for the Ahl al-Bayt.
Through anecdotes involving marja' (religious authorities), he illustrated that the pinnacle of tawassul is forgetting oneself and focusing exclusively on the love for the Ahl al-Bayt.
This spiritual state not only fulfills one's needs but also transforms the relationship with God from one of fear and greed to one of love and affection.
The program concluded with advice to maintain a balance between striving for a halal livelihood and preserving a spiritual connection with the Ahl al-Bayt.
