In this radio program, the host argues that Iran possesses immense natural, human, and infrastructural wealth in sectors such as oil, gas, agriculture, and transportation, providing a strong foundation for development. However, the primary challenge lies in "economic governance" and the lack of integrated management, which leads to inefficiency in utilizing these resources. The main criticism targets the state-centric economy, which suffers from a lack of independent oversight and competition, resulting in the brain drain of skilled professionals like doctors and engineers. Additionally, the country's extensive borders with neighboring nations are highlighted as a geopolitical advantage for bypassing sanctions and expanding regional trade, yet restrictive regulations currently limit this potential. In the cultural and scientific sphere, while Iran ranks highly in scientific production, the fragmentation of scientific institutions and the absence of a comprehensive governance roadmap hinder the translation of this output into sustainable growth. Ultimately, the discussion emphasizes that the government must shift its role from a direct provider to a policy-maker and supervisor. By adopting a unified national plan, the state can prevent brain drain and effectively convert its existing strong infrastructure into tangible national progress.
