The radio program "Kimia" emphasized the concept of worshipful monotheism, stressing that acts of worship must be performed solely for God's pleasure.
The program detailed various forms of shirk, including essential, attribute, action, and worship-related shirk, as violations of this principle.
The second part of the show addressed religious questions from followers based on the fatwas of Islamic scholars.
Key rulings included the obligation for women to cover their legs in front of non-mahrams and the validity of the Witr prayer if it ends after the Fajr adhan.
The permissibility of bank deposits within the framework of Sharia contracts and the method for making up missed fasts from puberty were also explained.
Regarding prayers for the deceased, it was clarified that voluntary prayers can be dedicated to them, but obligatory prayers cannot be performed as a paid service or gift.
Finally, the program stated that participating in computer games for the purpose of winning prizes is religiously prohibited according to Ayatollah Sadrani.
The show aimed to enhance religious awareness and provide answers to the theological and legal questions of its audience.
