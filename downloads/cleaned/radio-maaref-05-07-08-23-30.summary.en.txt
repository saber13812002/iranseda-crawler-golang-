This radio program presents a dramatic retelling of the book "In the Ambush of the Red Rose," which chronicles the life of Martyr General Ali Siad Shirazi. The narrative begins during his student days in Isfahan, where witnessing the massacre of civilians led him and his companions to embrace revolutionary ideals within the army. Siad Shirazi organized three-person cells to prepare the military to counter potential coups and support the people. He broke the oppressive atmosphere at the artillery training center by supporting young conscripts, which led to his conflict with anti-revolutionary officials and eventual arrest. While detained, he heard the sounds of the revolution's victory and the chants of "Allahu Akbar." Upon his release, he prioritized maintaining order in the barracks and preventing the looting of assets over immediate celebrations. The story highlights how a military officer used political and moral awareness to play a pivotal role in the revolution's success and the preservation of the military structure.
