Ayatollah Javadi Amoli, in his exegesis of Surah Al-An'am, emphasizes the importance of "traveling the earth" to understand cosmic signs and historical artifacts, noting that this requires specialized expertise and ijtihad. He analyzes the Quranic shift from third-person narration to direct address to distinguish between the punishments faced by past nations and the current community. The scholar presents the argument for the Day of Judgment using God's "Mercy" as the middle term, arguing that a Merciful God would not annihilate humans who possess the potential for eternal life and deserve reward. He clarifies the distinction between external compulsion and divine necessity, asserting that God's mercy stems from His essential nature and beautiful names rather than any external law. In this view, while nothing governs God's essence, His supreme names govern His other attributes, creating a hierarchy of perfection from which mercy flows. Consequently, the resurrection of humans is not merely a possibility but a necessary reality, confirmed by the Quran's absolute certainty.
