The radio program first examines the uprising of Zayd ibn Ali in Kufa and his summons to Damascus by Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik. Hisham humiliated Zayd and falsely accused him of embezzling funds, leading to Zayd's return to Kufa where he launched a rebellion that ultimately ended in his martyrdom. The discussion then addresses Imam Sadiq's (AS) stance on this uprising; while he did not openly endorse such revolts due to their lack of preparation and suicidal nature, he did not forbid them either and deeply mourned Zayd's death. It is noted that Zayd intended to transfer power to the rightful Imam if victorious, and Imam Sadiq was aware of Zayd's status. In the second part, the instructor details the Battle of Uhud, describing it as a retaliatory conflict following the Muslim victory at Badr. He explains that the Muslim defeat resulted from archers abandoning their strategic hill position to collect spoils, which allowed Khalid ibn al-Walid to launch a surprise attack from the rear. This defeat significantly damaged the military reputation of the Muslims and resulted in the martyrdom of 70 individuals, including Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib.
