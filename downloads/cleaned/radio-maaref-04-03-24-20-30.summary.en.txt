Haji Khanum Nasrat Amin, recognized as the first female mujtahid, established a unique position in religious seminaries through works like "Ma'ad" and "Naf'at al-Rahmaniyya," which reflect her spiritual insights. She founded the "Fatima School" and a girls' high school to provide free education in jurisprudence and principles, while maintaining a simple lifestyle that balanced her scholarly status with family duties. In the second part of the program, Ali Reza Nikpour recounts his captivity during the Iran-Iraq War, detailing how he was wounded and subsequently taken prisoner by Iraqi forces. He describes the harsh conditions in Basra, including severe shortages of water and food, and the neglect of his wounds in Iraqi hospitals. Nikpour also highlights the bravery of his comrades and the difficult process of transferring prisoners to Baghdad despite open wounds and inadequate medical care. These narratives depict the resilience and steadfastness of the freedmen during the most challenging periods of their captivity.
