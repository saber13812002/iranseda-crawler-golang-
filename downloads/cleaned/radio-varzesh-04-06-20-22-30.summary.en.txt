Amir Mirzayan recounts his playing career, highlighting his time at clubs such as Bank Melli, Pas, Zob Ahan, and the highly successful Tehran Electric team. He discusses the formation of the Pakizeh team and their championship win in the Tehran league, crediting coaches like Jamshid Vatandoust for their success. His international experience in Romania and Thailand, where he was named the league's best player, is described as a pivotal part of his professional journey. After retiring, Mirzayan transitioned into coaching, leading various teams including Shahin, Steel Ahvaz, Golgohar Sirjan, and Pars South. He criticizes the structural issues in Iranian football, arguing that non-football administrators hinder the sport's development. Mirzayan advocates for greater recognition of talents from remote provinces and emphasizes the importance of ethical training alongside technical skills. He identifies Ali Daei as the best coach in Iranian football history and expresses his commitment to serving the sport through future-oriented management.
