In this segment of the program, Ayatollah Misbah Yazdi examines two primary plagues that affect the Islamic community. The first social plague is the erosion of trustworthiness and honesty, which undermines the foundation of mutual trust and collective life. The second individual plague is the hardening of hearts and the lack of humility before the truth, leading to indifference toward religious realities. Citing the Quran, he contrasts the Jews, who became enemies of Islam due to their hardness of heart, with the Christians, who embraced faith more readily due to their softness and emotional response to verses. These virtues, such as trustworthiness, are not exclusive to Muslims but are rational axioms accepted by humanity even before Islam. The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) was also trusted by people before his prophethood due to his character as "Al-Amin," which was a key factor in the success of his mission. Subsequently, Ayatollah Makarem Shirazi discusses the jurisprudential issue of the appropriate time for changing the Ihram state during Hajj. He analyzes four jurisprudential opinions and, through the interpretation of narrations, proposes reconciling the permissibility of delaying Ihram in Umrah with its disapproval in Tamattu' Hajj.
