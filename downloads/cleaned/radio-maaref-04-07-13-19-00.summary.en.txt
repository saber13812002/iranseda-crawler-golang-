In this radio program, the concept of anger is analyzed from both praiseworthy and blameworthy perspectives, emphasizing that sacred anger is justified only when it stems from defending divine limits and opposing evil.
It is crucial that anger remains within the boundaries of Sharia and law, avoiding physical violence or insults, except in cases of public sin or betrayal where silence is impermissible.
Historical examples, such as Imam Ali's (PBUH) response to wine-drinkers and the Prophet's (PBUH) handling of a troublesome neighbor, serve as models for managing anger through public opinion rather than aggression.
The second part of the discussion, based on narrations from the book of Kafi, clarifies that asceticism does not mean abandoning the world entirely, but rather detaching one's heart from material pleasures.
Imam Baqir (PBUH) explains that worldly pleasures are largely limited to food, clothing, and sexual gratification, and believers should not measure their worth against these transient benefits.
The core message is that humans should view the world merely as a bridge to attain divine pleasure and paradise, avoiding long-term worldly ambitions.
Finally, listeners are advised not to grieve over lost worldly gains nor become arrogant over acquired ones, remaining always prepared for the transition to the afterlife.
