This radio program explores the intrinsic link between jurisprudential rulings and the belief in the Day of Judgment in the Quran. The central discussion revolves around the verse in Surah Al-Ma'idah where prophets state, "We have no knowledge," during the Day of Judgment. The speaker critiques Fakhr Razi's three interpretations, arguing that prophets possess knowledge of their followers' intentions through divine instruction. The phrase "We have no knowledge" is clarified not as absolute ignorance, but as an acknowledgment that absolute hidden knowledge belongs solely to God. Citing narrations and Al-Mizan's tafsir, the speaker explains that since every being is limited, knowing beyond one's limits requires a connection to God, who is omnipresent. Thus, the prophets' knowledge is derived entirely from divine grace and their connection to the Divine. This discourse highlights that faith in the afterlife and hidden knowledge forms the foundation of human accountability and the execution of divine laws.
