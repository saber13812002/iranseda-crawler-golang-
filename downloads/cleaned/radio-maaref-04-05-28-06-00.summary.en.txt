This program analyzes the concept of Velayat-e Faqih as both a theological and jurisprudential principle rooted in the human nature of monotheism and freedom. The speaker emphasizes that this form of authority signifies guardianship and management, distinct from the legal guardianship of minors or the incapacitated discussed in other fiqh chapters. It is argued that because the subject of this authority is the "act of God," it possesses a theological nature and is considered a core principle of the faith, unlike the Sunni view which treats it as a mere jurisprudential choice by the people. The speaker asserts that the Iranian people are not minors or fools but wise individuals who are under the authority of the Infallibles and the qualified jurist. As a legal entity and guardian, the qualified jurist issues authoritative rulings that are obligatory even for himself to follow. This specific type of authority differs from mutual ethical or social obligations and is based on divine appointment to maintain order and justice during the period of occultation. Ultimately, the fundamental difference between Shia and Sunni perspectives lies in the Shia view of authority as a necessary divine mandate, whereas Sunnis regard it as a jurisprudential duty for the people to select a leader.
