The radio program begins by discussing the high spiritual value of the tongue in worship and its dual nature as a tool for both sin and devotion.
It emphasizes that while the tongue is the easiest organ to misuse, it holds the highest rank in communicating with God.
The discussion then shifts to jurisprudential principles, specifically the validity of the apparent meaning of words, known as Zawaahir.
The speaker argues that Zawaahir are authoritative based on the general consensus and the lack of rejection by religious authorities.
The view of the Akhbari school, which denies the authority of the Quran's apparent meaning for non-infallibles, is critically examined.
The critique posits that the exclusive understanding of the Quran by the Ahl al-Bayt serves as a specific modifier for the general public.
Therefore, the public's understanding of the Quran's apparent meaning is contingent upon consulting the interpretations of the Infallibles.
Ultimately, the Usuli position is affirmed, stating that the Quran's apparent meaning is authoritative but must be understood in conjunction with the guidance of the Infallibles.
