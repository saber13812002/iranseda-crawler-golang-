In the first section, the concept of "Wajh Allah" is interpreted as the manifestation of divine attributes rather than just the absolute essence of God.
These manifestations remain eternal despite the transience of the physical world, allowing humans to connect with God through them.
The verse "You do not will except that Allah wills" indicates that human free will is part of divine will, not determinism.
The infallibility of the Imams stems from their profound knowledge and strong will, not from a lack of desires or temptations.
The second section examines Akhund Khorasani's view on denying apparent rulings and positing that real rulings are contingent on knowledge.
The main critique is that principles and indicators serve as excuses for ignorance rather than independent rulings, and removing them causes chaos.
The actual obligation for a person in a state of ignorance is to follow these principles to prevent greater harm.
This discussion demonstrates that the Islamic jurisprudential system is built on maintaining order and preventing chaos, not just pursuing reality without tools.
