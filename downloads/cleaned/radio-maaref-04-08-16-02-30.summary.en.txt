In this radio program, Dr. Mohammad Reza Jabbari addressed historical claims regarding the enemy army's celebration after the martyrdom of Imam Hussein's companions, noting that such behavior primarily stemmed from the Uthmanite faction and those with weak faith. He also clarified that the people of Kufa were mostly weeping and grieving when the Ahl al-Bayt's caravan entered the city, refuting reports of widespread public joy at that specific moment. The second part of the discussion introduced the First, Second, and Third Martyrs, identified as Shams al-Din Muhammad Amili, Zayn al-Din Amili, and Qazi Nur Allah Shushtari, respectively. The professor explained that their martyrdoms resulted from political conspiracies and plots by the enemies of Shiism during various historical periods. Finally, the Battle of Qadisiyah was highlighted as a pivotal event in Islamic conquests, where the Muslims achieved victory over the Sasanian army through the strategic guidance of Imam Ali. This battle was not an ethnic conflict but a continuation of the Prophet's mission to spread Islam, despite some criticisms of specific commanders' actions. The scholarly works of these three martyrs remain widely used in Islamic seminaries, keeping their legacy alive.
