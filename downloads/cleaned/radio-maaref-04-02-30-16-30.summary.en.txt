In this episode of the faith-based Q&A program, the respondent addresses two key questions regarding revenge and backbiting. He rejects both the extreme passivity of Christianity and the aggressive retaliation of Judaism, asserting that Islam advocates for self-defense strictly within the framework of law and justice. He emphasizes that children must be taught to seek their rights through legal channels rather than resorting to physical force or the "law of the jungle." While kindness and forgiveness should prevail in friendly and internal environments, firmness and strictness are required when dealing with foreign enemies. Regarding backbiting, the speaker clarifies based on jurisprudence that criticizing officials is generally prohibited, with very specific exceptions. One such exception is warning the public about a judge who rules unjustly, solely to prevent people from seeking his judgment. This permissible backbiting is limited strictly to the specific flaw of judicial injustice and must not extend to personal attacks. Ultimately, the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) serves as the ideal model, demonstrating a balanced approach that carefully distinguishes between when to show leniency and when to take firm action.
