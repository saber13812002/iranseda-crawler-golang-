In the radio program "Hekayat-e Azadegi," Jamshid Ghalami Natanj recounts his captivity experience following the Fath al-Mubin operation in Iraqi camps. He describes the humiliating treatment by Iraqi soldiers, including their denial of his right to pray and insults regarding his religious identity, as well as journalists' attempts to expose his true identity using old documents and photos. Ghalami highlights the harsh living conditions in the camps, such as severe shortages of water, hygiene supplies, and clothing, which led to the spread of diseases like lice and skin infections. Despite these hardships, the continuous recitation of the Quran played a vital role in maintaining morale, group cohesion, and a spiritual atmosphere among the captives. The program also introduced the book "Small Apple Slices," the memoirs of Hossein Ali Rahimi, a freedom fighter who returned to Iran after an exchange and later became a martyr. The broadcast concluded by emphasizing the inherent nature of the conflict between Iran and the United States and the Iranian people's resistance against enemy plots.
