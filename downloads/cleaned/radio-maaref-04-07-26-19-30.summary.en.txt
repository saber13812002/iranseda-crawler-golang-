In this radio program, Professor Mohammad Hadi Fallah discusses the linguistic harm of "ta'ir," which refers to fault-finding or nitpicking. He emphasizes that divine piety involves self-restraint and controlling one's ego, specifically by avoiding the habit of criticizing others, even in private settings. The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) advised an Arab man not to retaliate against someone who pointed out his flaws, warning that doing so would share the sin and its negative consequences. The discussion also references a narration from Khidr and Moses (AS) instructing believers not to rebuke others for their mistakes. A primary cause of this behavior is the delusion of being smarter or more perceptive than others, which indicates a lack of focus on self-improvement. The program concludes with the recitation of prayers from the Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya, highlighting the importance of monotheistic ethics and seeking God's pleasure in daily life.
