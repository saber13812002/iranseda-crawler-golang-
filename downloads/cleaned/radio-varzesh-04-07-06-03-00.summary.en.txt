Mohammad Bidarian, a former Iranian national swimmer, recounts his two-month training stint in Croatia, where he competed against Olympic medalists. He highlights the fundamental psychological gap between training and competition, noting how stress often causes athletes to forget their strategies during crucial moments. During the 2012 Olympic qualifiers, Bidarian qualified for the final but withdrew due to a draw against a representative of the "illegal regime," citing national principles. Despite suffering a torn ACL, he competed in the London Olympics driven by the motivation to redeem his absence from the 2008 Games. Following persistent injuries and a lack of adequate support for his return, he retired from competitive swimming. Bidarian currently works as a sports consultant in governmental bodies, emphasizing the need for young, educated professionals in sports management. He advocates for the integration of veteran athletes with the younger generation to drive transformation in the country's sports administration.
