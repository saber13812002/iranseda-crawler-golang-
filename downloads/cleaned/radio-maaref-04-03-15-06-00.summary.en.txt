In this radio program, the theological discussion on the validity of consensus (Ijma) and related Quranic verses continued. First, objections to using the "Path of the Believers" verse to prove consensus were raised, clarifying that "believers" refers specifically to the Infallibles rather than all Muslims. The discussion then shifted to the "Middle Nation" verse, which proponents of consensus interpret as evidence of the justice or superiority of all Muslims. However, multiple arguments were presented to refute this interpretation, noting that the Quran explicitly mentions the presence of wrongdoers and sinners within the Ummah. It was also argued that the concept of "middle" in this verse does not imply universal justice or superiority, but rather points to a specific characteristic. A key point in the debate is the verse's connection to witnessing on the Day of Judgment, which is only compatible with infallibility and divine knowledge. Therefore, this verse cannot serve as proof for the validity of consensus among all Muslims. The final conclusion is that only the statements of the Ahl al-Bayt, as the specific and infallible group, are authoritative, while general consensus lacks validity.
