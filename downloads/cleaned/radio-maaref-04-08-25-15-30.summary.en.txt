In this radio program, experts examined the philosophy of political jurisprudence, emphasizing fundamental methodological differences in deriving legal rulings. A key discussion focused on the role of reason and custom in legitimizing new transactions like insurance, where the absence of religious objection was deemed sufficient. The issue of the right of retention and high mahr was used to illustrate how a consequentialist approach can prevent family breakdown. Differences in views on the Guardianship of the Jurist were also analyzed; while some consider it rational and necessary, others limit it to public order due to weak narrations. The ultimate goal of political jurisprudence is to regulate individual and social life based on divine norms and enrich the legal system. This approach allows Islamic jurisprudence to adapt to contemporary challenges and better address societal needs. Finally, the program highlighted recent advancements in teaching political jurisprudence in seminaries and the necessity of continuing this path to fill theoretical gaps.
