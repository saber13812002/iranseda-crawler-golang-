This program examines the debate surrounding hijab, distinguishing between theoretical ijtihad and practical moderation. The speaker argues that some individuals use apparent tolerance to undermine the foundations of Islamic jurisprudence and religious rulings. By differentiating between private and public spheres, it is emphasized that public attire is a social act subject to law and the rights of others. In response to liberal arguments, the fundamental difference between Islamic and liberal bases is clarified; Islam mandates the preservation of collective dignity in public spaces, whereas liberals cannot legally enforce dress codes based on their own principles. The discussion also notes that some objections to hijab stem from emotional or practical issues rather than theoretical disagreements. Finally, the effort to preserve spiritual values and defend religious symbols is described as a form of cultural jihad against anti-Islamic currents.
