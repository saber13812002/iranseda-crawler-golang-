In the news segment, the head of Iran's Health Insurance Organization announced the electronic provision of radiotherapy services and the approval of new dental care processes. The report also highlighted the successful domestic production of a polymer serum for separating umbilical cord stem cells, a product previously available in only ten countries. Additionally, a spokesperson for a hospital in Gaza reported that over 70,000 people have contracted hepatitis and urgently requested the reopening of crossings for treatment. In the main discussion, Professor Mohammad Javad Fallah emphasized the virtue of honesty, citing religious texts and Saadi's poetry to argue that truthfulness is an existential trait that benefits individuals in the long run despite short-term costs. Subsequently, Professor Mohammad Hadi Falahati outlined four ethical guidelines for scientific supervision and evaluation in the "Ganj-e Sa'adat" program. These guidelines include avoiding personal biases, possessing adequate expertise, acting with responsibility before God, and adopting a comprehensive rather than reductionist perspective. He stressed that evaluators must refrain from judging based on personal grudges and should decline assignments if they lack the necessary specialized knowledge.
