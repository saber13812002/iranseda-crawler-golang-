In this program, Ayatollah Javadi Amoli analyzes the issue of slavery through the lens of the end of Surah Al-Ma'idah. He emphasizes that true servitude is exclusive to God, as no one can force internal faith or spiritual submission through coercion. The historical roots of slavery are attributed to the dominance of lust and anger over human reason, turning knowledge into a tool for oppression. Islam prioritizes the spiritual life based on monotheism, accepting slavery only as a necessary defensive measure rather than an inherent right. Captivity is permitted only when killing is justified, with the ultimate goal being the education and eventual liberation of the captive. Islamic jurisprudence systematically dismantled the institution of slavery by promoting manumission as a religious duty or recommendation. The text clarifies that enslaving a Muslim is strictly prohibited, and slavery was only a temporary state for those whose freedom threatened the community. Finally, the distinction between human discretionary freedom and the legislative right of servitude to God is explained to highlight that freedom from idolatry is essential for true spiritual life.
