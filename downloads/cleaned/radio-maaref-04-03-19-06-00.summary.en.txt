This program analyzes verses 106 to 108 of Surah Al-Ma'idah, based on Ayatollah Javadi Amoli's interpretation, to discuss the jurisprudential rules of wills and testimony.
A key point is the "preliminary obligation" of making a will when signs of death appear; it is not inherently obligatory if heirs already know the deceased's debts, but it is required to inform them.
The Quran and authentic narrations permit the testimony of two People of the Book (Jews and Christians) in financial matters if two just Muslim witnesses are unavailable.
This permission is conditional on necessity to ensure that the rights of Muslims are not compromised and the will remains valid.
A distinction is made between the jurisprudential concept of "returning the oath" and the Quranic "rejection of the oath," which occurs when the initial testimony is proven false.
The historical story of two Christians claiming a cup serves as a practical example, illustrating that once a falsehood is discovered, the initial testimony is voided and replaced by a new one.
Finally, it is emphasized that the testimony of non-Muslims is strictly limited to financial wills and does not extend to guardianship or other legal matters.
