This radio program introduced the Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) contract as a common tool for financing infrastructure projects. In this model, a foreign private entity constructs the project and, in exchange for not receiving direct payment, operates it for a specified period, such as 15 to 20 years, before transferring ownership to the government. Although BOT is recognized in the Foreign Investment Incentives and Protection Law, there are significant religious and legal sensitivities regarding energy resources, which the Guardian Council deemed problematic, leading to its approval by the Expediency Discernment Council. Consequently, BOT usage is limited in the oil and gas sector and is more commonly applied to projects like highways. The discussion also clarified that foreign investment is not inherently contradictory to the resistance economy, provided it facilitates technology transfer, increases employment, and safeguards national interests. Various financing methods, including leasing, factoring, and forfaiting, were analyzed from both jurisprudential and legal perspectives. Finally, it was emphasized that precise contract design and adherence to religious principles are essential to prevent foreign dominance over national resources.
