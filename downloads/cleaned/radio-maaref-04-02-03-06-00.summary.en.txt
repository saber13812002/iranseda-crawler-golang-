This text analyzes verses from Surah Al-Ma'idah that define the relationship between Muslims, Jews, polytheists, and Christians. Through an exceptional syllogism, hypocrites are shown to lack true faith by adopting disbelievers as their allies. Although the verse appears to state that Jews and polytheists are hostile while Christians are friendly, the Quran specifies four conditions for this Christian proximity. These conditions include the presence of compassionate clergy and monks, a lack of arrogance in society, and the acceptance of truth after hearing divine verses. The occasion of revelation points to the Muslim migration to Abyssinia and their asylum under the Negus. There, Ja'far ibn Abi Talib recited the Quran, converting the Negus and his companions, serving as a practical example of the verse. Consequently, the verse does not refer to Christianity as a whole but to specific groups of People of the Book with particular moral and intellectual traits. Ultimately, the Quran uses historical context to show that accepting truth and kindness toward believers depend on the presence of sincere intellectual and practical leaders in that society.
