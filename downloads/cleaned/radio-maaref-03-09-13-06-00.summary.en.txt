This program begins with an exegesis of the Surah An-Nisa, the most detailed Medinan chapter, which focuses on establishing the Islamic system and dismantling pre-Islamic customs.
The commentator emphasizes the inherent equality of men and women based on Quranic verses, rejecting any notion of gender superiority while assigning economic responsibilities to men.
Financial independence for women is affirmed through mahr and the right to earn, alongside strict protections for orphans against the exploitation of their assets by guardians.
The discussion on inheritance outlines five distinct scenarios, demonstrating that Islamic law does not rigidly favor men, as women may sometimes inherit equal or larger shares.
Combating superstitions and cultural innovations, such as the belief that death causes solar eclipses, is presented as a primary mission of prophets to liberate society from ignorance.
The Quran asserts the universality of Islam by mandating justice and truthful testimony, even against one's own relatives, regardless of social status or wealth.
The preservation of trusts and the prohibition of betrayal are highlighted as absolute commands that apply universally, irrespective of the owner's religion or nationality.
Finally, the necessity of obedience to the guardianship of the jurist and the validity of the Imams' traditions are identified as the core pillars for governing the Islamic society during the occultation.
