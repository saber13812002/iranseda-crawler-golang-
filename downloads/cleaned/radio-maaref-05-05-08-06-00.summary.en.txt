This radio program examines the theological and jurisprudential rule regarding the obligation to avert probable harm, analyzing the required degree of probability for a rational ruling. The first perspective, held by some Mu'tazilites and Imami scholars, argues that any level of probability, no matter how weak, necessitates the prevention of harm. Conversely, the second view, defended by scholars like Qadi Abd al-Jabbar, al-Khwarazmi, and Sheikh Mufid, stipulates that the probability must reach the level of "zann" or a likelihood exceeding fifty percent. Mu'tazilite theologians emphasized the necessity of guarding against both certain and probable harms, whether in the hereafter or in worldly matters. In the Imami school, scholars such as Sayyid Murtada and Sheikh Mufid insisted on the requirement of certainty or strong probability when applying this rule to issues like taqiyya and the obligation to seek knowledge. Although some texts appear to support an absolute interpretation, a closer look at later works reveals that the intended meaning of "probable harm" is harm whose occurrence is reached through certainty or strong probability. This discussion highlights that the boundary between a rational obligation and its absence lies in the degree of confidence regarding the occurrence of the harm.
