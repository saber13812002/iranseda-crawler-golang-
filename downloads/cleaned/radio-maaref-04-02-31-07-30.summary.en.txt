In the radio program "Let's Be Kind," the National Organ Donation Day and the visitation day of Imam Reza (PBUH) were commemorated, emphasizing the ethical and religious significance of organ donation.
The guest highlighted statistics showing that filling out donation cards could save eight lives daily, framing brain death as an opportunity to revive others' lives.
From a jurisprudential and narrational perspective, organ donation was presented as a praiseworthy act that brings joy to families and is rewarded with forgiveness and peace in the afterlife.
The second part of the program analyzed anger from a psychological and religious standpoint, citing Imam Ali's (PBUH) narration that defines anger as a form of madness and cognitive impairment.
Anger was described as a reckless, brakeless vehicle that deprives individuals of logical decision-making and leads to irrational, harmful behaviors.
This vice creates darkness and ignorance not only at the individual level but also within groups and society, with the solution being the avoidance of anger and seeking divine purification.
Ultimately, listeners were encouraged to achieve moral virtue and inner peace by completing donation forms and managing their anger.
