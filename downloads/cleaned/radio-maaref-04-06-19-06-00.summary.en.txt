This text presents Ayatollah Javadi Amoli's interpretation of the opening verses of Surah Al-An'am, focusing on the themes of contemplation and argumentation against atheists and polytheists. The commentator utilizes verses from Surah Az-Zumar to describe sleep as a temporary death and waking as a temporary resurrection, thereby elucidating the reality of the afterlife and the soul's departure. By reinterpreting Arabic terms with material roots, such as "mamarah" and "fuq," he demonstrates how the Quran conveys abstract spiritual concepts through tangible language. The discussion on monotheism reaches its peak by emphasizing the concept of "Shay'" (pervasiveness), asserting that God is present everywhere without being colored by material objects. The commentator argues that accepting the principle of eternal existence necessitates the acceptance of religion and revelation as a necessary guide. Ultimately, the lack of critical thinking and the denial of fundamental principles like monotheism and prophethood are identified as the root causes of humanity's rejection of divine signs and miracles.
