The radio program begins with a jurisprudential discussion on the necessity of verifying a spouse's conditions and the role of Istikhara in resolving doubts. The instructor then addresses a theological query regarding the relationship between "remembering God" and "divine agency," explaining that human will operates within the scope of God's will. In the second segment, a listener asks about the fate of human emotions and psychological traits after death. The response clarifies that acquired traits accompany the soul to the Barzakh realm, although certain worldly acts like fasting lose their specific meaning due to the change in physical form. It is also noted that negative effects of sins may be purified in the afterlife, whereas disbelief remains a permanent barrier to salvation. The program concludes by emphasizing the importance of gradual self-improvement among youth and their critical role in the nation's scientific and social progress.
