This radio program discussed the principles governing Islamic criminal policy regarding crimes against physical integrity. A key principle is the "legality of crime and punishment," which, based on Quranic verses, requires that prohibited acts and their penalties be clearly defined and publicized in advance. The jurisprudential rule of "the ugliness of punishment without notification" was highlighted as a rational and self-evident principle that emphasizes the necessity of full awareness for the offender, even surpassing Western legal standards. Another fundamental principle is the "individuality of punishment," rooted in the rule "no harm shall be inflicted" and the Quranic verse stating that no soul bears the burden of another. This principle explicitly forbids punishing innocent individuals for the crimes of others, contrasting with pre-Islamic tribal practices where entire tribes were punished for a single member's offense. Commentators emphasized, citing the verse "a free man for a free man," that retribution must be directed solely at the perpetrator and not their relatives or tribe. These principles demonstrate that Islam articulated concepts over fourteen centuries ago that were only later adopted in modern Western legal systems. The program concluded with the recitation and translation of verses from Surah Zumar.
