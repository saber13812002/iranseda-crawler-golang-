The radio program "Andisheha" began by announcing the acceptance of students for undergraduate seminary courses in 1404. It then critiqued the Western liberal theory of law, based on Allameh Tabatabaei's views, which posits law as the sole tool for social order while excluding personal ethics and beliefs from state intervention. Critics argue that this approach allows rulers to exploit coercive, cultural, and service-oriented tools for their private interests. In contrast, the Islamic theory grounds social order in monotheism, ethics, and social justice. In a religious society, citizens feel bound by monotheism to uphold ethics and jurisprudence even in their private lives. Consequently, Islam expands the scope of life to include the afterlife and links monotheism with moral virtues. This ensures the proper execution of law extends beyond external surveillance, creating a comprehensive system of divine governance.
