This radio program discusses the reactivation of UN sanctions against Iran via the snapback mechanism following a Security Council vote. Legal and economic experts emphasize that while this vote technically reinstates UN sanctions, their scope is strictly limited to nuclear and missile-related activities, with no direct impact on oil trade, essential goods, or the banking system. Analysts argue that the US's unilateral sanctions have historically inflicted far greater economic and psychological damage on Iran than the UN measures. The primary objective of the West is to exert political and psychological pressure to force Iran to disclose nuclear information and accept ballistic missile restrictions. Iran, along with allies like Russia and China, challenges the legal validity of this mechanism, citing Europe's failure to uphold commitments after the US withdrawal from the JCPOA. To counter these pressures, the program highlights the importance of deepening economic cooperation with Shanghai Cooperation Organization and BRICS members, including the adoption of common currencies to reduce reliance on the US dollar. Ultimately, it is predicted that despite the negative psychological atmosphere, the direct economic impact of these specific UN sanctions will be minimal.
