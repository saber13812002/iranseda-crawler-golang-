In this radio program, the host first addressed a listener's question regarding the influence of satanic symbols, advising against their use due to their negative spiritual and psychological impact. The claim that "non-prayers are infidels" was refuted, clarifying that prayer is a sign of faith and that true infidels do not pray by definition. The discussion then shifted to criticizing the Salafi movement's rejection of rational deduction in understanding religion, emphasizing the necessity of combining intellect with scriptural evidence. A significant portion of the program was dedicated to the historical context of Shah Abdul Aziz Dehlawi's book "Tuhfat al-Ashraf" and Mir Hamid Husain Hindi's comprehensive rebuttal, "Ibqat al-Anwar." These works emerged during the British colonial era in India, where such polemics were often used to sow discord between Shia and Sunni Muslims. Mir Hamid Husain Hindi systematically refuted the accusations against Shia principles, particularly regarding the Imamate, using extensive historical and theological evidence. The program concluded with brief mentions of the Supreme Leader's economic directives and solidarity with the people of Gaza.
