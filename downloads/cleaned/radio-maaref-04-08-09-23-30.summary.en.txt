This radio segment begins with a philosophical discussion on the nature of mental existence and its distinction from external substance. The speaker argues that a mental quality, or "nafsani kiyf," inherently exists within the mind and remains a mental quality even if hypothetically realized externally, rather than becoming a physical substance. He demonstrates that proponents of the "similitude" theory could make similar claims, highlighting that the core difference lies in the definition of ontological existence. The discussion critiques the notion that mental forms transform into external substances, labeling such assumptions as logically flawed. Following a recitation of religious prayers and blessings, the program shifts to socio-political themes. The speaker emphasizes the crucial role of Islamic culture in saving youth from division and foreign dependency, contrasting it with previous secular or royalist influences. He views universities as the driving force behind government and military strength, stressing the need to strengthen faith against anti-Islamic currents. The broadcast concludes with the announcement of Fajr prayer times in various cities and a preview of upcoming programs.
