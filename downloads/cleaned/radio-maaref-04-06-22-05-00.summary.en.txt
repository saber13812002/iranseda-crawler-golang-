In this radio program, the necessity of choosing a preacher who is personally purified and learning from pure sources like the Quran and Nahj al-Balagha is emphasized based on a hadith of Imam Ali.
It is stressed that eloquence alone is insufficient for guiding society without internal moral purification and adherence to Islamic teachings.
The discussion then shifts to the jurisprudential issue of "presumption in unknown time" regarding the overlap of wudu (ablution) and sleep.
The prevailing view, supported by the speaker, is that when the timing of both events is unknown, presumption applies to both and they become contradictory.
However, if the time of one event is known, presumption applies only to that known event, as the unknown one involves a potential contradiction of certainty.
The reasoning is that in cases of unknown timing, there is a doubt regarding the annulment of certainty, which cannot override the original certainty.
Finally, the views of other jurists, such as Akhund Khorasani and Mohaghegh Nayini, are examined and their differences from the majority opinion are explained.
