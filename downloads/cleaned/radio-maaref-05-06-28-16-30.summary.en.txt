In this radio program, Professor Maleki addresses various Quranic questions from listeners. The discussion begins with the dual meaning of the word "Khasim" in the Quran, referring to both an enemy and a defender, emphasizing that God does not defend the treacherous. The reason for placing Prophet Ismail's forehead on the ground during the sacrifice is explained as a practical method similar to slaughtering a sheep, symbolizing the complete submission of both father and son. The differences between Surah Nas and Surah Falaq are clarified regarding seeking refuge from whispers, natural disasters, and sorcerers, with a specific warning against the destructive nature of envy. The term "Kharasan" is defined as those who judge based on conjecture and guesswork rather than verified knowledge. A significant portion of the discussion focuses on the physical nature of the Prophet's Mi'raj, supported by verses from Surah Al-Isra and An-Najm that confirm the journey involved both body and soul. Finally, the possibility of Raj'ah (return) is validated through Quranic examples of resurrection, and the gathering of animals on Judgment Day is explained as a means to ensure divine justice.
