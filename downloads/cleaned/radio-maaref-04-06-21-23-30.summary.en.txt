The text examines the role of reason in understanding theological and philosophical concepts, arguing that while reason excels in grasping universals and the unity of existence, it requires intuition or revelation to comprehend specific details. The speaker rejects inductive and general methods, favoring the theoretical rational approach based on Transcendent Theosophy to prove divine justice and the gradations of existence. According to the principle of the primacy of existence, essences are conventional, while existence is real and possesses varying degrees from weak to strong. Theoretical reason confirms that a single species can have individuals with different existential levels, ranging from the Prophet to an ordinary human. Since God is the Absolute Generous One, the requirement of His mercy and justice is to bestow existence upon all levels of creation, including the weakest. The differences observed in the world stem not from injustice, but from the requirements of the Absolute Essence and the varying capacities of creatures. Finally, emphasizing the role of culture and faith in societal stability, the text concludes that fearing God is the only source of security and the basis for striving in the path of truth.
