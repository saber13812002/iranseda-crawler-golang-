In this program, Professor Jafari examines historical accounts regarding the martyrdom of Ali Asghar, confirming that the infant was martyred in Imam Hussein's (PBUH) arms during a farewell moment near the tents, rather than on the battlefield. He explains that the absence of a miracle in Karbala aligns with the divine tradition of human free will and testing, unlike the Battle of Badr where divine aid was granted due to the believers' piety. The discussion also traces the history of mourning rituals, identifying Umm Salama as the first mourner and noting that public, organized ceremonies began during the Buyid and Safavid eras. Finally, the number of Imam Hussein's (PBUH) companions in Karbala is cited as approximately 72, comprising about 20 to 21 members of the Ahl al-Bayt and the rest being his supporters. These individuals were under the Imam's protection and care, although some of his relatives, such as Muhammad Hanafiya, did not participate in the journey.
