In this program, Mr. Jafari recounts his memories of captivity in Iraqi camps, detailing physical hardships such as a broken hand and surgery under critical conditions. He describes the severe restrictions on movement and hygiene that the prisoners faced during their detention. The central theme of the discussion is the concept of "sincerity" in action, which reached its peak in captivity because there were no human witnesses. Prisoners demonstrated pure devotion by secretly helping one another, washing wounds, and worshipping in the darkness, proving that only God was the witness to their sincere deeds. In the second section, the program analyzed narrations from the book of Kafi regarding the chapter on justice. Imam Sadiq (AS) identifies justice as the best deed, encompassing fair treatment of people, solidarity with fellow believers, and constant remembrance of God. This section emphasizes that justice means desiring for others what one desires for oneself and rejecting for them what one rejects for oneself. The program concluded with the recitation of the Qunut prayer from the Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya, seeking rain and divine mercy.
