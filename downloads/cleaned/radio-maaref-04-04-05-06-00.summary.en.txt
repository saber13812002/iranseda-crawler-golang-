This theology lesson examines the validity of consensus (Ijma) by analyzing the Quranic verse "Obey Allah, the Messenger, and those in authority." The first interpretation argues that since the verse mandates referring to the Quran and Sunnah only during disagreement, consensus itself becomes authoritative when there is no dispute; however, this is refuted by the argument that obedience to divine sources is a general, unconditional rule. The second interpretation, proposed by Fakhr al-Razi, suggests that "those in authority" must be infallible because their obedience is absolute and parallel to the Quran, implying that the consensus of "those who resolve and bind" is valid. This view faces three major criticisms: the ambiguity of identifying "those who resolve and bind" is more difficult than identifying an infallible leader; Fakhr al-Razi contradicts himself by acknowledging the infallibility of Ali (AS) in other contexts; and the linguistic concept of "collective generality" required for this proof is rare and unsupported by the verse's apparent "distributive generality." Ultimately, relying on prophetic traditions and the Prophet's duty to interpret the Quran, it is concluded that "those in authority" does not refer to general consensus, rendering these arguments insufficient to prove the authority of Ijma.
