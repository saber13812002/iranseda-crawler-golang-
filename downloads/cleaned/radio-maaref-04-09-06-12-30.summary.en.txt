In this lecture, the late Ayatollah Motahhari presents the concept of Mahdism not merely as a Shia belief, but as a universal and definitive promise found in the Quran and narrated by both Shia and Sunni traditions. He cites historical and literary evidence, including the poetry of great figures like Daqbal and Hafez, to demonstrate that the anticipation of the Imam's appearance has been deeply rooted in Islamic history since its inception. Motahhari emphasizes that waiting for the Mahdi does not imply suspending religious duties or indifference to injustice; rather, it is our duty to facilitate the path to perfection through gradual reform and moral purity. He likens the appearance of the Imam to a fruit ripening rather than an explosion, suggesting that it requires the completion of evolutionary stages in human society. According to him, human history follows a spiral trajectory where current global chaos, while severe, represents a higher level of development compared to past eras. Thus, Islam preserves hope for a bright future against modern nihilism and despair by promising ultimate justice and peace. Finally, he urges the audience to understand Mahdism as a grand global philosophy aimed at saving humanity from its moral and political crises.
