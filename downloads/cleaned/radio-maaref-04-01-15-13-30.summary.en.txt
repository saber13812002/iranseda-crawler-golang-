The text refutes the "culture of the time" and "language of the people" perspectives, which view revelation as merely a human, fallible product. The speaker argues that while prophets use the language and culture of their audience to establish communication, this does not imply endorsing superstitions or denying the divine, infallible nature of God's word. These ideas originated in the West, among thinkers like Schleiermacher and Kant, as a strategy to save Christianity from rational and scientific challenges by labeling the Bible's language as symbolic and non-cognitive. This trend, introduced to Iran as "religious rethinking," aims to escape contradictions by denying the cognitive value of religious texts. In contrast, citing Fatimah (SA), the Quran is described as the "Leader of Truth" and "Truthful," meaning it aligns with reality and serves as sufficient proof for human guidance. Therefore, blindly adopting Western interpretations that reduce revelation to a psychological experience is incompatible with Quranic principles. The segment concludes by highlighting the importance of poetry among youth and the need to improve its quality alongside its quantity.
