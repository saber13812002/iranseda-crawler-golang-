This segment of the program examines Ayatollah Mischbah Yazdi's philosophical arguments regarding the nature of "mental existence" and its relationship with "external existence."
The speaker critiques the views of Sayyid Sa'd al-Din and Muhaqqiq Davani, arguing that mental existence is neither entirely distinct from the external world nor identical to the external object.
Instead of accepting these two opposing views, a third position is proposed where mental existence is "quasi-mimetic" and narrates the external reality.
Although this mental entity is a quality, its natural capacity for narration provides us with awareness of the external object.
The discussion then shifts to analyzing the imposed war and the role of external powers like the US and the USSR in weakening Islam in Iran.
These powers utilize media and propaganda to discredit the Islamic Republic and contain Islam within Iran's borders.
Their ultimate goal is to prevent the spread of Islam to other countries and to establish a Western-dependent Islamic republic in Iran.
Finally, the importance of strengthening Islamic propaganda and resisting external media conspiracies to preserve the society's religious identity is emphasized.
