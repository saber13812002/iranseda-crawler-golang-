In this program, Professor Hassan Rahimpour refutes the notion that Mahdism is exclusively a Shia concept, emphasizing its acceptance as a religious necessity across Sunni schools of thought. He references two historical letters from Imam Mahdi (AJ) during the Minor Occultation, addressing the theological doubts and challenges faced by early followers after the martyrdom of Imam Hassan (AS). In the first letter, the Imam urges his followers to remain steadfast against fitna and to avoid "su' al-munqalib," which refers to moral or political failure at the end of one's journey. He warns against internal deviations and enemy conspiracies, stressing the importance of maintaining unity and obedience to the Wilayah during difficult and covert periods. The second letter details a historic covenant between the Imam and 313 of his special companions at the dawn of the global uprising. This pact establishes 30 ethical and behavioral principles for the revolution's leaders and 8 reciprocal commitments from the Imam to ensure justice and simplicity. These principles include abstaining from theft, violence, usury, corruption, and elitism, while respecting the rights of all humans, including non-Muslims. The discussion concludes that the Mahdian revolution is founded on global justice, human equality, and the simple living of its leaders, requiring followers to adhere to these principles while awaiting the appearance of the Imam and the establishment of a unified global government.
