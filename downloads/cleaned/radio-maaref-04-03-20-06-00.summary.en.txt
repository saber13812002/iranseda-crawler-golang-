This program features a tafsir of verses 108 and 109 of Surah Al-Ma'idah by Ayatollah Javadi Amoli, emphasizing the necessity of social order through lawmaking, execution, and judicial adjudication. The commentator distinguishes between "agent justice" (personal morality) and "action justice" (correct performance of duty), arguing that a law enforcer must act justly in their official capacity even if they are not personally sinless. In the Islamic judicial system, judges and witnesses must be just, whereas the oath-taker does not necessarily require moral justice, though their oath must be truthful. The discussion further differentiates between jurisprudential justice (performing duties and avoiding prohibitions) and ethical justice (internal balance and moderation), where the intellectual, appetitive, and irascible powers are kept in check. Finally, the Quran highlights the role of prophets as witnesses who observed their communities' actions in this world and will testify to them in the divine court on the Day of Judgment. This testimony illustrates that this world is the container for "bearing" actions, while the Day of Judgment is the time for "testifying" and final adjudication.
