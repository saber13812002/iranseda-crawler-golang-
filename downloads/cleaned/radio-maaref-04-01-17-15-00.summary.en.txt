In this radio program, Professors Mohammadi and Davodi emphasized the religious obligation of hijab as a natural and essential Islamic principle, citing Quranic verses and narrations. Relying on verse 59 of Surah Al-Ahzab and verse 31 of Surah An-Nur, they demonstrated that proper covering is a definitive jurisprudential ruling rather than merely a moral recommendation. The discussion highlighted that hijab must effectively conceal a woman's hidden adornment from non-mahrams, noting that simply covering the hair is insufficient. Referring to the views of Ayatollah Makarem Shirazi and both Shia and Sunni jurists, they stated that covering the entire body except the face and palms is a consensus among Islamic scholars. These texts indicate that while covering has natural roots predating Islam, the religion refined it with specific, purposeful details. Finally, the distinction between moral and jurisprudential obligations was clarified, positioning hijab as a specific religious duty with defined rulings that transcends the realm of mere ethics.
