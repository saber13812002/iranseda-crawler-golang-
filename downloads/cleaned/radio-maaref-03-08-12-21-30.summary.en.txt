This program examines the Islamic concepts of "consent and aversion" and "loyalty and disavowal" within jurisprudence and theology. It highlights that consenting to sin, even if not a distinct legal offense, indicates spiritual corruption, while approving others' sins, particularly disbelief, is considered a major sin or even disbelief itself. The levels of forbidding evil include heart, tongue, and action, and neglecting these duties while approving evil renders a person a "living dead." Regarding loyalty and disavowal, allegiance to hostile non-Muslims is forbidden, yet peaceful human relations with non-hostile non-Muslims are encouraged by the Quran. The program also notes that granting asylum to non-Muslims for religious inquiry is a religious duty, and their life and property are respected. Finally, it warns that sharing state secrets with non-Muslims is strictly prohibited, and hypocrites face the lowest level of punishment due to their accumulated spiritual corruption.
