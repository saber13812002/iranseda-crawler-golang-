In this radio program, the speaker emphasizes the critical role of clerics and universities in raising a generation of faithful and pious individuals, arguing that knowledge without faith can be harmful. He warns that a lack of piety among society's educators leads to corruption and the deviation of youth, suggesting that only through the cooperation of religious and academic sectors can the nation be protected from external plots and internal decay. The speaker calls for a transformation in cultural and educational programs to prioritize faith and ethics over purely material concerns. The second part of the program featured jurisprudential answers to listeners' questions, including the ruling that the prayer of a person with weak concentration is valid if the essential acts are performed correctly. It was also stated that partnering with an alcoholic in a lawful business is permissible, though avoiding such partnerships is recommended. Regarding marriage, the intention not to pay the mahr does not invalidate the contract but is morally condemned. Finally, the rulings on khums for work vehicles and the payment of missed prayers and fasts for the deceased were explained by representatives of the marjas.
