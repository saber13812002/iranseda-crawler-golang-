The program begins with an analysis of the poem "Hast az Malal" by Mohtasham Kashani, where the poet expresses grief for Imam Hussein despite acknowledging God's majesty.
The poets emphasize the significance of the poem's twelve stanzas as a symbol of a Shia perspective and the balance between form and meaning in expressing divine concepts.
Poetic images, such as comparing the Imam's wounds to fish scales and stars, demonstrate Mohtasham's imaginative power in depicting the event of Karbala.
The second part features the memoirs of Mostafa Davoudi, a freedom fighter who was captured in the Fifth Karbala Operation in 1986.
Davoudi recounts the harsh conditions of his captivity in Iraqi camps, including siege, injury, and severe food and hygiene restrictions.
He recalls the prisoners' efforts to maintain morale by organizing religious and artistic ceremonies during the Fatr and Bahman months.
The segment concludes with a description of the prisoners' intense reaction to the news of Imam Khomeini's passing and their resistance against Iraqi suppression to hold mourning rituals.
The program ends with a review of the book "Nordin, Son of Iran," highlighting its honest and unadorned narration of the Sacred Defense.
