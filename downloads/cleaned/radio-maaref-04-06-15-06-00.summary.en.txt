In this radio program, the Surah Al-An'am is introduced as the "Surah of Contention," distinguished by its sudden revelation and the escort of seventy thousand angels.
A key feature of this surah is the frequent repetition of the word "Qul," which signifies the presentation of approximately forty theological arguments to establish monotheism and refute doubts.
Fakhr al-Razi explains the fundamental difference between verses of core beliefs, which are revealed at once, and those of secondary rulings, which are revealed in response to specific events.
Linguistic and theological concepts, such as the distinctions between "Hamd" (praise), "Madh" (commendation), and "Shukr" (gratitude), are analyzed using the narrations and supplications of the Ahl al-Bayt.
Verse 36 of Surah Al-Jasiyah is identified as the most comprehensive verse in praising God due to its absolute attribution of praise to Him and its reference to universal lordship.
Based on the principle that one who does not thank the created cannot thank the Creator, acknowledging the blessings of creation is a prerequisite for thanking the Absolute Creator.
Ultimately, Surah Al-An'am is presented as a collection of rational and transmitted proofs that resolve all doubts regarding the divine essence and attributes.
