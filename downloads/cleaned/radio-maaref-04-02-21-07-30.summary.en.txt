The radio program begins by emphasizing the vital role of traditional customs in preserving collective identity and spiritual bonds.
Reciting the Quran at home is highlighted as a key tradition that brings blessings and expands one's life.
The introduction of technology and a neglect of religious principles have created a significant gap between generations and communal beauty.
The second part of the show, featuring Professor Fala, explores the ethics of anger from the perspective of the Quran and Hadith.
Anger is identified as a moral vice rooted in ignorance, which can lead to both worldly and otherworldly punishments.
The professor stresses the importance of self-discipline and overcoming internal anger rather than confronting others.
He also warns that a society where anger and aggression replace logical dialogue is extremely dangerous.
Finally, controlling anger and maintaining patience against provocations are presented as the most difficult yet essential moral tasks.
