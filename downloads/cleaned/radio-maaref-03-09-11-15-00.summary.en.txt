This radio program examined criminal liability defenses for AI systems, including lack of free will, insanity, and factual or legal mistakes.
Legal experts argue that if an AI system voluntarily enters a situation leading to a crime, it can be held accountable.
However, from a jurisprudential perspective, current AI is considered "weak" and lacks a human soul, so liability is attributed to the creator, designer, or user.
The concept of "insanity" in AI is compared to severe system malfunctions, such as damage caused by toxic gases.
Regarding legal mistakes, if a system's data is incomplete, it cannot be deemed guilty, which contradicts the definition of strong AI.
Strong AI, possessing complete big data and precise algorithms, should not be subject to factual or legal errors.
Therefore, any errors in current systems stem from data or design flaws, placing ultimate responsibility on the humans involved in its creation and use.
