The discussion explains the different levels of receiving divine revelation, noting that the Perfect Human sometimes receives knowledge directly from God and at other times through intermediaries like angels. In certain stages, the Perfect Human acts as a teacher to angels and serves as the channel for divine grace to descend to lower levels. The Quran emphasizes that every nation had a prophet, but it is not necessary for every city or village to have a separate messenger, as the central prophet's message reaches all surrounding areas. During periods of interregnum, if a person did not hear the call of a prophet, God does not punish them because the divine argument was not fully established. The concept of "God's testimony" in the Quran is not merely about God's internal knowledge but serves as a formal argument to prove the Prophet's mission. This divine testimony, along with the testimony of angels and People of the Book, constitutes definitive proof of the Quran's truth and the Prophet's status. Ultimately, God's testimony is the greatest form of witness, encompassing all other testimonies and completing the argument against those who deny the revelation.
