The lecture on Arbaeen presents Karbala as the simultaneous manifestation of humanity's ultimate wickedness and salvation. It argues that individuals, through free will, either ascend to martyrdom by following their innate light or become obstacles to truth by succumbing to their ego. The narrative from the book "Let Your Wound Bleed," told through the perspective of Harb ibn Yazid Rayahi, illustrates this internal conflict. Despite commanding the army, Harb is deeply remorseful about fighting Imam Hussein and resists the harshness demanded by Shamar. He laments the siege of the Euphrates as the start of an unequal war and witnesses the profound spiritual contrast between the Imam's companions and his own indifferent soldiers. The story highlights Harb's internal struggle and his eventual hesitation to leave the army, emphasizing the eternal and internal nature of Karbala in human history.
