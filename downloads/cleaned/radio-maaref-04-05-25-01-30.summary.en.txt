The program begins by interpreting Imam Sajjad's prayer, which states that a human life should either be spent in God's obedience or be taken away if it becomes a tool for Satan. Imam Sajjad spent forty years transforming the post-Karbala fear and passivity of the people into a dynamic revolutionary society, turning the event from an unequal battle into a lasting revolution. He kept the memory of Imam Hussein's thirst and martyrdom alive through meaningful weeping in public places like markets and weddings, thereby awakening public consciousness. The second part of the program presents a literary narrative from the book "Sayyidman Hosseini," depicting the Arbaeen walk of a teacher named Mohammad Taghi. Despite the blisters on his feet, Mohammad Taghi is inspired by seeing a man who has walked for years with burnt feet. Among the crowd, he meets a young Basij member named Alireza and hears the miraculous story of a wheelchair user who was healed after the Arbaeen journey. These experiences motivate Mohammad Taghi to continue his path toward Karbala with greater enthusiasm, despite the heat and fatigue.
