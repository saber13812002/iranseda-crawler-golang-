In the narrative of the book "Jamandeh az Pesar," Salareh retreats to a cellar after his son Sohrab is wounded during the 15 Khordad uprising, consumed by guilt and anger.
Upon seeing the bullet that hit his son, Salareh becomes convinced that one of his own allies fired the shot, leading him to dark thoughts of revenge.
Zabih, Salareh's friend, attempts to calm him down and prevent him from acting on these dangerous impulses by addressing his massive error in judgment.
In a coffeehouse scene, Zabih confronts Esmail Haft and Houshang, refusing to accept his money and instead threatening to expose their secrets to demand justice.
The story illustrates how a lack of insight and impulsive decisions can drive individuals toward revenge and a cycle of violence, whereas true insight could have prevented such tragedies.
