The radio program explores the ancient Iranian cultural roots, emphasizing the pillars of benevolence, justice, and spirituality.
It argues that ancient Iranians believed sovereignty belonged only to divinely chosen individuals possessing the "Farr" or divine glory.
This ideal was found in the Prophet's family, leading to the emergence of Sufi-Shia movements against Mongol and Timurid oppression.
Movements like the Sardarans and Meshashayis in Khorasan and Khuzestan exemplified this fusion of mysticism and Shia Islam.
These scattered currents eventually united under the leadership of Sheikh Safi al-Din Ardabili's descendants and the Qizilbash.
Shah Ismail Safavi formalized Shia Islam in Tabriz, transforming this social unity into the Safavid state and birthing a new Iran.
The Safavid government was not merely religious but a political entity with distinct national and geographical dimensions.
This 250-year period integrated national, local, and historical elements to establish the foundation of modern Iranian identity.
