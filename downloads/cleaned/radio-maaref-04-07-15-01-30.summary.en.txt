The sermon by Hujjat al-Islam Saeidi focuses on the Prophet's (PBUH) concern regarding the "fitna of comfort" and the community's negligence during times of abundance. Using the metaphor of misusing medicine, he warns that desires for food and sexual pleasure become toxic if not exercised in their proper context. The primary fear is that material wealth leads to a lack of gratitude and deteriorates social relationships and moral character. The program then shifts to the memoirs of Colonel Mohammad Hossein Ansari, detailing his ten years of secret captivity in Iraqi black holes. He describes how prisoners created a battery from pomegranate peels to listen to news from Iran while remaining unknown to their families. The final segment features the memoirs of the chaplain of the 159th Brigade during the Holy Defense. It highlights the unique bond between the chaplain and Martyr Rostami, who recited the Quran for the chaplain's father on the eve of the Karbala 5 operation. These narratives demonstrate that faith and hope in victory remained steadfast even under the harshest conditions of captivity and war.
