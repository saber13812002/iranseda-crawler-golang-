In this radio program, Dr. Dehghan and Dr. Milani discussed the impact of gender on the process of Islamic legal deduction. They rejected two extreme views: the claim that jurisprudence is inherently male-dominated and the notion that jurists are merely passive mirrors of the text. Dr. Dehghan provided examples from Shia jurisprudence showing that jurists have historically adapted rulings to better reflect women's evolving social status and rights. Dr. Milani cautioned that women's entry into the field of jurisprudence should not be driven solely by the desire to produce "feminist" rulings or satisfy modern ideological demands. Instead, he argued that the primary goal should be to eliminate gender-based biases to achieve a more accurate and objective understanding of religious texts. Therefore, the participation of women in jurisprudence should aim to enhance the quality of legal deduction and reduce cognitive errors, rather than simply shifting ideological directions.
