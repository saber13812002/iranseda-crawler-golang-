This program emphasizes the principle of fulfilling one's duties under any circumstances and avoiding despair, drawing on Quranic stories and narrations.
The main focus is on five hopeful actions taken by Imam Sajjad (PBUH) after the event of Karbala, which serve as a model for religious action.
The first action was indirect education of workers and people through daily moral lessons in work environments.
The second action involved promoting the religion by releasing trained workers and merchants to spread Islamic messages to distant regions.
The third action was delivering sermons without a direct audience by compiling the Sahifa Sajjadiya, which remains influential today.
The fourth action was holding subtle, symbolic mourning ceremonies in markets to keep the memory of the martyrs alive during the siege.
The fifth and final action was transforming the duty of enjoining good and forbidding evil from verbal to written and practical forms to prevent direct harm.
The overall conclusion is that no religious duty is absolute in form; with creativity, one can implement them even in difficult conditions.
