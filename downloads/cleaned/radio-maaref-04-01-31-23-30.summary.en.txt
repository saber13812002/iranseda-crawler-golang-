The program guest distinguishes between mere speed and true precedence, emphasizing the priority of performing good deeds and virtues. By citing Quranic verses and narrations, he highlights the special merit of being first in faith, prayer, jihad, charity, and serving parents. Historical figures such as Imam Ali, Khadija, and Bilal are presented as prime examples of those who achieved precedence in righteousness. The guest identifies obstacles like stinginess, procrastination, hesitation, obsession, and projection as barriers to attaining these spiritual rewards. The discussion then shifts to the necessity of national economic and scientific self-sufficiency to preserve independence. He argues that relying on enemies for basic needs, such as grain, is the most bitter form of dependency. Consequently, universities and various sectors of society must strive for domestic production to reduce external reliance. Finally, he calls for cultivating a culture of precedence in good deeds to prevent the loss of valuable opportunities.
