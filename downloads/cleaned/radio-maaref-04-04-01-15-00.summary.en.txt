In this episode of Radio Ma'aref, the military attack by the Zionist regime on the Islamic Republic of Iran's broadcasting headquarters was analyzed from jurisprudential and legal perspectives. Dr. Seyed Mohsen Qaemi, citing the Quranic verse "until they hear the Word of Allah" and the jurisprudential concept of "Aman" (safe conduct), identified journalists as heralds of truth who deserve even greater protection than other non-combatants. He emphasized that Shia jurisprudence, based on the criterion of "non-combatancy," extends specific protections to journalists during armed conflicts, viewing their role in documenting history and preserving collective memory as a fundamental right. Dr. Mohammad Saleh Tashkiri, drawing on international law, highlighted the principle of distinction between military and non-military targets as the basis for protecting media personnel. He noted that while there is no specific binding convention for journalist protection, international norms are evolving toward enforceability, and news reports serve as crucial evidence in international courts for prosecuting war criminals. The program concluded that attacking media institutions is a blatant violation of human rights and international law, representing a crime against historical memory and the rights of future generations.
