This radio program examines the pivotal role of Rashid al-Din Delvari in the resistance of southern Iran against British colonialism and domestic despotism. As the military arm of religious authorities, Delvari's seizure of Bushehr and his defiance against British forces symbolized the unity of religious and national spirit in the face of foreign aggression. The British, viewing Iran's ancient civilization and resistance potential as a threat to their interests, plotted its disintegration and weakening through the 1919 treaty and their support for Zionism. These schemes included attempts to establish a Zionist regime in the region and use Iran as a transit terminal for Jewish migration to Palestine, culminating in the 1921 coup and the imposition of foreign influence. Delvari's martyrdom, resulting from internal betrayal, is considered a turning point in the anti-colonial struggle, now commemorated as the National Day of Struggle Against Colonialism. The analyst emphasizes that Britain and its Zionist allies continue to target regional security and Iran through projects like Greater Israel. Ultimately, Iran's resilience and national identity are highlighted as the primary factors that have thwarted these centuries-old plots and serve as a major deterrent against any aggressor.
