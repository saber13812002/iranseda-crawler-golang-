This radio program examined the challenges of supporting the elderly from the perspectives of Islamic jurisprudence and Iranian law.
According to jurisprudence and civil law, providing for parents is the duty of their children, but this obligation is conditional upon the children's financial ability and the preservation of their own livelihood.
If children lack the financial means, the responsibility for supporting the elderly shifts to the community and the government.
Religious texts and narrations from Imam Ali (AS) emphasize the Islamic government's duty to care for the elderly and orphans, particularly those who are too modest to ask for help.
Article 29 of the Constitution also mandates the government to provide social security and support for vulnerable groups, including the elderly.
The program guests stressed the need to prioritize governmental duties and establish practical mechanisms to identify and support those in need.
They also highlighted legal gaps regarding the accountability of government officials for inaction and the necessity for clearer laws to ensure better enforcement.
The discussion concluded that supporting the elderly is a fundamental right that must be practically realized by the state and society when children are unable to provide.
