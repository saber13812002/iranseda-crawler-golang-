This program addresses the question of Iran's service to Islam, arguing that this contribution stems from the deep faith and love of Iranians for the religion. The speaker highlights the pivotal role of Iranians in authoring major theological texts, which helped preserve Islamic doctrines. Furthermore, the resistance of Iranians against moral corruption and Zandiqah movements in early Islam demonstrates their stronger adherence compared to Arabs. A significant portion of the discussion focuses on the role of Iranian merchants in spreading Islam to India, Pakistan, China, and Indonesia through trade and cultural interaction rather than force. The influence of the Persian language in the religious culture of these regions and the continuation of Iranian religious rituals there serve as evidence of this profound impact. Beyond proselytization, Persian art, architecture, and literature are presented as primary tools for promoting Islam and describing the Prophet. Finally, the speaker warns that attempts to strip the Islamic character from the Persian language are essentially a war against Iran's religious and historical identity.
