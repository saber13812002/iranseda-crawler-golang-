The program began with religious prayers and hymns dedicated to Imam Mahdi before transitioning to a discussion on linguistic vices. Professor Mohammad Hadi Falahati introduced "Ta'yeer," or nitpicking and fault-finding, as a moral and religious flaw that contradicts reason and ethics. He argued that true intellect does not criticize other intellects, suggesting that such behavior stems from internal conflicts and hidden intentions. The distinction between constructive scientific critique or religious duty and destructive, personal nitpicking was clearly explained. The professor emphasized that these behaviors cause tension and psychological harm in various contexts, from personal relationships to political factions. Finally, listeners were advised to examine their intentions and the consequences of their words to avoid this vice and promote social harmony.
