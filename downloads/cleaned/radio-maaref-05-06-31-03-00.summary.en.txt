The program begins by explaining the concept of "Takhliq," which refers to purifying the soul from sins to achieve human perfection.
The core principle of this path is to wish for others what one wishes for oneself and to avoid envy and backbiting.
Citing narrations from the Ahl al-Bayt, a true Shia is defined as someone who strives to meet the needs of others and avoids betraying them.
The second part of the program is dedicated to answering religious questions from followers of various Maraji.
Regarding beer, its consumption for pleasure is forbidden, but it is permissible in necessary amounts for medical treatment if no alternative exists.
For temporary marriage, the permission of the father or grandfather of a virgin girl is required for validity, though their physical presence is not mandatory.
In the context of Wudu, if a person doubts whether they have performed the head wiping, they must repeat it and continue the ritual.
The Prayer of Signs (Salat al-Ayat) is obligatory in four cases: solar eclipse, lunar eclipse, earthquake, and frightening celestial events.
