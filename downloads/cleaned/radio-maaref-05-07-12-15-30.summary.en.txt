This radio program explored the legal and jurisprudential challenges regarding mahr (dowry) and the role of government regulation.
A key point was the 1997 Mahr Adjustment Law, which preserves the purchasing power of older mahr amounts through inflation calculations, benefiting women.
Conversely, the legality of setting a legal cap on mahr was debated, with prominent jurists like Ayatollah Sadrani opposing it as a violation of spouses' autonomy.
However, it was suggested that instead of direct caps, the government should use indirect methods or "soft law" to reduce excessive mahr amounts.
These methods include limiting the wife's right of retention, refusing to hear cases involving very high mahr, or imposing taxes on mahr.
The goal of this approach is to create incentives for spouses to agree on reasonable mahr amounts without directly interfering with their choice.
The program concluded with the recitation and translation of verses 1 to 56 of Surah Al-Muddaththir, addressing themes of warning and social justice.
Finally, listeners were encouraged to follow future programs for a deeper analysis of these legal solutions.
