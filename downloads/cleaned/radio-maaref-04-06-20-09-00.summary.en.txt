In the first segment, experts emphasized that the snapback mechanism can automatically reactivate international sanctions against Iran without a new UN Security Council vote, due to the US veto power.
They argued that European nations are activating this mechanism not because of Iran's alleged violations, but to exert maximum pressure and force Tehran to capitulate in negotiations.
The discussion then addressed the false dichotomy between "people's livelihood" and "religious values," asserting that compromise with the West does not lift sanctions but incurs even higher costs.
The experience of the last decade demonstrates that even with the closest relations with the West and full compliance with the JCPOA, the harshest sanctions were imposed, indicating that economic issues stem from internal management weaknesses.
Economic resistance and domestic prudence were presented as the primary solutions for improving livelihoods, with Yemen cited as a model of resilience against sanctions and military attacks.
In the final segment, the history of the Friday Prayer's formation in early Islam and its role as a major social and political gathering were explained.
The presence of the Infallible Imams and their emphasis on attending Friday Prayer highlight its importance in maintaining Muslim unity and cohesion against enemy plots.
