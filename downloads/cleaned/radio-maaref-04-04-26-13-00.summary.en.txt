In this radio program, the teachings of the late Ayatollah Mohammad Taghi Falasi on the Islamic duty of "enjoining good and forbidding evil" were presented. He emphasized that public oversight of laws is a mandatory religious obligation in Islam, unlike in many Western countries where it is treated as an optional right. A key point of discussion was the distinction between individual sins and social sins, arguing that social evils like tyranny and foreign domination pose far greater threats than personal transgressions. Citing the uprising of Imam Hussein (PBUH), the speaker illustrated that fighting against social corruption is obligatory, even if it leads to martyrdom. The lecture also addressed the Quranic verse "Take care of yourselves," clarifying that it does not imply isolation but rather advises true believers to maintain their own righteousness when societal reform seems impossible. Based on narrations, it was explained that while individual piety is prioritized in corrupt societies, the duty to forbid evil remains as much as one is capable. Ultimately, the core message is that preserving the integrity of Islam and combating social injustice takes precedence over individual matters.
