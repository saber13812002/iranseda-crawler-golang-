This program interprets Quranic verses to emphasize avoiding extremism in religion, criticizing the People of the Book for their excessive interpretations and attribution of imperfections to prophets.
The commentator explains that God explicitly mentions the imperfections of prophets but uses allusion for non-prophets to prevent them from being adopted as deities.
The concept of "ghulu" (exaggeration) is defined in two ways: exceeding the necessary limit, which includes hyperbole, and exceeding the permissible limit, where no heart is considered correct.
The distinction is drawn between "speech from the mouth," which is superficial, and "speech from nature," which is rooted in intellect and essence.
The verse "Do not follow the desires of a people who have gone astray before" refers to those who are themselves misguided and lead others astray by following their own desires.
The historical cursing of the Children of Israel by David and Jesus due to continuous sins and refusal to accept prohibition of evil is cited as an example of this deviation.
Finally, the necessity of independent research in religious principles or following credible, rational sources is stressed, while warning against following the changing and misleading desires of corrupt groups.
