The radio program "Zamzam Ahkam" began with religious chants and prayers seeking refuge from the fire of hell. In the first segment, Hujjat al-Islam Vahidpour explained the Sharia ruling on using prohibited substances for medical treatment, emphasizing that it is only permissible in cases of genuine necessity when no halal alternative exists, subject to the patient's assessment and doctor's confirmation. The fatwa section addressed various listener questions, including the validity of prayer and fasting during work-related versus non-work travel, and the desirability of meeting the needs of animals like cats. The fatwa of Ayatollah Makarem Shirazi was cited, stating that attending wedding ceremonies involving sin is prohibited unless participation is strictly necessary. For women who missed fasting due to breastfeeding, paying kaffarah was recommended instead of making up the fasts for days after Ramadan. The issue of khums on purchasing a car was also discussed, clarifying that it is not required if the vehicle is a life necessity but is due if bought solely for comfort. Finally, the prayers and fasts of an individual whose mother did not perform the postpartum bath were deemed valid, with no need for makeup prayers.
