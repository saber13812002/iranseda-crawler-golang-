This program discusses the theological debate regarding the meaning of "Ahl al-Bayt" in the Verse of Purification and the Hadith of the Cloak. Sharaf al-Din Makhari, in his book "Al-Kalima al-Tahra," examines various opinions, including those of Akrama ibn Abi Jahal and Muqatil ibn Sulayman. Some commentators like Fakhr al-Razi and Alusi prefer a combined interpretation including the Prophet's wives and the people of the cloak, but contextual and narrational evidence supports the exclusive view. The Prophet's action of placing Ali, Fatima, Hasan, and Husayn under the cloak and praying "O Allah, these are my Ahl al-Bayt" indicates restriction and specificity. Multiple narrations from Umm Salama show that she was prevented from entering under the cloak, indicating she was not included in this specific group. Abu Ja'far al-Tahawi also explicitly stated in "Mushkil al-Athar" that these narrations do not include the women. In the second part, the virtues of saying "Alhamdulillah Rabb al-Alamin" after sneezing and during meals are explained based on Shia and Sunni narrations. This phrase is highlighted as a form of gratitude for blessings and a means of warding off misfortune, holding a special place in the hadith literature.
