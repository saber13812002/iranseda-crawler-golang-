The program defines true anticipation of the Mahdi's appearance as a practical and moral readiness to accept his leadership and constant supervision over one's actions. Genuine anticipation requires abstaining from sin, guarding one's tongue from backbiting, and strictly adhering to religious laws, including the payment of the Imam's share and maintaining trust. By citing examples from the lives of great scholars, the speaker demonstrates that even in severe financial hardship, adherence to religious principles and integrity are hallmarks of true anticipators. The discussion then shifts to the concept of "covenant" and fidelity to it, which is rooted in faith and piety, while breaking it indicates a weakness in belief. A covenant with God can be either legislative or ontological, such as the intellect and senses God granted humans to use for the sake of truth. In the jurisprudential section, the issue of "istishab" (presumption of continuity) is examined in cases where there is doubt between two contradictory states, such as ritual purity and impurity. It is concluded that istishab is applicable in known and historical states, but not in unknown and historical states due to potential case ambiguity and the possibility of the certainty being invalidated.
