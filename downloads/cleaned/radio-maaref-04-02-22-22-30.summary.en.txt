In this program, Haghajagha Panahian emphasized the importance of not separating spirituality from materiality, defining spirituality as a deeper rest for the human being. He highlighted that intelligent religious figures, such as Ayatollah Haghighatshenas, do not abandon worldly pleasures but rather utilize them to serve higher spiritual joys. The discussion focused on the concept of "contentment," which signifies acceptance of the past and peace in the present, but does not imply passivity or a refusal to strive for change in the future. Panahian argued that a mind burdened by dissatisfaction lacks the creative power to effect change, whereas contentment combined with trust in God and effort is the key to success and tranquility. Citing religious narrations, he clarified that being content with God's will does not mean abandoning effort, but rather accepting the outcome after fulfilling one's duty. In the second part, Dr. Mirhamid Reza Ghaedri discussed post-revolutionary social cinema, noting that Iranian cinema has consistently responded to social issues, poverty, and class disparities. He highlighted the successes of war and social cinema, emphasizing its role in reflecting societal realities and fostering empathy with various groups, including hardworking drivers.
