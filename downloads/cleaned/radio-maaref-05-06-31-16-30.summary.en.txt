In this program, Professor Mohammadi refutes the notion of sorrow in Paradise, explaining that Barzakh grief differs fundamentally from worldly sadness and that reports regarding Lady Zahra (SA) are largely rhetorical rather than literal. He emphasizes that while repeating dhikr does not change God's perspective, it is essential for purifying the soul, and some prayers are delayed to elevate the supplicant's spiritual rank. Regarding the afterlife, he asserts that Shia Muslims believe in bodily resurrection, distinguishing their view from other Islamic sects that favor spiritual resurrection. The discussion also clarifies that divine justice is deferred to the Day of Judgment because the world lacks the capacity for full retribution, and parents who fulfilled their educational duties are not accountable for their children's sins. Finally, the program addresses the concept of seven heavens and earths, and explains that while women are not appointed as Imams due to incompatibility with social responsibilities and modesty, their spiritual rank in Wilayah can surpass that of prophets.
