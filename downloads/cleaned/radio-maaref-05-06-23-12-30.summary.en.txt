The radio program "Hekayat-e Azadegi" features the testimony of Mr. Bagheri, a former prisoner of war from the Iran-Iraq conflict. He describes the harsh conditions of captivity, including severe beatings, deprivation of basic necessities, and psychological pressure aimed at breaking the prisoners' spirit. The narrative highlights the crucial role of Ayatollah Abutrabee, whose leadership and wisdom helped preserve the physical and mental well-being of the Iranian POWs. Bagheri also recounts how information was secretly transmitted between camps using hidden notes in medicine capsules and the strategic use of radios. He addresses the attempts by fraudulent organizations to exploit the prisoners and the rejection of such claims by religious leaders. This account underscores the resilience, patience, and steadfast resistance of Iranian prisoners against the brutality of the Ba'athist regime. The program concludes by referencing the book "Summer 1369" and emphasizing the importance of documenting these memories for future generations.
