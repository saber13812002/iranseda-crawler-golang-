The ultimate purpose of human creation is to achieve perfection in scientific, ethical, and practical dimensions through deep understanding and adherence to religion.
The Quran outlines two essential stages of learning and action through the verses "so that they may understand the religion" and "so that they may be purified."
Imam Ali (AS) taught his students about detachment from worldly attachments and the importance of sincerity during a visit to the graves.
People are essentially divided into three groups: divine scholars, those learning for salvation, and the masses who are solely focused on material pleasures.
Reaching the rank of divine scholars requires sincerity in actions and a spiritual connection with the Ahl al-Bayt through proper visits.
The philosophical segment discusses the nature of mental existence and the debate among theologians regarding whether it is a substance or an attribute.
Muhakkik Dawani argues that the mental form remains a substance, while Sayyid Sadr al-Din Dashtaki considers it an attribute, rejecting the transformation of essence.
The final conclusion is that mental forms are mental qualities that possess the meanings of substances through primary predication.
