In this program, Professor Javadi Amoli interprets verses 162 to 164 of Surah An-Nisa, highlighting the exception made for "those firmly grounded in knowledge" and true believers among the People of the Book. He argues that true mastery of knowledge is defined by its practical manifestation in one's life, asserting that knowledge without action is transient. These scholars and their sincere followers accept the Quran and the Prophet of Islam without prejudice or stopping at previous revelations. The commentator distinguishes between "ta'bir" (external reality) and "tafsir" (mental concepts) based on Quranic verses, emphasizing the depth of understanding required for the "Rasikhun." He also addresses the grammatical case of "those who establish prayer," interpreting it as a sign of honor for prayer and zakat alongside core religious principles. The lecture posits that the criteria for proving prophethet have been consistent throughout history, leading true scholars to accept the final message. Finally, the discussion touches upon the ruins of ancient civilizations along the route from Mecca to Sham as a lesson for the contemplative.
