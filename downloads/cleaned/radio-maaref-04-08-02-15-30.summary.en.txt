In the radio program "Rahbord," Professor Mehdi Meshkibaf analyzed Imam Khomeini's strategic decisions during the eight-year Iran-Iraq war. He highlighted the Imam's initial rejection of UN Resolutions 514 and 598, citing the lack of security guarantees, unresolved border issues, and the need for deterrence. The eventual acceptance of Resolution 598 in 1988 resulted from a complex interplay of international pressure, Western financial support for Iraq, and detailed military and social assessments. The Imam described this bitter decision as "drinking the cup of poison," yet he viewed it as a necessary step to preserve national interests and counter the US strategy of gradual transformation. Following the acceptance, Iran reinforced its position through military operations like "Morasat" in response to Iraqi attacks. This analysis demonstrates that the Imam's decisions were grounded in religious and political rationality, ultimately benefiting the nation. The program concluded with a recitation and translation of verses from Surah Al-Imran and a message of solidarity with the people of Gaza.
