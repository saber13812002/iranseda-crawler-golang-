In this radio program, Professor Tarashian addresses the challenges of child-rearing and religious education for young children. Based on religious narrations and educational systems, he advises against direct instruction in reading and writing the Quran for children under seven, recommending indirect methods like storytelling and listening to the Quran in a relaxed environment instead. To foster religious interest in four-to-five-year-olds, he emphasizes the importance of attending religious environments without coercion, where facilities for play and activities are available. Using everyday stories to model religious behaviors and leveraging children's tendency to identify with story characters is also presented as an effective approach. The discussion further covers aggressive behaviors in children, noting that punishment and scolding can exacerbate these issues. Instead, planning for group and strategic games at home is suggested to enhance social skills and problem-solving abilities. Finally, it is recommended that children be introduced to work environments through productive and service-oriented jobs with adults, while avoiding retail settings.
