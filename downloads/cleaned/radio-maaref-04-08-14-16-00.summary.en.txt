In the radio program "Porsaman," Professor Mohammadi addressed listeners' theological questions. He categorized knowledge into three branches: worldview, ethics, and legal rulings, emphasizing that true knowledge must be illuminating and guide humans toward their ultimate destination. Regarding charity, he described it as nourishment for the soul and a means to bridge social gaps, effectively converting worldly wealth into capital for the afterlife. The nature of the Barzakh realm was also explained, where believers enjoy heavenly blessings, maintain connections with the physical world, and engage in worship without the burden of legal obligations. Furthermore, the distinction between wealth production, which is encouraged, and hoarding, which is condemned, was clarified. The professor noted that hoarding knowledge without sharing it is also considered a form of accumulation. Finally, the priorities for voluntary charity were discussed, ranging from relatives and neighbors to public projects and collective benefits.
