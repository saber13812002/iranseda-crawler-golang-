The first segment of the program detailed the rituals of Umrah al-Tamattu, including Tawaf, prayer, Sa'i, and finally Taqsir. During Taqsir, pilgrims must trim a portion of their hair or beard and nails to exit the state of Ihram, with a specific prohibition against fully shaving the head for men. In the Q&A section, it was clarified that performing voluntary prayers at home and dedicating their reward to martyrs is permissible without any religious disapproval. For individuals suffering from urinary incontinence, the use of protective devices and maintaining ritual purity before prayer were recommended as necessary steps. The method for cleaning a mosque carpet soiled by a child's urine depends on the child's age and whether they are potty-trained, requiring either one or two rinses. The permissible uses of voluntary charity were explained to be similar to Zakat, focusing on assisting the poor and resolving the financial issues of believers. The program concluded with a spiritual segment featuring a lecture on the wisdom of prayer, describing it as a means to purify the soul from arrogance and polytheism.
