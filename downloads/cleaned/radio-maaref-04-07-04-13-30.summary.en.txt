The text highlights the fundamental difference between oppressive, colonial armies and the revolutionary Islamic army, noting that while appearances may be similar in peacetime, ideological and political distinctions become evident during war.
Drawing on the experience of the eight-year war, the author demonstrates that the Iraqi Ba'athist army collapsed when Western logistical support waned, whereas Iranian forces fought with limited equipment but strong faith.
Based on Nahj al-Balagha, the Islamic army is defined as "God's soldiers," with the primary goal of defending religion, justice, and the nation's security rather than serving colonial interests or suppressing the people.
A true army must combine physical power, military discipline, and spiritual strength; without faith and ethics, an army is merely a soulless war machine.
The reciprocal relationship between the army and society, including economic support and a popular base, is essential for maintaining national authority, as the army must symbolize the country's glory and security against external threats.
Ultimately, a nation's true authority is derived not just from weapons, but from the faith, courage, and commitment of its soldiers to divine and human values.
