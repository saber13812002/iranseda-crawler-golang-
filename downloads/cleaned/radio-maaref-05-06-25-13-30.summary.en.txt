The program begins by outlining five religious duties for Muslims during the occultation of the Imam, emphasizing obedience to scholars, love for the Ahl al-Bayt, adherence to their conduct, support, and visiting their shrines.
The broadcast then examines the criminal case of Ahmad Alaei, who was prosecuted for the accidental killing of his uncle during a wedding ceremony using an unauthorized firearm.
The defendant argued that firing into the air was a traditional custom for celebrations and that he had no intent to kill, claiming the incident was a tragic mistake.
The judge rejected the cultural defense, stressing the importance of legal accountability and the severe consequences of handling weapons irresponsibly.
Alaei was sentenced to three years in prison and the confiscation of his weapon, while his wife was also required to appear in court due to her expired firearm license.
In the concluding segment, Judge Akbari warned against the physical and psychological dangers of shooting in public gatherings and urged citizens to surrender illegal weapons to authorities.
He further emphasized the need to foster a spirit of work, national unity, and motivation to address economic challenges and avoid a state of "neither war nor peace."
