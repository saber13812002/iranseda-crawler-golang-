This text is a personal account of a five-year captivity in Iraqi prisons, highlighting the inhumane conditions endured by the prisoners. The narrator shares old photographs, describing his father's severe emaciation after returning from captivity and the physical and mental hardships of the war. Memories of extreme deprivation, including the lack of water and food, and the unbearable heat in military camps are recounted. The narrative also details torture and humiliation methods used by Iraqi forces, such as forcing prisoners to strip naked and beating them with chains and whips. The narrator emphasizes the resistance and steadfastness of the Iranian captives against the enemy's attempts to break their will. The book "Prisoners of Criscon" is introduced as a resource for understanding these captivity experiences. This account serves not only as a record of personal suffering but also as a symbol of the freedom and resilience of the combatants against oppression.
