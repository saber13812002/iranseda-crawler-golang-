Coinciding with Eid al-Adha, Muslims in various countries including Turkey, Egypt, and Pakistan celebrated the holiday by performing prayers and rituals. Over 1.6 million pilgrims in Mecca officially became Hajjis by completing the stoning of the devil and performing sacrifices. In Gaza, Palestinians held Eid prayers in ruined mosques despite destruction and Israeli restrictions, viewing their martyrs as symbols of divine sacrifice. The Alawi Mobile Shrine initiated preparations for the annual Eid al-Ghadir ceremony by receiving its flag from Karbala. The Iraqi embassy in Damascus issued a statement strongly condemning the armed attack on Ayatollah Sistani's office and the mistreatment of its staff. Muslims in Catalonia, Spain, are demanding the continuation of halal animal slaughter rights for the holiday, citing the need for certified facilities. In Spain, the Andalus Manuscript Library, which houses valuable historical texts, has faced years of neglect and restricted access. In India, unprecedented restrictions on animal slaughter in Muslim-populated states have significantly impacted the festive atmosphere.
