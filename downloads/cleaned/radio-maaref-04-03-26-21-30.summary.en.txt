The text provides a Quranic exegesis focusing on the concept of prophets' knowledge of the unseen, arguing that while God may hide this knowledge from sinners to preserve their dignity, it does not negate the prophets' actual awareness. It clarifies that narrations like the Dua Kumayl, which suggest God prevents angels from knowing certain secrets, apply specifically to angels and do not contradict explicit Quranic verses stating that prophets witness the deeds of their communities. The discussion also highlights the global miracle of Jesus's birth without a father, emphasizing that his miracles were the result of divine support through the Holy Spirit rather than mere answered prayers. Furthermore, the text distinguishes between the act of speaking and the content of speech, noting that while speaking as an infant was a miracle, the prophetic content of his words in adulthood required continuous divine inspiration. This analysis reinforces the theological stance that the infallible leaders possess comprehensive knowledge of human actions, a principle supported by both Quranic verses and established traditions.
