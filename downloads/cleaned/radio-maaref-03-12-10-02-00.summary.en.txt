The program begins by analyzing the "Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya," highlighting its profound answers to fundamental questions in anthropology, sociology, philosophy, and economics.
The central focus is on the 38th supplication (Du'a Raji'), where Imam Sajjad (AS) seeks forgiveness for neglecting the rights of the oppressed, failing to be grateful, and lacking the capacity for forgiveness and self-sacrifice.
Special emphasis is placed on the concepts of "protecting honor" and "self-sacrifice," arguing that justice and human rights begin with safeguarding human dignity rather than abstract slogans.
The speaker asserts that human rights derive their sanctity from "God's rights" (Haqq Allah), rejecting the false dichotomy between God and humanity.
The discussion then shifts to a jurisprudential analysis of the Quranic verse regarding "corruption on earth twice" in Surah Al-Isra, critically examining the chains of narration.
The interpretation identifies the two stages of corruption as the distortion of Torah laws and the killing of prophets, including Zechariah and the attempted assassination of Jesus (AS).
The divine punishment for this corruption is described as the subjugation of the oppressors by even more tyrannical forces, leading to insecurity within their own homes.
The conclusion emphasizes that oppression and arrogance lead to societal destruction, and the only path to salvation is a return to ethical principles and justice.
