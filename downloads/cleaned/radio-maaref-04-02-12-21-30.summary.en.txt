Following the host's farewell, the program shifts to a specialized discussion on the status of Jews during the era of the Mahdi's reappearance and the verification of related narrations. The core of the debate focuses on assessing the credibility of "Abayah," the narrator of a hadith stating that Jews and Christians will be expelled from cities during the Mahdi's rule. Citing the views of Shia scholars such as Sheikh Tusi and Sheikh Shoushtari, the testimony of Barqi regarding Abayah's status as a close companion of Imam Ali serves to validate the narration. The discussion then transitions to the figure of "Uthman ibn Isa," who, despite accusations of doctrinal deviation and embezzling funds from Imam Kazim, had his narrations accepted due to his repentance and the endorsement of other scholars. The program also highlights differing opinions on whether doctrinal beliefs affect a narrator's reliability, noting that some Shia scholars accept narrations from truthful individuals even if they have theological issues. Finally, the segment concludes by referencing a narration from Imam Ali regarding his unique virtues and his paramount role in preserving the religion and Sharia compared to the Prophet's companions.
