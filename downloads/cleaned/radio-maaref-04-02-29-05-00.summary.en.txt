The first section analyzes Quranic verses that address the claim of God's inability to resurrect humans after decay, attributing this doubt to a forgetfulness of the initial creation.
In response, the Quran affirms the possibility of resurrection by emphasizing God's absolute attributes of knowledge and power.
Verses highlighting the creation of the heavens and earth, as well as the transformation of green wood into fire, illustrate God's infinite power.
The concept of "Be" (Kun) is interpreted as the key to existence and absolute sovereignty, indicating that the being of all things is directly under God's control.
The second section discusses the jurisprudential issue of "Irraq and Muhdum Alayh," which concerns determining inheritance shares when the exact time of death of multiple individuals is unknown.
The primary condition for inheritance transfer is that the heir must be alive at the moment of the deceased's death, which is difficult to verify in cases of simultaneous death.
Principled arguments, such as the principle of the continuity of life, are insufficient to resolve this issue due to mutual conflict and the failure to prove the necessary sequence.
Therefore, determining the exact inheritance shares in these specific cases requires a more detailed examination of authentic narrations and jurisprudential texts.
