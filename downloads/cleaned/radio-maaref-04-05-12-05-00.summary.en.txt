This lesson examines sins from a jurisprudential and Quranic perspective, emphasizing the distinction between major and minor sins. The interpretation of terms like "khati'a" and "ithm" reveals that sins are inherently harmful and dynamic, leaving lasting ripples in society. According to a verse in Surah An-Nisa, abstaining from major sins ensures that minor sins are covered and forgiven. However, backbiting, as a major sin, erases one's good deeds and transfers the sinner's bad actions to the victim's record. The Quran compares backbiting to eating one's dead brother's flesh, symbolizing the destruction of character and spiritual exploitation. Envy is also identified as a primary root of backbiting, consuming faith like fire consumes wood and potentially leading to greater crimes. In the second part, the compilation of the Quran during the Prophet's time is affirmed, and the claim of its delay to the Caliphs is rejected based on common sense. The lesson also highlights the science of letters in the Quran, demonstrating that its linguistic and semantic structure possesses a precise and unalterable harmony.
