The main discussion focuses on interpreting "humility" (Tawadu) as the greatest act of worship and the pinnacle of piety.
Imam Sadiq (AS) defined humility as sitting in a lower position than one's actual status for the sake of God, rather than seeking the highest seat out of arrogance.
The degrees of humility were explored, including self-awareness, treating others as one wishes to be treated, and responding to evil with good.
A distinction was made between "forgiveness" and "forgetting," noting that while forgetting trauma is not within human control, restraining anger and avoiding revenge are virtuous acts.
The highest form of humility belongs to those who maintain modesty despite possessing power and high status, thereby refraining from seeking retribution.
Ultimately, humility was presented as the hallmark of the best servants and a sign of God's love for righteous individuals.
