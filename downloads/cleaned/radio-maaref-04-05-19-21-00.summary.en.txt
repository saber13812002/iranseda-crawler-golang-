In the radio program "Ma'aref," Hojjat al-Islam Vahidi emphasized the importance of serving people and meeting their needs, citing the conduct of Imams and great scholars. He highlighted that anonymous service and preserving the dignity of the needy are central to Islamic ethics, as demonstrated by figures like Imam Reza, Imam Sajjad, and Ayatollah Marashi Najafi. The program then narrated the story of Mohammad Taghi, who overcame financial difficulties and passport delays through faith and spiritual support to join the Arbaeen pilgrimage. This narrative illustrates how belief in the Wilayah and patience in the face of hardship can remove obstacles. During his journey, Mohammad Taghi experienced a deep sense of belonging and love for Karbala by observing the pilgrims and service camps. He visited the shrine of Hazrat Masoumeh and received the good news that his passport was ready, allowing him to reach the Mehran border. The story serves as a blend of ethical and spiritual lessons, emphasizing the role of faith in overcoming life's challenges.
