The radio program begins by citing Quranic verses and narrations to emphasize that divine prophets were sent to various nations and regions, not exclusively to the Arabian Peninsula.
It then explores the historical and jurisprudential school of Akhbarism, founded by Muhammad Amin Astarabadi in the 10th and 11th centuries of the Hijri calendar.
Key differences between Akhbaris and Usulis include the former's rejection of the Quran's apparent meanings as proof, their acceptance of narrations without scrutiny, and their denial of reason's role in legal deduction.
The Usuli school eventually prevailed after Sheikh Morteza Ansari systematized the principles of jurisprudence, marginalizing the Akhbari movement.
The second part of the program analyzes a hadith from Imam Sadiq (AS) regarding the balance between fear and hope in God.
The hadith states that one who fears God is granted a dignity that makes others respect them, whereas one who does not fear God will fear everything.
The core message is to align life's calculations with God's satisfaction to avoid hidden polytheism and excessive reliance on public opinion.
The program concludes with recommendations to study the Usul al-Kafi and Tadhkirat al-A'yan, ending with prayers for the Ahl al-Bayt (AS).
