In this program, Hojjat al-Islam Rafiei emphasizes the importance of "moderation" (modarrah) based on the Prophetic tradition, citing it as one of God's primary commands to the Prophet of Islam. He defines moderation not as submission to falsehood, but as a rational strategy to preserve the health of both religion and worldly life while fostering social peace. Notably, the principles adopted by UNESCO in 1995 as the "Principles of Tolerance" were already present in Islamic narrations centuries earlier. The speaker examined four types of moderation: first, in religious practice, aiming to attract youth through gentleness and avoiding excessive strictness or obsessive behavior. Second, in the family, highlighting the need for good manners, generosity, and men's sensitivity to their wives' modesty to prevent unwanted attention. Third, with children, advocating against comparison and blame, and showing attention to their feelings, as the Prophet did when he shortened his prayer due to a crying child. Fourth, with wrongdoers, promoting forgiveness, education, and addressing the root causes of sin rather than using harsh punishment. Finally, he called upon cultural and social leaders to promote a culture of moderation to create a peaceful and dynamic society.
