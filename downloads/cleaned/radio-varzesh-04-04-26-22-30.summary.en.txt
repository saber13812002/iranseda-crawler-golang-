Former national swimmer Mohammad Bidarian discusses his training experiences in Croatia and the critical differences between practice and competition. He recounts the painful experience of being eliminated from the Beijing Olympic trials due to an unfavorable draw against an Israeli competitor, which led him to attend those Games as a spectator. Relying on FINA's new qualification rules and his top score at the Shanghai World Championships, he secured a spot in the 2012 London Olympics. Despite suffering a torn knee ligament during a football match, he competed in London with the injury and recorded a personal best time. This injury forced him to retire from competitive swimming at a young age, prompting a career shift towards coaching and sports management. Bidarian emphasizes the importance of trusting the younger generation and utilizing the experience of veteran athletes in administrative structures. He argues that retired athletes should be retained in sports administration to drive organizational transformation. His greatest wish is to see the Iranian flag raised in international arenas and to bring joy to the people through sports.
