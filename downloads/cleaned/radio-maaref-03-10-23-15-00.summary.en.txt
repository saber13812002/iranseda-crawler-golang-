This radio program explored the challenges and opportunities of "gender and ijtihad" from a Shia jurisprudential perspective. Dr. Milani emphasized that Shia ijtihad is "la bishrat" (unconditional) and should not be viewed through a project-based, utilitarian lens aimed at advancing women's rights according to contemporary tastes, as this contradicts the principles of deduction. He argued that a jurist must set aside personal and gender biases, similar to Allama Hilli's approach, to reach the truth of the religious text rather than serving a specific gender's interests. However, he identified the primary value of female ijtihad in creating an "other" to the male understanding, enabling mutual critique and comparison between the two interpretive approaches. Dr. Dehkhaneh interpreted the Supreme Leader's statements on following women as applicable to specific topics where women possess greater expertise and experience, such as sexual issues or gender reassignment, provided the jurist meets the condition of expertise. He stressed that being female alone is insufficient for taqlid (following) and that the impact of gender on deduction must be scientifically proven in academic forums. Finally, the speakers recommended focusing on women's active research and presence in jurisprudential circles to enrich Islamic law and uncover the truth, rather than solely focusing on the act of following female jurists.
