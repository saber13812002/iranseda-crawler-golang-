Veteran football referee Gholamreza Jafari recounts his career progression from a first-class referee to a national official in 2001, highlighting his logical and non-punitive refereeing style. He admits to making errors, such as a controversial penalty call in a match between Railways and Foolad Khuzestan, but insists he never whistled out of fear. A memorable moment in his career was refereeing the decisive 2005 league final between Sepahan and Saipa, which resulted in Sepahan's championship and widespread celebration in Isfahan. Jafari addresses the issue of corruption in refereeing, arguing that establishing a formal "Refereeing System" and an independent "Referees Organization" is essential to ensure integrity and oversight. Drawing on his 30 years of teaching, 25 years of refereeing, and 18 years of executive management within the Football Federation, he positions himself as a qualified candidate for the Federation presidency. He believes that combining educational expertise, field experience, and administrative skills is the key to resolving Iran's football crises. Jafari states he is prepared to run for the presidency if his main competitor does not stand, aiming to implement a strategic roadmap for the sport.
