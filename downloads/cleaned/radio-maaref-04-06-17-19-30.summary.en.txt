In this program, Professor Tabrizi highlights the contradictions in US policy during the events of September 1978 using declassified spy camp documents.
He cites reports from the US Embassy and the Human Rights Defense Committee to illustrate the scale of killings and disappearances.
Tabrizi argues that despite Carter's rhetoric on human rights, Washington not only failed to support the Shah but actively aided him with intelligence equipment and military advice.
By comparing Carter's official statements with the actions of US ambassadors, he demonstrates that human rights were merely a tool to cover up atrocities.
In the second segment, Hujjat al-Islam Fala discusses the vice of slander, defining it as the sum of lying and backbiting.
He describes slander as a character assassination that violates divine traditions and moral virtues.
The speaker emphasizes that slander is an act of the ego-driven soul that destroys social trust and creates darkness in human relations.
Finally, abandoning this vice and pursuing moral virtues is deemed essential for preserving beliefs and the social order.
