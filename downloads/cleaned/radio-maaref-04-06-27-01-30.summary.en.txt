The program begins by highlighting the pivotal role of Iranian merchants in spreading Islam across India, Indonesia, and China. Historical evidence suggests that Iranians, while maintaining their religious identity, established Islam in these regions through trade and intermarriage. In Indonesia, the influence of Islam was so profound that it constitutes 90% of the population, with Shia traditions like Muharram also introduced by Iranians. Furthermore, the use of the title "Akhund" for clergy in China indicates the significant impact of Persian culture and language on the region's religious structure. The speaker emphasizes that the Dari Persian language, particularly in classical literature, has been a major servant to Islam and carries a distinct Islamic character. Subsequently, a radio dramatization of the book "Eastern Ocean" is aired, narrating the journey of a man named Amir in search of the water of life. During his encounter with Haj Karim, a cobbler, the latter argues for the Imamate and the concept of Wilayat by recounting how Imam Reza (AS) knew the intentions of a would-be assassin. Upon hearing these arguments, Amir expresses his readiness to accept the Imam's authority.
