This text explores the concept of Tawhid from a Quranic and narrational perspective, arguing that knowing God is a result of spiritual growth rather than ignorance or fear.
Prophets and Imams, described as the wisest and bravest humans, elevate humanity from material needs to spiritual insight and divine vision.
In contrast, concepts like freedom or contentment created by revolutionaries or rulers are tools used to manipulate public opinion and suppress the oppressed.
The fundamental difference between "Wahid" (one) and "Ahad" (absolute one) is explained, with "Ahad" signifying God's unique, partnerless oneness.
God will appear as the "Wahid al-Qahhar" (One and Subduer) on the Day of Judgment, demonstrating absolute power and sovereignty.
Quranic verses refute the worship of idols by proving their inability to benefit or harm even themselves, thus establishing the necessity of relying solely on God.
Finally, the absolute Caliph of God is introduced as the manifestation of divine grace and the medium for human connection with the Divine.
