In this radio program, Professor Mohammad Hadi Fala analyzed the vice of "stinginess" using Quranic and hadith sources, identifying it as a major barrier to spiritual and human growth. Citing the hadith "Whoever walks a path seeking knowledge," he emphasized that seekers of monotheistic knowledge receive blessings from angels and even sea creatures, noting that scholars are the true heirs of the prophets. A key point was that to benefit from this spiritual inheritance, an individual must first struggle against and detach from their ego and worldly attachments. Professor Fala warned that the illusion of sophistication gained from academic terminology should not lead to arrogance or stinginess in sharing knowledge with others. The second part of the program featured Dr. Bidar, who explained the annual slogan "Investment for Production," distinguishing it from merely saving for the future by emphasizing active economic contribution. A field report from Urmia followed, highlighting a support fund for rural women where 2,000 women are actively engaged in various production activities, from handicrafts to agriculture. The report demonstrated that through consumption management and contentment, even lower-income groups can participate effectively in the production cycle.
