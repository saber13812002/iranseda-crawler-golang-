The threat to assassinate the leader of the Islamic Republic of Iran by the United States constitutes a blatant violation of the right to self-determination and the principle of political independence. From an international law perspective, official state leaders enjoy immunity, and threats of assassination contradict international conventions and the UN Charter. Simultaneously, from a jurisprudential and Quranic viewpoint, insulting religious leaders and inciting war between nations is considered following the path of Satan and is contrary to Islamic teachings. Iran, with its history of religious diversity, has always been a model of religious tolerance, making such threats incompatible with its cultural and religious heritage. The US attack on Iran's nuclear facilities is legally unjustified, especially given Iran's unilateral declaration against nuclear weapons and the IAEA's directive to wait until the end of summer. The use or threat of nuclear weapons is inconsistent with both humanitarian law and Islamic jurisprudence, which prohibit mass killing and environmental damage. Consequently, the recent actions of the US and Israel are deemed completely invalid and illegal from both legal and religious standpoints.
