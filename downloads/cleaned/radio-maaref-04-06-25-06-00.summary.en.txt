This program examines historical and theological doubts regarding the nature of divine revelation and the Prophet of Islam. Some polytheists argued that if revelation were true, it should descend as a tangible book or the messenger should be an angel to possess greater authority. The Quran addresses these doubts by distinguishing between initial and requested miracles, stating that even if God fulfilled their demands, their internal disbelief and arrogance would have prevented them from believing. It is explained that if an angel were sent as a prophet, they would inevitably have to appear in human form, which would simply recreate the same initial doubts. Scholars like Sheikh Tusi argue that God exercises mercy by performing whatever is necessary to stop the people's disbelief, provided that such an act is possible. Finally, the verse "We clothe them with what they clothe themselves" signifies that God returns to humans the very errors and misguidance they have imposed upon themselves and others through their own free will.
