This radio program explained the rulings for exiting the state of Ihram and becoming a Hajj pilgrim after the stoning of the Jamarat and the sacrifice.
Men on their first Hajj must shave their heads, while those on subsequent trips may simply trim their hair and nails.
Women exit Ihram by trimming a few centimeters of hair and cutting their nails, which lifts most Ihram prohibitions.
However, using perfume and marrying a new spouse remain forbidden until the completion of the Mecca rituals.
Most Marja' consider the sacrifice and head shaving valid even if performed on the night of the 11th of Dhu al-Hijjah, though some advise caution.
The program also addressed other jurisprudential questions, noting that travel under 22.5 kilometers does not invalidate prayer or fasting.
Paying Khums based on an estimate is permissible only if one is certain the amount is not less than the actual due.
Finally, receiving a fee for loan intermediation is allowed if legal, and one person's invalid prayer does not invalidate the prayers of others in the congregation.
