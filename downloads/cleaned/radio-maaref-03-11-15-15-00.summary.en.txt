In this radio program, two legal and jurisprudential experts discussed the mechanism of "guardianship in marriage" and its role in protecting children's rights after the Islamic Revolution. They explained that the legislator, while adhering to jurisprudential principles, shifted the legal stance from the permissibility of child marriage to a principle of prohibition, with exceptions only upon judicial verification of the child's best interest. This new approach was designed to prevent errors in assessing a child's welfare and to guard against the abuse of guardianship powers, particularly in cases of poverty or financial hardship. According to Article 1041 of the Civil Code, the marriage of girls under 13 and boys under 15 is void without the approval of a competent court, aiming to prevent physical, psychological, and social harm. The experts emphasized that courts rarely grant such permissions, effectively blocking this path by considering social, ethical, and financial interests. These legal developments have strengthened the security and rights of women and children within the family compared to the pre-revolution era, thereby alleviating international concerns regarding child marriage. Ultimately, these legal reforms were evaluated as a positive step toward ensuring psychological and financial security for family members and improving social culture.
