The radio broadcast reported on the ongoing Israeli attacks in Gaza, highlighting the deaths of dozens of civilians and journalists, as well as widespread global protests in support of the Palestinian people. The news segment also covered violent clashes between Druze groups and pro-Jolani forces in Syria's Suweida province, noting the interim government's efforts to restore order. The start of the Arbaeen pilgrimage from southern Iraq, despite extreme heat, was also featured in the news updates. The program then transitioned to a cultural segment titled "Footprints of Stories," which examined the European origins of the "Boy Who Cried Wolf" tale in Persian literature. Citing historical documents and memoirs, the segment argued that this story is an import and that real-life Iranian counterparts, such as the story of Kashf al-Soltaneh and the introduction of tea cultivation, differ significantly from the fictional narrative.
