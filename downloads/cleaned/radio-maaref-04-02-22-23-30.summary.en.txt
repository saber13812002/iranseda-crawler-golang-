In this radio program, Dr. Mirhamid Rezaei Ghaedi discusses the roots of Iranian social cinema in the 1980s and its role in offering diverse options to the audience. The guest emphasizes that restricting cinema to a single stream deprives viewers of their choice and leads to the rejection of cinema by the public. Using a medical analogy, he argues that social cinema should not be viewed merely as a vaccine or cure for society, but should encompass the varied perspectives of different filmmakers. Rezaei Ghaedi insists on accepting the inherent differences in how filmmakers view society, stating that eliminating any specific style is an injustice to cinema. He also highlights the role of cinema in raising public awareness about modern challenges, such as cybercrimes, and believes it can make society more conscious of hidden social issues. In another segment, the animation "Dolphin Boy 2" is introduced as a successful example of Iranian animation with a strong narrative and international acclaim. The program concludes with a broadcast of Imam Khomeini's words on the necessity of combining knowledge with piety and the continuous need for human education.
