The first segment of the program detailed the Hajj rituals, specifying that agents must perform the stoning of the Aqaba pillar on the tenth of Dhu al-Hijjah and subsequently carry out the sacrifice in mechanized facilities while adhering to strict Sharia conditions for the animal. Following these acts, the sixth obligation, which is shaving or shortening the hair, is performed. The program then addressed various jurisprudential inquiries, including the permissibility of reciting healing prayers in different languages during the Ghunut prayer and the lack of necessity to completely remove grease from feet before Wudu. Additionally, rulings were provided regarding the timely payment of Khums, the jurisprudential distinction between Kaffarah and Fidyah, and the obligation to donate to the poor on behalf of deceased debtors whose heirs are unknown. In the second segment, Hujjat al-Islam Najafi emphasized the spiritual importance of listening to sermons, citing examples of prominent Maraji who never considered themselves exempt from the need for moral advice. The broadcast concluded by introducing Professor Shakarin and initiating a discussion on the critique of new theology from the perspective of quantum physics.
