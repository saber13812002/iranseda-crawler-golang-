Allameh Tabatabai critiques Kantian epistemology and Marxist materialism by arguing that knowledge is a combination of external influences and internal mental structures, which negates a perfect correspondence between awareness and material reality. By rigorously distinguishing between empirical and philosophical claims, he demonstrates that theories like Darwinism lack definitive logical proofs and comprehensive empirical evidence, relying instead on conjectures and fragmented data. Tabatabai emphasizes that providing natural explanations for phenomena does not necessarily refute supernatural or theological interpretations, a misconception largely propagated by nineteenth-century historians. He asserts that one can simultaneously adhere to empirical scientific explanations and philosophical-theological interpretations of existence without contradiction. This approach dismantles the false dichotomy between science and religion, showing that strict materialism is not an inevitable consequence of scientific discoveries. Finally, the text highlights the importance of preserving national identity and supporting the oppressed, urging small but effective actions to foster societal well-being and uphold human values.
