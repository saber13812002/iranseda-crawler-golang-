The text examines the dual nature of humans, arguing that their natural needs make them inherently dependent on leadership. It redefines the concept of Wilayat from the limited legal meaning of guardianship for the incapacitated to a broader sense of governance and political authority. By citing Quranic verses and hadiths, the speaker asserts that Wilayat is essential for managing the foundations of Islam, such as Zakat, Hajj, and the enforcement of divine laws. The speaker refutes the claim that the Wilayat of the Jurist is incompatible with the people's vote, clarifying that public acceptance is a confirmation of divine appointment rather than a delegation of power. Consequently, the Wilayat of the Jurist as a societal leader is fully compatible with the people's acceptance and the principles of the Islamic Republic. This form of leadership ensures the proper implementation of religious laws and the protection of public interests. The argument concludes that this system is rooted in divine revelation and requires the people's wise acceptance to function effectively.
