The narrative highlights Imam Sajjad's pivotal role in Damascus, where his public prayer and recitation of the tragedy of Karbala shook Yazid and secured the honorable return of the Ahl al-Bayt to Medina. By establishing mourning rituals and openly weeping for his father, Imam Sajjad kept the memory of Karbala alive and became the founder of these religious traditions. The second part follows the story of Mohammad Taghi, a young teacher from Zanjan, who faced financial and administrative obstacles in his journey to Karbala. Relying on divine trust and receiving unexpected help from those around him, he successfully obtained his passport. After arriving in Qom and visiting the shrine of Hazrat Masoum (SA), he set off toward the Mehran border with two companions. Along the way, seeing the caravans and pilgrims further strengthened his motivation for the Arbaeen walk, and he remained hopeful of reaching Karbala with his wife's prayers.
