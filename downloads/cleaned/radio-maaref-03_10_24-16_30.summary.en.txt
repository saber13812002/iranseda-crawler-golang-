In this radio program, experts emphasized the importance of logical, process-oriented interaction between parents and children, criticizing arbitrary parenting methods that lack explanation.
A key point highlighted is providing "unconditional attention" to the child; even during busy moments, parents should dedicate specific time to affection and attention without moral conditions.
To manage misbehavior, creating a "behavioral plan" with clearly defined rules and their logical consequences is recommended so the child understands the impact of breaking rules.
Physical punishment and yelling are ineffective and may actually encourage children to repeat misbehavior to gain attention; therefore, parents must control their anger.
Firmness in enforcing agreed-upon privileges is crucial, and parents should not disagree with each other or hesitate to implement consequences during critical moments.
In response to a question about a first-grader's stubbornness, it was explained that resistance is a natural part of a child's growth and identity discovery, not disrespect.
Parents were advised to use game-based approaches for tasks instead of force and prolonged arguments, and to help children manage emotions by teaching them cognitive skills to identify feelings.
Finally, aligning parental expectations with the child's age and capabilities was deemed essential for reducing stress and fostering peace within the family.
