In the first segment, a psychologist emphasized that parents should not use their children as tools for revenge against an ex-spouse, as this harms the child's mental health and fails to meet their emotional needs.
The discussion then shifted to managing sleep and study habits during exam periods, highlighting challenges such as teenagers' inability to plan effectively and the distraction caused by family gatherings.
The expert advised parents to avoid hosting parties during exam weeks and to inform relatives about this restriction to ensure a quiet environment for studying.
To combat fatigue and lack of motivation, the Pomodoro technique was recommended, involving 25-minute focused study sessions followed by 5-minute breaks to enhance mental concentration.
It was also stressed that realistic estimation of the remaining workload is crucial to prevent burnout and avoid unproductive all-nighters.
The second part of the program focused on the life of Mohammad Bahmanbegi, the founder of Iran's nomadic schools, who started mobile schools in 1951 to educate the Qashqai and other nomadic tribes.
By overcoming geographical and cultural barriers, Bahmanbegi not only promoted literacy but also encouraged traditional arts, paving the way for the professional and social advancement of nomadic men and women.
Today, many graduates of these schools have achieved success in academic and professional roles, and Bahmanbegi's legacy is recognized as a model for educational justice in remote areas.
