This radio program explained the fifth obligatory ritual of Hajj, which is the sacrifice performed in Mecca and Mina.
The sacrificial animal must be healthy and meet specific religious criteria regarding its age, appearance, and physical condition.
Today, pilgrims typically perform the sacrifice through their representatives and travel groups rather than attending the slaughterhouse in person.
Once the sacrifice is completed, the sixth obligatory ritual of Hajj, which is shaving or trimming the hair, begins.
In the Q&A section, it was confirmed that non-Arabic prayers are permissible in the Ghunut of prayer and that minor grease on feet does not invalidate Wudu.
Delayed payment of Khums is considered a religious debt, and paying it at current market value to the poor on behalf of deceased creditors is allowed.
The distinction between Fidyah and Kaffarah for missed fasts was discussed, noting that one Kaffarah of Takhir suffices for all delayed fasts.
Finally, the virtues of prayer and the etiquette of choosing friends were highlighted based on the narrations of the Ahl al-Bayt.
