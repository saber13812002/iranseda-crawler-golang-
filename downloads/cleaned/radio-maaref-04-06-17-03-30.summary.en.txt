In the fiqh segment, Professor Vahidpour explained that unconventional medical methods like hypnosis are religiously permissible only if their effectiveness is certain and they cause no harm to the patient. However, legally, doctors are not authorized to use such methods as they lack official regulatory approval. Other rulings clarified that multiple obligatory and recommended ghusls can be performed with a single intention, and missing the bowing position (ruku) invalidates the prayer, requiring it to be repeated. Sunlight can purify impure land only after the initial impurity has been removed with water, and creating a veil for a bride is considered haram in any form. Regarding wudu and ghusl, barriers on the skin or hair are prohibited, but subcutaneous injections like tattoos are not considered obstacles. Minor impurities on small garments like socks do not invalidate prayer, and washing only the affected area is sufficient for purification. In the ethical section, Professor Razani emphasized the importance of gratitude and avoiding arrogance regarding divine blessings, citing the 20th supplication of Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya. He noted that acknowledging all capabilities as gifts from God fosters humility, reliance on the Divine, and inner peace.
