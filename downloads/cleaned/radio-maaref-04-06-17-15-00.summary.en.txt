In this radio program, legal experts emphasized the prohibition of killing non-combatants, such as women, children, and the elderly, based on Quranic verses and religious narrations. Even if these individuals are present on the battlefield, Islam mandates avoiding their killing whenever possible. The principle of "no bearing another's burden" serves as the primary basis for this ruling, ensuring that innocent civilians are not punished for the actions of others. However, direct participation in war, such as military training or weapons production, disqualifies individuals or locations from non-combatant status, making them legitimate targets for retaliation. Regarding prisoners of war, torture and execution are strictly forbidden, even if the enemy has mistreated Muslim captives. Nevertheless, in critical situations where the Islamic government's survival is threatened, the use of deterrent methods to prevent further aggression is permitted under the rule of necessity. Thus, the boundary between legitimate retaliation and unlawful harm to civilians is determined by the actual level of participation in the war effort.
