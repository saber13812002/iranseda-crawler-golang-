This segment of the program continues the philosophical and theological discussion on the relationship between infallibility (Ismat) and free will (Ikhtiyar) in the Infallibles.
The key distinction made is between "conferred infallibility" (Ismat I'tari), granted by God from the beginning, and "acquired infallibility" (Ismat Iqtisabi), which is attained after a period of time.
The speaker argues that if infallibility is present from the start, it would negate free will, contradicting the Islamic principle that divine commandments (Taklif) require a free agent.
Therefore, asserting that the Infallibles possessed infallibility from the moment of creation leads to the logical fallacy of stripping them of agency and rendering moral testing meaningless.
Conversely, the theory of acquired infallibility is more consistent with the rule that "necessity arising from choice does not negate choice," as the individual was initially free and chose the path leading to infallibility.
The speaker emphasizes that even in the case of acquired infallibility, God remains the ultimate source, while the human role is limited to causality and preparing the capacity for this divine gift.
The conclusion is that to preserve both infallibility and free will, one must reject the notion of absolute, initial infallibility and accept a model where the individual's initial free will plays a significant role.
