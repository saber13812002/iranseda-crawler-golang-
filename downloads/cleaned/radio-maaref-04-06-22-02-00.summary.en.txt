The radio program begins by emphasizing the importance of good morals and helping the poor, citing a narration from Imam Sadiq (AS).
Three real-life stories from Ayatollah Salehi Mazandarani, Ayatollah Haeri, and Hojjatolislam Yazdi illustrate practical examples of discreet charity and supporting the needy during difficult historical times.
The second part of the program focuses on Quranic exegesis, analyzing the rhetorical and semantic nuances of the words "Layl" (night) and "Nahar" (day).
The commentator refutes the polytheists' claim that God has children, explaining that as the Absolute Self-Sufficient, God needs no helper or partner.
A linguistic analysis distinguishes between "Wald" as a biological child and "Ittiyad al-Wald" as adoption, using verses from Surah Yusuf and Ghafir to clarify these concepts.
The concept of "Subhan" is interpreted as rapid movement in worship and an expression of God's transcendence from the attributes of His servants.
It is described as the first step in the path of worship, affirming God's absolute oneness.
The final conclusion of the discussion emphasizes the absolute uniqueness of God and the denial of any partner or helper in the management of the universe.
