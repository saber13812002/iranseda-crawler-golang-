In this radio program, Dr. Mohammadi and Dr. Sadeghi analyzed the jurisprudential and legal criteria for the crime of Moharebeh. They emphasized that merely drawing a real weapon, even without ammunition or firing, constitutes Moharebeh if it has a public dimension and instills fear in the public. Conversely, using a fake or dummy weapon does not qualify as Moharebeh according to the majority of jurists and the apparent law due to the lack of real danger. A crucial point is that frightening even a single individual is sufficient, as the term "people" is a collective noun and not limited to a specific number. Furthermore, the fear must stem directly from the perpetrator's action, not from incidental factors like the broadcast of images by others. The legislator explicitly identifies armed bandits and smugglers as instances of Moharebeh in Article 281 of the Islamic Penal Code. Finally, a personal motive in a private dispute lacking a public dimension prevents the crime of Moharebeh from being established.
