Pakistan and Afghanistan agreed to a 48-hour ceasefire in border regions following Pakistani airstrikes on Kabul that killed the leader of the Pakistani Taliban. In the Gaza Strip, Israeli forces violated the truce through continued shelling and arrests, while returning residents faced widespread destruction and bodies trapped under rubble. Analysts describe the Sharm el-Sheikh agreement as a three-phase process, with the first phase involving the exchange of 20 Israeli hostages for approximately 2,000 Palestinian prisoners. The second phase, which includes further Israeli withdrawal and the formation of a technocratic government, faces significant challenges due to Western skepticism and political complexities. Discussions regarding the resistance's weapons and long-term reconstruction are deferred to the third phase, which is expected to encounter strong opposition. Elsewhere, the King of Jordan emphasized the necessity of an independent Palestinian state, while cultural and interfaith events were held in Ankara and India. Additionally, statistics indicate that the number of mosques in Spain has doubled over the past decade.
