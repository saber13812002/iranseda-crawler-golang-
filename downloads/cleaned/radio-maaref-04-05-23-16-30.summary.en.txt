Iran's governance is firmly rooted in the inseparable combination of Iranian identity, Islam, and Shiaism, creating a robust cultural barrier against foreign aggression.
During the twelve-day war, the unity of various ethnic, religious, and political groups demonstrated the nation's significant deterrent power against its enemies.
Recognizing their failure in military and economic fronts, adversaries have shifted their focus to cultural aggression aimed at dismantling the nation's social cohesion from within.
The emotional and ideological bond between the people and the system of Velayat-e Faqih serves as a critical pillar of political stability and resistance.
Opposition groups abroad, including monarchists, MEK members, and liberals, lack internal cohesion and fail to garner substantial public support.
These factions have historically been unable to play a significant role in domestic protests and are plagued by severe internal disagreements.
History indicates that the Iranian people, dissatisfied with the Pahlavi era's issues, possess a genuine will for a religious system and reject a return to the past.
Ultimately, due to strong national identity, hard military and scientific power, and public disapproval of the opposition, Iran will remain resilient against enemy plots.
