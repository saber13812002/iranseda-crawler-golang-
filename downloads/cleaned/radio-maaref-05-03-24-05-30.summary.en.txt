The radio program begins with a mystical interpretation of Gabriel's black-and-white horse, symbolizing the inevitable mix of pleasure and pain in worldly life. The Prophet Muhammad is highlighted for rejecting the offer of worldly treasures, emphasizing instead the spiritual balance of gratitude during abundance and reliance on God during scarcity. The main focus shifts to the suffering of Imam Ali, using Nahj al-Balagha to document the community's ingratitude and mistreatment of him. Imam Ali expressed his unwavering dedication to serving the people and freeing them from oppression, while lamenting their lack of kindness and support. The program also recounts a historical dialogue where the first Caliph acknowledged Ali's superior qualifications for leadership, despite having bypassed him. Finally, the sixty-second letter of Nahj al-Balagha is presented as a "Letter of Sorrow," revealing Imam Ali's profound shock and grief at being sidelined from the caliphate immediately after the Prophet's passing.
