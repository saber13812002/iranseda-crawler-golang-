This radio program discussed Islamic rulings regarding the use of prohibited or impure substances on the skin, as well as issues related to prayer and social ethics. It was established that using haram but pure substances, such as snake oil, on the skin is permissible, whereas using najis substances like the carcass of fish with circulating blood, while not haram, renders the body impure and requires purification. Regarding gelatin, it is acceptable if derived from an Islamic-slaughtered cow, but if there is doubt about it being pork-derived, topical use is permitted, though it makes the body impure. Listeners of backbiting or haram music in public settings are not considered sinful, provided they avoid listening and, if possible, perform the duty of enjoining good and forbidding evil. Recording the voice or image of individuals without their consent is impermissible, except in completely public spaces, as individuals have ownership rights over their voice and image. In congregational prayer, believers should recite the takbir immediately after the imam to ensure they receive the full reward of the prayer. Women must cover their entire body during prayer, but seeing their own reflection under a veil is not an obstacle. Finally, for fatherless girls, if they possess the capacity for judgment, they may make their own decisions regarding marriage.
