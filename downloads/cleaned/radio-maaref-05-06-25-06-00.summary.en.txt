Ayatollah Javadi Amoli emphasizes in his Quranic exegesis that the Quran's narratives are the "best of stories" because they are free from falsehood and exaggeration, being accompanied by divine wisdom and instruction. He explains that prophets are sent from among their own people to make the acceptance of guidance possible, and on the Day of Judgment, the oppressed who knowingly rejected the truth will face double punishment. The sermon clarifies that the command to obey the Messenger is a directive to follow the Prophet's inherent and governmental orders, rather than a separate, independent obligation. Regarding the afterlife, it is noted that Paradise is devoid of idle talk, with interactions based on peace and mutual respect. Finally, the verse stating that the arrogant disbelievers will not enter Paradise until a camel passes through the eye of a needle is interpreted as indicating the absolute impossibility of their salvation, although some scholars have argued it is a natural impossibility given God's unlimited power.
