In this radio program, the first guest explains the concept of "stinginess" as a vice that opposes the virtue of charity. He describes stinginess as a disease stemming from the ego-driven soul and an excessive attachment to the material world, rooted in disbelief and hypocrisy. Citing Quranic verses, he emphasizes that true ownership belongs to God, and refusing to give is a disregard for the divine command to be charitable. The speaker warns that the stingy will face the manifestation of their actions in the form of fiery chains around their necks on Judgment Day. In the second part, the second guest discusses the Islamic obligation of hijab and the society's role in enforcing it. He argues that hijab is a divine duty with social implications that extend beyond the individual. Therefore, guidance and education by families and the government are considered a religious obligation under the principle of enjoining good and forbidding evil. He stresses the importance of culturally explaining the rationale of hijab through media and educational institutions.
