Relations between Iran and Syria shifted from coldness to a deep strategic alliance following the Islamic Revolution, rooted in Syria's unwavering support for Iran during the eight-year war. Hafez al-Assad condemned Iraq's invasion and cut off Iraqi oil pipelines, incurring significant costs that established a foundation of mutual trust between the two nations. In subsequent decades, shared threats from the US and Israel, along with regional interests, strengthened this bond through defense pacts and military cooperation. When the Syrian crisis began in 2011, Iran entered the scene with a one-year delay to mediate between legitimate popular demands and the prevention of total state collapse driven by Western and Gulf-backed armed groups. Iran's presence was not solely aimed at preserving Bashar al-Assad, but rather at managing the internal crisis and preventing the country from becoming a threat to Iran's national security. By proposing reforms and training security forces, Iran sought to balance popular aspirations with the stability of the central government. Ultimately, these interactions demonstrate that Iran's policy in Syria was based on preserving strategic interests and regional security, rather than merely supporting a specific regime.
