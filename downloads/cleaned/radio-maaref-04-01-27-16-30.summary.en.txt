The radio program begins by explaining the concept of "Divine Mercy" in Paradise, describing it as the ultimate eternal blessing whose intensity varies according to an individual's spiritual capacity. It then addresses a theological question regarding whether Jinn have their own prophets, clarifying that they do not have legislative prophets from their own kind but must believe in human prophets, with some Jinn acting merely as propagators of these laws. The discussion continues with a response to the paradox of why God creates humans knowing their fate, emphasizing that free will is essential for human growth and that trials are necessary for spiritual development. The latter part of the program focuses on the Islamic tradition of "Silat ar-Rahim," or maintaining family ties, which is cited in the Quran and Hadith as a factor for extending life and increasing blessings. The guest expert highlights the importance of mutual respect and preventing moral deviation among relatives, drawing from the conduct of the Ahl al-Bayt. Listeners are also reminded that social sins such as backbiting and envy hinder the acceptance of prayers and spiritual connection with God. Finally, the program concludes by stressing the necessity of fulfilling both material and spiritual rights of relatives to preserve family bonds and ensure peace in daily life.
