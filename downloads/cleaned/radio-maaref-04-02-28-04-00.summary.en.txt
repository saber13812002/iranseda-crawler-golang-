The radio program began by clarifying the rules for stoning the pillars, noting that women and those with valid excuses may perform this ritual at night, while able-bodied men must do so during the day. The discussion then addressed the fasting obligations of pregnant and breastfeeding women, stating that if fasting harms the woman herself, she only pays a fidya of 750 grams of wheat per day, but if the harm is solely to the fetus, she must make up the fasts and pay a penalty for delay. It was also ruled that found items can be used after giving charity on behalf of the owner or donating to the poor. The speakers emphasized that negligence in prayer leads to losing intercession, and a fasting person invalidates their fast only by actually committing a prohibited act, not merely by intending to. Regarding puberty, religious duties become obligatory for boys upon reaching 15 lunar years or showing any physical sign of maturity. The program concluded with stories of asceticism from prominent clerics, urging listeners to detach from worldly possessions and focus on charity and the afterlife.
