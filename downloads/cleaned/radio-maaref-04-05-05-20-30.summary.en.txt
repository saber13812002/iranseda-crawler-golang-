During Reza Shah's reign, following the Allied occupation of Iran, secular and Westernization policies were strictly enforced by banning traditional and religious attire.
This era was marked by severe suppression of the press and the assassination of critical journalists, while cinema and universities were utilized to promote the government's ideology.
Despite the establishment of Tehran University, there was no significant scientific or technological output by the end of the Pahlavi rule.
In contrast, after the Islamic Revolution, the country's scientific growth surged, with global rankings indicating an eleven-fold increase compared to the world average.
The second part of the program features the memories of Davoud Ghanbarian, a freedom fighter captured during the Ramadan Operation.
He recounts the prisoners' creative efforts to maintain morale, such as secretly baking sweets and holding religious ceremonies in Iraqi camps.
The captives preserved their identity and faith through clandestine education and sports, defying the harsh conditions and Iraqi prohibitions.
These narratives highlight the cultural and spiritual resistance of the fighters against enemy pressure and the hardships of captivity.
