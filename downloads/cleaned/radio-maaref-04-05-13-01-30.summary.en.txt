The first segment of the program emphasizes, based on the life of Imam Sajjad (AS), that no religious duty such as upbringing, preaching, public speaking, mourning, or enjoining good is ever suspended.
Imam Sajjad demonstrated that even in critical conditions like the pandemic or siege, these duties must be fulfilled through creativity and adaptation rather than abandonment.
The second segment presents a dramatic retelling from the book "Let It Blow from the Pet," focusing on the life of Har ibn Yazid Rayahi, a commander in Har's army.
Har encounters a group of travelers from Kufa on the road to Karbala and, upon hearing the name of Imam Hussein (AS), experiences a profound internal transformation and doubt regarding his position.
Despite military orders, he refrains from arresting the travelers, struggling internally with the "storm of Hussein" that awakens him from his slumber of ignorance.
At the end of the segment, Har is left in deep anxiety after learning that Imam Hussein (AS) secretly visited Ibn al-Hurr to invite him to repentance, placing him on the verge of a final decision.
This narrative illustrates that even enemies can face an identity crisis and return to the truth when confronted with the spiritual magnitude of Imam Hussein (AS).
