Imam Baqir (AS) defined a true Shia as one who possesses humility, trustworthiness, remembrance of God, prayer, fasting, charity, kindness to parents and neighbors, honesty, and Quran recitation. He emphasized that guarding one's tongue from defaming others, both in real life and online, is a fundamental characteristic of a Shia. Two narratives regarding Imam Baqir's interaction with people were then presented; in the first, a man was recognized as a neighbor of the Commander of the Faithful in Paradise for expressing loyalty, adhering to religious laws, and awaiting the Mahdi. In the second narrative, the Imam considered controlling one's appetite and desires as the highest form of worship, stressing the importance of avoiding the forbidden. The second part of the program introduces the novel "Left Behind by the Son," which offers a dramatic retelling of the 1963 uprising. The story centers on Salari, a man who enters a security conflict to earn money and encounters shrouded protesters heading to Tehran to protest the arrest of Imam Khomeini. These scenes depict the security forces' violence and their confrontation with protesters on the Warmin road and at Uncle Jamshid's coffeehouse.
