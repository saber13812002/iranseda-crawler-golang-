The Ma'aref network provided live coverage of Iraq's preparations for the Arbaeen pilgrimage, announcing that official security services and camp setups would begin on the first day of Safar. Meanwhile, the police chief confirmed full readiness to ensure security and traffic control along entry routes, while the largest Iranian camp, comprising thousands of pilgrims, was reported to have crossed the Shalamcheh border into Iraq. Radio Arbaeen also launched its 24-hour broadcast to coincide with the start of Safar, replacing Radio Muharram, which concluded its operations with a focus on heroism and enemy awareness. Nationwide marches condemned the Zionist regime's crimes in Gaza and called for Egypt and Jordan to open their borders for humanitarian aid. International reports highlighted that Islam's offer of spiritual peace and life order is increasingly attractive to Western youth, while France's decision to recognize Palestine sparked significant global reactions. Additionally, a former Israeli Prime Minister warned that the state is becoming a pariah, and Syrian sources reported the discovery of dozens of bodies and the abduction of Alawite women by armed groups. The program concluded with the broadcast of listener-submitted audio segments from Radio Marf and an introduction to upcoming evening shows.
