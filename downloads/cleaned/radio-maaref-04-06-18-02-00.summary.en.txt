In the Parsian program, Hojjat al-Islam Vahidi emphasized the importance of self-respect and avoiding asking for small favors from others, citing prophetic traditions and the personal conduct of Imam Khomeini and Ayatollah Bahjat. He illustrated how these scholars maintained their independence by performing their own tasks, even when ill or elderly, to set a moral example. In the second segment, Professor Sahibi analyzed Imam Khomeini's views on Khwaja Nasir al-Din Tusi. The Imam regarded Tusi as a profound philosopher who preserved Islamic law and prevented the destruction of science by joining the Mongol court. Tusi successfully defended the Mashshai philosophical school against Fakhr al-Razi's critiques, thereby strengthening the status of philosophy and theology in religious seminaries. However, Imam Khomeini disagreed with Tusi's Illuminationist (Ishraqi) stance on God's detailed knowledge, rejecting the notion that divine knowledge operates in the physical realm.
