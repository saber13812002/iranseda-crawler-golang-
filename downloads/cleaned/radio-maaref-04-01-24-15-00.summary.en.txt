This radio program discusses whether hijab is a customary or a religious obligation. The experts argue, based on Quranic verses and hadiths, that hijab is a religious command where the divine lawgiver has directly defined its boundaries. One argument used by those claiming hijab is customary is the hadiths regarding Muslim slave women who were told not to wear hijab; however, jurisprudential analysis indicates that this command implies permissibility rather than obligation, as it serves to lift a previous requirement. Furthermore, the legal distinctions between slave women and free women in the Quran demonstrate the lawgiver's active role in defining subjects, which contradicts the idea that hijab is merely customary. The divine law has specified the limits of hijab, requiring the covering of the entire body except the face and hands, while leaving the specific style, color, and form of the clothing to social custom. Therefore, although the appearance of the clothing may vary, the essence and boundaries of hijab are determined by religious law and are not subject to change by custom.
