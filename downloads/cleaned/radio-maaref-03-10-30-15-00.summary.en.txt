This radio program examined the challenges women face in achieving the rank of Ijtihad and participating effectively in Islamic seminaries. The guests highlighted structural and cultural barriers, such as the dominance of traditional gender stereotypes among policymakers and the unequal distribution of educational resources between women's and men's seminaries. They also pointed out the lack of appropriate professional positions for female jurists in national structures and the subconscious biases regarding women's understanding as significant epistemological and social harms. To address these issues, the necessity of distinguishing between affirmative action and nepotism, as well as avoiding the complete isolation of women's seminaries at advanced academic levels, was emphasized. It was recommended that educational policies be designed intelligently, considering women's domestic responsibilities, to prevent a decline in academic quality. Finally, it was stressed that creating a space for cross-gender academic interaction at high levels and ensuring fair competition are key to the flourishing and validation of female jurists' capabilities.
