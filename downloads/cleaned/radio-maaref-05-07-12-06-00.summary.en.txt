Ayatollah Javadi Amoli interprets the concept of "ta'wil" in Quranic exegesis as the ultimate, tangible outcome of events, distinguishing it from "tafsir," which is a mental or conceptual interpretation. He argues that while tafsir deals with meanings, ta'wil refers to the external reality of an event's conclusion, which may manifest in this world or the hereafter. The scholar clarifies that knowing the ta'wil of an event does not make the ta'wil itself a mental concept; rather, it is an external existence that can be known through divine knowledge. This is further differentiated from dream interpretation, which is a psychological process of decoding symbols, whereas "ta'wil al-ahadith" involves predicting the actual future outcome of real-world occurrences. Quranic evidence from Surahs An-Nisa, Yunus, and Yusuf supports the view that ta'wil is an external reality that will be fully realized on the Day of Judgment. Prophet Joseph (AS) is cited as an example of someone who possessed the knowledge of ta'wil, allowing him to predict the outcomes of his fellow prisoners' dreams and the eventual fulfillment of his father's vision. Consequently, believers await the ta'wil of the Quran not as a new textual explanation, but as the concrete manifestation of divine promises in history and the afterlife.
