In this radio program, the hosts reference the poem "Muslims, be Muslim" to warn against being deceived by foreign superficiality before narrating the story "The Right of a Muslim." The story centers on a dispute between Zayd, his wife Hanieh, and their neighbor Samura, who repeatedly enters their property without permission. While Zayd advocates for patience and Islamic kindness, Hanieh views his silence as weakness and urges him to defend their rights firmly. After a heated debate, Zayd suggests taking the matter to the Prophet Muhammad for a fair judgment. The Prophet attempts to resolve the issue by offering to buy the tree or exchange it for ten others, but Samura remains stubborn and self-centered. Ultimately, the Prophet declares that God does not permit selfishness among Muslims and orders the companions to uproot the tree. This final ruling emphasizes that the rights of a Muslim include respecting others' boundaries and that violations must be addressed with decisive justice.
