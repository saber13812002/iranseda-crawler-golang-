This program explores the concept of justice from the perspective of Nahj al-Balagha and religious traditions across fourteen domains, including public conduct, family relations, and administration.
Key points emphasize the humility of officials, the necessity of equality in treating visitors, and avoiding discrimination in inheritance and parental affection.
It is stressed that administrative staff must act with fairness and without bias, respecting the religious and revolutionary symbols present in their workplaces.
In the second section, wisdom is presented as the key to the good of both this world and the hereafter, summarized in three pillars: asceticism, jurisprudence, and awareness of worldly flaws.
Asceticism does not mean abandoning the world entirely, but rather avoiding attachment to it in order to experience the true taste of faith.
The sweetness of divine love is only attainable for those who are free from worldly negligence and do not engage with anything other than God.
Finally, the importance of maintaining justice in speech, charity, and worship to achieve a balance between livelihood and the afterlife is highlighted.
