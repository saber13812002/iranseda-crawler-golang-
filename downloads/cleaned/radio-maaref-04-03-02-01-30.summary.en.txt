The radio program begins by refuting the claim that Islam is a religion of war, emphasizing instead that it is a faith of peace and mercy where violence is strictly limited to self-defense and protecting the oppressed.
Key theological points include the obligation to respect the rights of non-Muslims and the preference for inviting others to monotheism through wisdom and moral persuasion rather than force.
Jihad is redefined not as aggression, but as the "garment of piety" necessary to preserve social justice, ethics, and divine values against tyranny.
In the Q&A segment, the expert clarifies that remote pilgrimage (Ziyarat) dates back to the era of the Imams and does not require physical presence at the shrines.
The forced migration of Imam Reza (AS) to Khorasan was orchestrated by Harun al-Rashid to monitor the Imam's political influence, a threat the Imam foresaw by refusing to bring his family along.
The concept of "Dahul al-Ard" is explained as the emergence of land from water, marking the 25th of Dhu al-Qi'dah as one of four blessed days with specific recommended rituals.
The life story of Salman al-Farisi is recounted, highlighting his Iranian origins, his search for truth that led him from Zoroastrianism to Christianity, and his eventual enslavement in Medina.
Salman recognized the Prophet's prophetic signs during his captivity, converted to Islam, and was later freed by the Prophet, while the segment concludes by introducing the uprising of Zayd ibn Ali in 122 AH.
