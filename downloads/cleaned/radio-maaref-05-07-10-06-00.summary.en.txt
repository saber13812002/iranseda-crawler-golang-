In this radio program, Hujjat al-Islam Rabani Golepaygani examines the theological issue of Imamate and addresses Sunni objections. The core discussion focuses on refuting the argument that Quranic narrations regarding Imamate are transmitted exclusively through Shia narrators and are not cited by Sunnis. Sharaf al-Din argues that both the minor and major premises of this objection are invalid, demonstrating that many of these narrations are also found in authoritative Sunni books with similar chains of transmission. He explains that although some Shia narrators were labeled as Rafidi or Ghalia in Sunni biographical dictionaries, these labels did not negate their recognized justice and trustworthiness in transmitting hadiths. Emphasizing the piety, caution, and exceptional precision of Shia scholars, Sharaf al-Din asserts that they are unparalleled in preserving and memorizing narrations, with the Holy Family serving as their practical model. He also highlights the significance of key Shia sources like the Kitab al-Kafi, noting that dismissing them stems from the ignorance of the objectors. Finally, the speaker praises Sharaf al-Din's coherent and powerful writing style, stressing the importance of accurately understanding Shia narrators to correctly interpret Quranic hadiths.
