In this radio program, Hojjat al-Islam Iran-Dost emphasizes the spiritual significance of "salam" (peace) as a name of God and a path to moral elevation. Citing prophetic traditions, he identifies stinginess in offering greetings as a major sin that severs one's connection to divine grace. The discussion argues that a true Shia is not merely one who verbally greets Imam Hussein (AS), but one whose character and lifestyle embody the essence of peace and mercy. The program then transitions to a story from Saadi's Gulistan regarding contentment, highlighting the disgrace of begging without genuine need. Saadi advises that seeking help from dignified individuals without necessity is shameful and can lead to a loss of respect. The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) is quoted as instructing believers to meet their needs while preserving their self-esteem. It is further noted that in the culture of the Ahl al-Bayt, unmet needs are considered less harmful than asking for them from inappropriate sources. The episode concludes with a prayer for the reappearance of the Imam and a reflection on the virtue of silence and avoiding repetition in speech.
