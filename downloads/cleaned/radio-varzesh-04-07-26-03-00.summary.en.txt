Gholamreza Jabbari, a veteran football referee in Iran, discusses his initial reluctance to referee despite his passion for sports management, noting that he was persuaded by colleagues to continue. He shares memorable anecdotes from high-stakes matches, including the 2005 championship game involving Sepahan, and acknowledges past refereeing errors while emphasizing his commitment to logical and fearless decision-making. Jabbari reflects on his 18-year tenure as the head of the education department at the Football Federation, where he worked to improve professional standards. When asked about corruption in refereeing, he admits that while some individuals may be involved, it is not representative of the entire profession. He argues that establishing a formal "Refereeing System" and a dedicated "Referees Organization," similar to the existing League Organization, is essential for quality control and preventing corruption. Leveraging his 30 years of teaching and 25 years of executive experience, Jabbari expresses his readiness to run for the presidency of the Football Federation. He concludes by stressing the need for a clear strategic roadmap to develop football and sports in the country.
