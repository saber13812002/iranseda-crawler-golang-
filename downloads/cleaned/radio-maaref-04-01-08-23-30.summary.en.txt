This radio program examined the historical conflicts between Muslims and the Eastern Roman Empire during the early Islamic period.
Key events discussed include the Battle of Mu'tah in the eighth year of Hijra and the military expedition to Tabuk in the ninth year.
Although the Tabuk campaign did not result in a direct battle, it served as a major military maneuver that led to significant political gains and widespread conversions to Islam.
The program also addressed the delayed deployment of Usama's army during the Prophet's time and its eventual execution under Caliph Abu Bakr.
The second part of the broadcast focused on the story of Zayd ibn Harithah and the abolition of the pre-Islamic adoption system.
Through the Prophet's marriage to Zayd's ex-wife, Zaynab bint Jahsh, this Jahili tradition was effectively and Quranically negated.
Finally, the discussion highlighted Israel's broken promises and US support, emphasizing the need for Muslim nations to awaken and act independently.
These analyses suggest that global powers are indifferent to Muslim rights, necessitating direct action by the people themselves.
