This program critically examines the claim that "the Ummah will not agree upon error" from both chain of narration and content perspectives. The analysis reveals that the supporting hadiths lack authentic chains of transmission, with many likely being later fabrications or additions. Even if the chains were accepted, the texts do not imply a universal consensus but rather serve as prohibitions against misguidance or are interpreted as prohibitions in the form of news. Furthermore, relying on "majority rule" during disagreement contradicts Quranic verses that establish truth and adherence to the Book and Sunnah as the primary criteria. The Shia perspective argues that God preserves the community from error by designating the Infallibles (Ahl al-Bayt) as divine proofs to prevent deviation. Consequently, a consensus based solely on the general public is not valid; only a consensus involving or aligned with the Infallible is considered authoritative. Ultimately, the Sunni foundations for establishing consensus are deemed unacceptable due to flaws in their chains of transmission and logical reasoning.
