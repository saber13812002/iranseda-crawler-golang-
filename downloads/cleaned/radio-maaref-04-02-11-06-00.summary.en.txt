This program examines the theoretical and historical foundations of the jurisprudential rule regarding the obligation to avert probable harm. The speaker argues that this rule is rooted in reason and nature, noting that its basis is rational for the Ash'aris but textual and consensus-based for the Mu'tazilites. Historically, the Mu'tazilites applied this rule from the third century, the Ash'aris from the fifth, and the Imamiyya from the fourth century, although it lacked independent application in the second and third centuries. In theological discourse, obligation is defined as the rational perception of the necessity of an action, rather than merely the liability for punishment, a distinction clarified through the views of Shahid Sadr. The text also distinguishes between legal obligations governed by divine law and rational obligations governed by intellect. Finally, the speaker emphasizes the importance of semantic analysis in theology and usul, deferring the etymological discussion of the term "averting" to the next session.
