The radio program "Andisheha" discusses the political implications of promoting ancientism in Iran, following holiday greetings. The episode distinguishes between archaeology as a legitimate scientific discipline and ancientism as a targeted political ideology. Dr. Rasoul Rezaei defines ancientism as an excessive glorification of the past that seeks to revive outdated laws, potentially leading to the worship of antiquity. He warns that foreign media exploit this trend to sow discord and division within Iranian society. A primary conflict arises when ancientist views contradict religious teachings, particularly when archaic customs clash with rational and divine principles. The practice of female infanticide in pre-Islamic Arabia is cited as an example of the danger in justifying harmful traditions solely based on their age. The program concludes by emphasizing the need to carefully separate historical research from deviant ideologies to prevent unnecessary societal sensitivities.
