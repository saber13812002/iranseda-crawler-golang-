This radio program explores the concepts of divine knowledge and worthy worship, emphasizing that fully comprehending God's essence is impossible for anyone, including prophets and angels. However, knowing and worshipping God to the extent required by one's religious duty is both possible and obligatory. The speaker cites Nahj al-Balagha, specifically the Istikhara sermon, to stress the importance of sincerity in worship and warns against treating religious acts as mere trade. The discussion also highlights the duality of exoteric and esoteric meanings in Islamic sciences and the Quran, noting that each level of interpretation contains further depths. Towards the end, the program addresses the social responsibility of Muslims to reform and support one another, as derived from specific Quranic verses. Finally, it distinguishes between worldly pleasures, which are contingent on relieving suffering, and eternal pleasures, which are pure and free from such prerequisites.
