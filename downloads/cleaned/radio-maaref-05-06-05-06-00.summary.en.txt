In this session of the "Mabna" theology program, the discussion continued on the jurisprudential rule of "repelling probable harm" and the required degree of probability for precaution. The speaker examined various scholarly views, including the absolute theory that accepts any probability, and the differentiated theory that distinguishes between otherworldly harm (where even a vague suspicion suffices) and worldly harm (which requires a majority probability or doubt). The fifth view, attributed to Sheikh Ansari and Akhund Khorasani, emphasizes the obligation to repel doubtful harm, rejecting probabilities lower than doubt. Finally, the speaker endorsed the sixth view, which uses "rational probability" (aqlai) as the standard; this is a level of likelihood that common sense and sound conscience find significant and fear-inducing. This perspective rejects both negligible probabilities and a strict limit of doubt, instead relying on the general human perception of risk. The speaker critiqued the arguments for the absolute theory by referencing the "conflict with sound conscience" and the necessity of preserving the prophetic argument. The conclusion is that the criterion for action is a rationally significant probability where the harm is important and irreparable.
