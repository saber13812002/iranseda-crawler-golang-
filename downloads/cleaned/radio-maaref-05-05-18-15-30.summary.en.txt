In this radio program, two jurists discussed the principle of caution regarding human blood and its implications for proving murder.
They explained that this principle acts as a double-edged sword; on one hand, it establishes the Qasama oath to prevent the victim's blood from being wasted when there is no witness or confession.
On the other hand, it establishes the rule of doubt to prevent unjust retribution when the judge has any suspicion about the accused's guilt.
Dr. Peyvandi emphasized that the majority of jurists consider the rule of doubt applicable to retribution, meaning any uncertainty in guilt nullifies the punishment.
The distinction between Qasama and the rule of doubt was also clarified; Qasama is executed only when the judge has a strong presumption of guilt, but if doubt exists, Qasama is also invalid.
The final conclusion was that the legal policy prefers a judge to err on the side of pardon rather than to err in punishment.
Therefore, until the judge reaches certainty or full confidence, they have no right to issue a retribution verdict and must acquit the accused.
