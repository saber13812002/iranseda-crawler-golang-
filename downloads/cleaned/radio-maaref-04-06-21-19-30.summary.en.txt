In the first segment, Judge Ahmed cites the Caliph's order to stop Afshin from killing Aboudalf, aiming to prevent a major fitna. Afshin leaves the palace in anger, and the Judge is reprimanded by the Caliph for falsely attributing the order to him. The Judge's slave also criticizes him for his oppressive rule and involvement in the blood of the innocent. The second segment explores Islamic narrations regarding the severity of slander and false accusations. Imam Ali (AS) described slander as heavier than the sky and mountains, while Imam Sadiq (AS) stated that it dissolves the faith in a believer's heart. The worldly consequences include the destruction of social respect, while the afterlife punishments involve being trapped in filth and fire on Judgment Day. Finally, slander is deemed a sin harsher than adultery and backbiting, which can only be forgiven by the victim's consent.
