The radio program primarily discusses "dynamic jurisprudence" and the dichotomy of "fixed and variable" elements in Islamic law.
The guest argues that concepts like "childhood" are not inherently static but evolve over time due to social changes.
It is emphasized that contemporary jurisprudential discourse no longer accepts the fixed-variable dichotomy, asserting that all domains are subject to change.
This shift in discourse requires new strategies, termed "jurisprudence of resistance," to adapt to the challenges of the current era.
The program also included a tribute to Sardar Amir Ali Hajizadeh, highlighting the importance of national unity and support for the leadership.
The spiritual segment featured the recitation and translation of verses from the Quranic chapters of Fussilat and Ash-Shura.
Listeners were finally encouraged to participate in the National Conference on Religion, Culture, and New Media.
The discussion illustrates that jurisprudence must align with temporal changes to remain dynamic and applicable.
