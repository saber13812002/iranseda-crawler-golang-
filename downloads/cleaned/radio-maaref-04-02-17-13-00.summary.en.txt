In this radio program, the speaker explores the spiritual and practical expectations that Imam Reza (PBUH) has for his followers, reversing the common dynamic of petitioning the Imam. The first expectation is action-oriented faith, emphasizing that pure intentions are insufficient without the fulfillment of religious obligations such as prayer and khums. The second expectation is moderation in religious practice, urging believers to avoid both the extremes of obsessive ritualism and the underestimation of the Imams' spiritual status. The speaker highlights that Islam is a religion of reason, making rationality and the rejection of superstition or baseless advice the third key expectation for a true Shia. Furthermore, the centrality of the Quran as a guide for resolving doubts and the necessity of adhering to the principle of Wilayat (guardianship) are presented as the fourth and fifth expectations. The talk references a historical narrative where Imam Reza initially refused a group of visitors from Medina because their actions contradicted their claim of being Shias, underscoring the need for self-reflection. The program concludes with a comprehensive prayer for healing, social stability, and the early appearance of Imam Mahdi (AJ).
