The radio program "Green Blue Club," featuring Hujjat al-Islam Lughmani, explored the concepts of "Karim" (generosity) and "Karamat" (dignity) during the Decade of Karamat.
The speaker categorized people into four groups—generous, stingy, miserly, and noble—based on a hadith, defining the noble person as one who feeds others even if they do not consume themselves.
Through anecdotes about Hujjat al-Islam Shafati and the interactions of a wealthy farmer, he illustrated that nobility can lead to prosperity even in the most difficult circumstances.
The discussion also highlighted the subtle differences between stinginess and greed, emphasizing the importance of preserving others' dignity in noble behavior.
Referencing the story of Abdullah ibn Umar, the speaker identified two keys to peace: sleeping without resentment toward others and viewing oneself as a guest in this world.
He stressed that noble individuals, by relying on God and avoiding fear of the past or future, lead lives filled with joy and tranquility.
Finally, the Prophet Muhammad was presented as the ultimate model of nobility, whose forgiveness and trust in divine justice reassured his followers regarding the Day of Judgment.
