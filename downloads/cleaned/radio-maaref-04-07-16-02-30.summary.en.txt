In this radio program, Professor Mohammad Sharoudi addressed various theological questions from listeners. He explained that the Throne (Arsh) is not a physical location but a symbol of God's infinite knowledge and power, which transcends space and time. Regarding Islam, he emphasized that the faith is inherently authoritative, rejecting the division into "authoritarian" and "non-authoritarian" Islam. The pressure of the grave was described not as a physical punishment, but as a spiritual and barzakhic pressure resulting from witnessing one's sins in the afterlife. He also clarified that the duration of the world and the exact time of the apocalypse are known only to God, and that the governance of the Ahl al-Bayt continues through their returns. The concept of self-accounting was defined as a daily review of one's actions and speech to achieve spiritual perfection. Finally, he interpreted Fatima Zahra's visit to Imam Hussein's grave as a spiritual connection and illumination to the holy site of Karbala, rather than a physical meeting with her martyred son.
