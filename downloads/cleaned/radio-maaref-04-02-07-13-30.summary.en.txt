The radio program "Andisheha" examined the characteristics of secular spirituality and contrasted them with religious spirituality. Dr. Mohammad Jafari described secular spirituality as a personalized, religion-averse structure rooted in liberalism, aiming solely for physical and mental comfort. In contrast, religious spirituality is founded on rationality, the pursuit of perfection, and proximity to God, defining humans beyond mere animal needs. The speaker criticized the secular concept of the "inner voice," arguing it lacks divine color and differs from the Islamic "call of nature." He also addressed the harms of extreme emotionalism and superstitious beliefs, such as jinn, among some religious individuals. Furthermore, he deemed the Law of Attraction incorrect from both jurisprudential and rational perspectives, emphasizing the need to evaluate actions based on reason and revelation. Ultimately, these spiritual practices were presented as commercial packages for escaping stress, lacking genuine mystical depth or divine purpose.
