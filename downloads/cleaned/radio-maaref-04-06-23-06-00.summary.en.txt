This program explores the exegesis of verses 3 to 7 of Surah Al-An'am, drawing on the insights of Ayatollah Javadi Amoli. The central discussion distinguishes between proving God's existence and validating prophethood, clarifying that miracles serve to confirm a prophet's claim rather than to prove God's essence, which is established through rational arguments like contingency and order. The interpretation of "they only denied the truth" suggests that by rejecting the fundamental principles of monotheism and absolute lordship, the disbelievers implicitly rejected prophethood and miracles, as accepting the messenger is contingent on accepting the sender. It is further explained that the polytheists' primary objection was not to God as a creator, but to the concept of specific divine management over human affairs. The text also highlights the direct correlation between social sins and the withdrawal of divine blessings, indicating that blessings are fundamentally tests that lead to destruction if met with ingratitude. The mention of the destruction of previous nations underscores the consistent causal laws in history. Finally, the resistance of disbelievers to accepting the Quran, even when presented with tangible evidence, stems from their fundamental rejection of divine revelation and human prophethood.
