The text refutes the claim that the British manipulated the Shahnameh during the Pahlavi era, instead highlighting Ferdowsi's pivotal role in reviving the Persian language and theorizing an "Islamic Iran." The speaker argues that Ferdowsi embedded Islamic and Shia ethical values into pre-Islamic heroes, thereby combating both the Arab tyranny of the Umayyads and Abbasids and anti-Islamic nationalist (Shu'ubi) currents. It is asserted that the Shahnameh does not merely defend monarchy but judges kings based on Islamic criteria, presenting Rostam as a symbol of Islamic courage and morality. The text also notes the historical dominance of Iranian culture during the Abbasid period, which contradicts Western nationalist narratives. Finally, the speaker praises the readiness and deep understanding of the new generation of students and lauds the Islamic Revolution as a national movement that cut foreign dependence by trusting the people.
