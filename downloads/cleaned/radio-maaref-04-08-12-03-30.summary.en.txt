In this program, Professor Shakarin explained Darwin's evolutionary principles, emphasizing Malthusian population dynamics. The first principle, "struggle for existence," refers to the competition organisms face with environmental threats to survive. The second principle, "survival of the fittest" or natural selection, states that only those with greater adaptation to environmental conditions survive. This process involves no conscious selection; rather, survival is a deterministic and automatic outcome of adaptation. Better-adapted organisms have more opportunities to reproduce, passing their traits to subsequent generations. These two principles form the foundation for the third principle, "heredity," which will be discussed in a future episode.
