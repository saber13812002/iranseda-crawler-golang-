The program begins with harsh criticism of the Zionist regime, describing it as an artificial entity sustained by military and political support from the United States and Britain. The speaker argues that the regime is on the verge of collapse, with Western aid serving merely as artificial respiration to keep it alive. The Palestinian resistance is praised as a symbol of faith triumphing over imperial powers, with a promise that this resistance will ultimately lead to the regime's complete destruction. The second part of the broadcast introduces the radio drama "Jame'e Dast-Pas," based on Sahrab Sepheri's novel, which depicts the events of the 1963 uprising. In this episode, Salār struggles to treat his son Sohrab, who was wounded during the clashes, with the help of Maluk and Afthana. Family conflicts and disputes over unpaid funds by officials highlight the social tensions of that era. Finally, Salār, seeing his son's critical condition and political awakening, resolves to seek revenge against the irresponsible authorities.
