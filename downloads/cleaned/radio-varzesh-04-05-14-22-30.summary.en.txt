Dr. Zahra Nateghi recounts the establishment and growth of women's wushu in Iran during this interview. She describes the formation of the wushu association within the martial arts association and the appointment of Ms. Najati-Zadeh as its head due to wushu's rapid advancement compared to other disciplines. With support from the National Olympic Committee and the hiring of the first Chinese coach, the athletes' technical level improved, leading to the national team's debut in Macau in 2001. After wushu became independent from the martial arts federation in 2003, Dr. Nateghi left due to managerial differences. She subsequently launched emerging sports like women's rugby, cricket, and skating by personally learning and training coaches, achieving national and international success. Finally, she returned as the vice president of women's affairs in the wushu federation after a 15-year absence, emphasizing the importance of women's sports in strengthening families and society.
