The text provides a critical analysis of the post-revolutionary society, categorizing individuals into four distinct types.
The first group consists of corrupt individuals who exploit religious tools to pursue power, wealth, and status, using divine appearances to mask their sins.
The second group are hypocritical preachers who claim moral authority but actually obstruct justice and seek worldly gains under the guise of the afterlife.
The third group are those who feign asceticism and contentment after failing to attain power, relying on silence and concealment to survive a repressive political climate.
The fourth group are the remaining pure revolutionaries who, despite fear and persecution, maintain their faith in the afterlife and justice.
Imam Ali (AS) warns that societal corruption stems from materialism and the neglect of asceticism and certainty, rather than from religious differences.
He emphasizes that corrupt rulers have misinterpreted ownership, treating public assets as their personal spoils of war.
Finally, the concept of Wilayat is defined as a model for justice, compassion, and understanding people's suffering, which should serve as a unifying force for Islam rather than a source of division.
