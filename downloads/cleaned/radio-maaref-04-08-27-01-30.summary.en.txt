In this radio program, Professor Fatemiya first interprets the 17th wisdom of Nahj al-Balagha, warning against arrogance stemming from divine grace and being deceived by others' praise. He cites the stories of Sheikh Jafar Kabir and Sheikh Morteza Ansari to emphasize that academic and spiritual achievements are gifts from God and should not lead to pride. The program then shifts to the memoirs of Mehdi Gholfshani, a freedom fighter who spent six years in Iraqi captivity. He describes the resistance of himself and his fellow prisoners against the enemy's attempts at brainwashing and the broadcasting of vulgar songs in the camps. Gholfshani highlights the burning of a television and a one-week hunger strike to stop sound broadcasts during prayer times as examples of their religious steadfastness. He asserts that adherence to prayer and the Quran was the sole factor that preserved the identity and faith of the captives under the harshest conditions. These memories demonstrate that with strong will and God's help, one can remain dignified in the face of enemy pressure.
