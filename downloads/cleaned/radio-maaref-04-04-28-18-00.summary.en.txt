In this episode of the "Ganj-e Sa'adat" series, Professor Mohammad Hadi Falah examines the moral vice of "stinginess" through the lens of the Quran and Islamic traditions. He argues that stinginess is not merely about financial restraint but represents a fundamental mistrust of God that undermines a person's faith and spiritual freedom. Citing narrations from Imam Ali (AS), the professor describes stinginess as the "garment of all evils," a "robe of poverty and humiliation," and a source of disgrace that is incompatible with the spiritual path of Shias. A crucial distinction is drawn between stinginess and contentment; while contentment is a virtue of reason and generosity, stinginess is characterized as a tool of the ego and a sign of intellectual failure to discern proper use of wealth. The discussion also highlights how stinginess contradicts the expected conduct in Islamic organizations, causing toxic effects on group dynamics. Finally, the program introduces "excuse-making" as a key behavioral sign of stinginess, which will be explored in greater detail in future episodes.
