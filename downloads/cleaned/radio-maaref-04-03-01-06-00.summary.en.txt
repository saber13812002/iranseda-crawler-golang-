This segment of the program continues the specialized discussion on the "rule of repelling probable harm" and its foundations in Islamic theology.
The primary disagreement among schools of thought concerns whether the basis for accepting this rule is rational or traditional, specifically consensus.
The Ash'aris argue that the rule is based on the consensus of prophets and nations, whereas the Shia view it as a self-evident rational principle.
Shia scholars argue that since even non-Muslims accept this principle, its root must be innate reason rather than religious transmission.
The discussion then examined the possibility of achieving consensus, concluding that while logically possible, it is practically impossible for humans to verify.
Finally, the program critiqued the Sunni reliance on the "Verse of the Believers" and analyzed the challenges regarding the independent validity of consensus in both traditions.
