In the radio program "Ganj-e Sa'adat," Hojjat al-Islam Falasi discussed the importance of cultivating the tongue and its role in self-knowledge. He described the tongue at the level of the commanding soul (nafs amara) as a dangerous and corruptible tool that can lead to sins like lying and backbiting if left uncontrolled. Falasi emphasized that this aspect of the tongue is like a blade in the hands of a drunkard and must be supervised by the faculty of reason to transform from a devil's instrument into God's soldier. Citing religious narrations, he warned that an uncultivated tongue has far greater destructive power than wild animals and can easily destroy others' reputations. A key point in the discussion is that training the tongue is an inseparable part of self-discipline and requires focused effort. Finally, he noted that a healthy, cultivated tongue becomes one of the best tools for serving monotheism and moral virtues.
