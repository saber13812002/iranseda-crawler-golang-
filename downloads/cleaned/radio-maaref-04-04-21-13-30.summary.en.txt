Dr. Seyed Javad Miri defines the concept of "Cultural Iran" as a vast region extending from the Caucasus to Central Asia and India, shaped by millennia of civilizational, linguistic, and religious interaction. Rejecting a Eurocentric historical lens, he argues that in Iran, religion and sect have served as unifying forces for national identity rather than conflicting with it, unlike the Western experience. Following recent conflicts, Miri emphasizes that the country's discursive landscape has fundamentally changed, making internal blame-shifting counterproductive at this critical juncture. He advocates for a focus on national interests and the concept of a "resilient society," which requires strengthening not only military capabilities but also the economy, education, and media against hybrid and cognitive warfare. Miri asserts that the Islamic Republic emerged from the society itself, and therefore, necessary reforms must be driven from within to navigate the current precarious situation. He concludes by urging a redefinition of Iranian identity based on shared historical and cultural realities to prevent ideological fragmentation and ensure collective survival.
