This radio program examined the jurisprudential criteria for unlawful economic activities, identifying usury, hoarding, and collusion as primary examples of prohibited transactions.
It was emphasized that the vast majority of professions and investments are permissible in Islam, with some, such as agriculture, being even recommended if they generate value for society.
Non-productive investments, such as speculative real estate or currency trading that creates no added value, are considered religious and economic violations.
Foreign investment is deemed necessary and permissible provided it adheres to the principle of preventing foreign domination and avoids creating absolute dependency.
The government's role in achieving annual economic goals should focus on facilitating private sector growth rather than direct state intervention, which stifles competition.
Governance must adopt a benevolent approach to build public trust, removing bureaucratic hurdles and acting as a supporter rather than a competitor to producers.
The gradual reduction of state ownership and the transfer of responsibilities to the private sector are key principles of Islamic economic jurisprudence.
