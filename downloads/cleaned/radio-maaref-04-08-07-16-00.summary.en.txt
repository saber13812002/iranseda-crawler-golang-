In the radio program "Persaman Iteqadi" featuring Professor Mohammadi, various Islamic theological topics were discussed. The session began by emphasizing the value of knowledge as both a material and spiritual treasure, noting that seeking answers invites divine mercy. Regarding the permissibility of cursing officials, the professor argued against superficial judgments, using the parable of the blind men and the elephant to highlight the need for understanding hidden causes and consequences. The distinction between "cursing" and "sending blessings" was also clarified, with blessings holding a higher rank while cursing serves a natural function of purifying the heart. The program explained that enduring illness yields a specific divine "reward" (awj) distinct from the "merit" (thawab) of worship. It was further stated that reading the Persian translation of the Quran does not carry the specific merit of reciting the Arabic text, and listeners were advised to learn Arabic gradually by listening to recitations. Finally, the discussion covered the concept of "khuruj" (rebellion) and the rulings regarding vows made conditional on the fulfillment of a wish.
