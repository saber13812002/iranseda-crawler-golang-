The text critiques the analyses of left and right intellectuals who attribute the failure of the Islamic Revolution to the society's backwardness and lack of civil institutions. The speaker argues that these analyses are merely excuses for their own failures and a way to evade historical responsibility. He asserts that the Western liberal democratic model, rooted in capitalism, is incompatible with Iran's social and cultural structures, and its imposition has led to failure. Instead, the speaker advocates for a hybrid discourse combining leftist concepts, nationalism, and religion to organize the middle class and gain a real social base. The latter part of the program examines a narration from Imam Kazim regarding the global governance of Imam Zaman and the treatment of non-Muslims. The speaker validates this narration by referencing various sources, considering it reliable despite being technically marasil (unconnected chain). He concludes that the narration is trustworthy due to multiple corroborating evidences and reflects the divine plan for global monotheism.
