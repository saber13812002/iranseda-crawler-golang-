This radio program detailed the jurisprudential rulings regarding Hajj rituals and daily religious matters. According to the majority of Maraji, if the sacrifice is delayed until the night of the 11th of Dhul-Hijjah due to crowds, it remains valid, and pilgrims can shave their heads and exit Ihram that same night, though followers of specific Maraji must wait until the next day. Transporting the sacrificial meat to one's home country for the needy is religiously permissible and facilitated by the Saudi government. After completing the rituals, the prohibitions of Ihram are lifted, except for the use of perfume and marital relations with one's spouse. The program also clarified that a journey of less than 20.5 kilometers does not constitute travel, meaning prayers and fasting remain complete. Paying Khums approximately is only permissible if one is certain the amount is not less than the due or after consulting a Marja. Charging a fee for notarization or power of attorney is lawful if the service is legal. In congregational prayer, the invalidity of one or two individuals' prayers does not invalidate the prayers of the others. Finally, doubt regarding major ritual impurity does not invalidate fasting or prayer, and the ritual bath is not obligatory in such cases.
