In this tafsir of Surah Al-Ma'idah, it is emphasized that belief in the Day of Judgment is the primary condition for implementing jurisprudential and legal rulings, as it compels individuals to monitor their actions and intentions.
Hajjati Amoli critiques Fakhr Razi's view, arguing that prophets are aware of the inner intentions of their communities on the Day of Judgment, and their testimony is based on divine knowledge of the unseen, not merely external observation.
The verse where prophets say "We have no knowledge" does not imply absolute ignorance, but rather indicates that their knowledge is limited to what God has taught them.
Based on philosophical discussions, the knowledge of any being is proportional to its existence and limitations, and knowing the unseen requires a connection with God, who is present everywhere.
The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) neither withholds nor fails to understand the revealed laws of the unseen; rather, he is the trustee of revelation, and each person benefits from these laws according to their capacity for understanding.
Ultimately, knowing the unseen and having certainty in the afterlife requires understanding God, as He is the source of knowledge and presence, and prophets have attained this knowledge through their connection with Him.
