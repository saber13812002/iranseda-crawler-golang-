This radio program examined the legal and jurisprudential frameworks for addressing elder abuse in Iran.
A key point discussed is that, unlike some other countries, Iran's legal system lacks a specific chapter in the Islamic Penal Code dedicated solely to elder abuse.
However, experts emphasized that general criminal laws regarding physical integrity, personality rights, and neglect can be applied to perpetrators of elder abuse.
Islamic jurisprudence also provides mechanisms such as "Ta'zir" that allow the ruler to enact specific criminal policies if deemed necessary for societal welfare.
In family relationships, the prevailing approach favors non-criminal methods and cultural awareness to avoid excessive criminalization within the household.
Conversely, in care facilities and the broader community where legal or contractual duties exist, the application of criminal and supplementary penalties is considered more essential.
Although scattered regulations like Article 633 of the Ta'zirat Law and Article 295 of the Penal Code cover some aspects of abuse, significant enforcement challenges and the need for more precise mechanisms remain.
