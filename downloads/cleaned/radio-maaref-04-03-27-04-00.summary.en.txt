The program addressed various jurisprudential questions, including the permissibility of specific supplications in prayer, the validity of wudu with minor interruptions, and the ruling on khums for retirement gold.
In the "Sarmash" segment, the discussion focused on Nahj al-Balagha, emphasizing the reciprocal rights and duties between rulers and citizens as a divine mandate for societal stability.
Imam Ali (AS) described this mutual obligation as essential for the dignity of religion, noting that one-sided rights only exist in the relationship between humans and God.
The Sufi section, led by a scholar, analyzed the profound spiritual dimensions of prayer according to Imam Khomeini, particularly regarding the inner purity of the prayer place.
The concept of "time" in Sufism was explained as a state of balance between submission to divine commands and adherence to Sharia law.
These segments aim to enhance religious awareness and address audience inquiries in both fiqh and mysticism.
