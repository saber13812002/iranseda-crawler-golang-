In the "Porsoman-e Qur'ani" program, Hujjat al-Islam Maleki addressed listeners' questions regarding various Quranic concepts. He defined the "middle nation" as the moderate Islamic community that serves as a model of balance, rejecting both materialism and extreme otherworldliness. Regarding the compilation of the Quran, it was emphasized that the writing and memorization of verses were strictly supervised and approved by the Prophet, with the arrangement of surahs either following divine command or being organized by a committee led by Zayd ibn Thabit. The scholar also clarified that differing interpretations of the Quran stem from personal biases and a lack of knowledge in Arabic grammar and tafsir rules, rather than any flaw in the text itself. Furthermore, the concept of God's "gratitude" was explained as a generous reward for human deeds, highlighting divine mercy and kindness. The program also explored the distinct meanings of terms like "repentance" and "faith" when applied to God versus humans, noting their educational and psychological impacts. The episode concluded with a call to stay connected to the Quran and to support the suffering people of Gaza.
