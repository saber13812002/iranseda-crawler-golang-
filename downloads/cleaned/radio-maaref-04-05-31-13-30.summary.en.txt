The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) identified the core of arrogance and dictatorship as the humiliation of divine truth and the devaluation of creation, a struggle all prophets undertook. Imam Hassan (AS) signed the peace treaty not as a surrender, but as a strategic continuation of Imam Ali's path to prevent the total destruction of the Islamic movement. He explained to the people that achieving justice and prosperity requires enduring the costs of resistance rather than yielding to the enemy. Following the treaty, Imam Hassan (AS) actively mobilized public opinion in Medina by delivering harsh sermons against the oppressive Umayyad government. The discussion then shifts to Imam Reza (AS), who combated deviant theological currents and organized a global network known as "Wakalat" to resist Abbasid pressures. Caliph al-Ma'mun exiled Imam Reza (AS) to Marw to curb his influence and suppress the widespread Shia uprisings across the Islamic world. Ultimately, the text asserts that Imam Hassan's peace was a historical and logical necessity that even Imam Hussein (AS) would have been compelled to accept under those specific conditions.
