In this segment of the program, the claim that Jews will have no role in the era of the Mahdi's appearance is discussed based on historical narratives. The speaker critically examines the lineage and conduct of Zuhri (Muhammad ibn Muslim), a key figure in Sunni hadith, arguing that his reliability is questionable due to his close ties with the Umayyad caliphs. Despite having no direct connection to the Prophet, Zuhri is portrayed as a supporter of oppressors, with his ancestors having fought against Muslims in the battles of Badr and Uhud. The speaker highlights that Zuhri served Yazid and Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik and was known as the "handkerchief of the rulers," effectively justifying their injustices to cleanse their hands of sin. Following a recitation of religious litanies and poetry, the focus shifts to the necessity of awakening Islamic nations to fight against internal enemies. The speaker emphasizes that overcoming internal corruption and fostering unity and brotherhood are essential for achieving spiritual power and societal cohesion. This process requires abandoning base desires and adhering to human and religious principles to build a resilient community.
