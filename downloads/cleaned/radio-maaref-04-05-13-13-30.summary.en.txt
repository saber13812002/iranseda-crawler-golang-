In this radio program, Professor Seyed Ahmad Faqih emphasized the central role of prayer and intercession through the Ahl al-Bayt in establishing a connection with God and achieving effective repentance. Citing Quranic verses, he explained that God's door is open only through the Prophet and his family, making supplication in this path the key to divine mercy. The story of Prophet Adam receiving the words of repentance from Gabriel was referenced to demonstrate that intercession is the primary solution for repentance to be accepted. The second part of the discussion compared Islamic asceticism with non-Islamic spiritual practices. Professor Faqih stressed that religious asceticism is purposeful, reasonable, and preserves physical health, whereas non-religious practices are often aimless and harmful to the body. The fundamental difference lies in the ultimate intention; Islamic asceticism aims for divine proximity and service to society, not for gaining ego power or commercial gain. Finally, observing limits in worship, such as fasting and night prayers, was highlighted as a defining characteristic of Islamic asceticism.
