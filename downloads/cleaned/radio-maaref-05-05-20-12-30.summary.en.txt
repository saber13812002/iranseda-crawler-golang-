The radio program "Hekayat-e Azadegi" began by recounting the memories of Javanshahr Kalani, an Isfahan native who spent ten years in Ba'athist Iraqi POW camps. He described the harsh living conditions, including extreme overcrowding, the lack of proper sewage systems, and significant difficulties in accessing clean water and basic hygiene. Kalani also highlighted the astonishment of Red Cross officials regarding the high level of order, morality, and solidarity among Iranian prisoners compared to those in other conflicts, attributing these traits to Islamic teachings and emulation of Imam Ali. The second part of the program focused on the life of Sardar Alireza Tangsiri, a commander of the IRGC Navy, detailing his wartime concerns for his family and his final moments. His family spoke of his courage and calmness in the face of death, noting that his hands were clenched in fists after his martyrdom as a sign of remembering the Supreme Leader and seeking revenge against the enemy. The program aimed to honor the memory of freedmen and martyrs, emphasizing the role of faith and self-mastery in enduring the hardships of captivity and war.
