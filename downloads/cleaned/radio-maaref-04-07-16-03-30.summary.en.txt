Imam Ali (AS) refused Talha and Zubayr's request for a direct share of the public treasury, emphasizing his role as a neutral custodian of the Muslim community's funds. He suggested that if they needed financial support, they should seek it through public consensus and voluntary contributions from the people rather than unilateral appropriation. The Imam firmly rejected the idea of preemptively imprisoning them based on mere suspicion, insisting on strict adherence to justice and the prohibition of oppression. Meanwhile, news reached Medina that Talha and Zubayr had mobilized in Mecca to avenge Uthman, joined by key figures like Marwan ibn al-Hakam. This gathering marked the beginning of a significant conspiracy that would eventually lead to the Battle of the Camel.
