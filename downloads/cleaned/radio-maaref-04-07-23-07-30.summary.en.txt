In the radio program "Mehrbān Bāshim," Dr. Seyed Javad Hosseini, head of the Welfare Organization, emphasized the capabilities of visually impaired individuals and urged officials to trust their potential rather than imposing restrictive views.
The guest highlighted that blind individuals fully utilize their other senses to compensate for their lack of sight, calling for their voices to be taken seriously and for job opportunities that match their qualifications.
The health segment introduced gene therapy breakthroughs that have restored vision to patients with hereditary blindness, offering hope for treating difficult genetic diseases.
Additionally, the head of the Medical Council of Iran refuted claims of a specialist shortage, identifying the core issue as the improper distribution of staff and the centralization of complex services in major cities.
In the ethics section, Professor Mohammad Hadi Fallah discussed the vices of the tongue, specifically focusing on the harmful effects of blaming and criticizing others.
Citing narrations from the Ahl al-Bayt, he warned that those who criticize others will inevitably be afflicted with the same faults they attribute to others.
Practical solutions included gaining awareness, practicing patience and silence in the face of adversity, avoiding envy and arrogance, and repenting from the habit of blame.
Finally, it was stressed that an individual's problems or misfortunes are not necessarily indicative of their moral character or sins, advocating for empathy and understanding.
