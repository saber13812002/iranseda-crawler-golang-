Gratitude for divine blessings is not only a rational and religious duty but also a divine attribute emphasized in the Quran. True gratitude goes beyond verbal praise; it involves utilizing blessings in the path of God to achieve spiritual perfection. The Quran highlights two primary outcomes of gratitude: the increase of divine blessings and liberation from Satan's influence. Satan admits in the Quran that he is powerless against sincere, grateful servants and actively tries to prevent humans from being thankful through various temptations. Worship based on gratitude is termed the "worship of the free," which is considered the highest form of devotion. Imam Sadiq (AS) emphasized that gratitude ensures the permanence and growth of blessings, while seeking forgiveness removes obstacles to divine grace. Ultimately, expressing gratitude to the intermediaries of divine grace, such as the Ahl al-Bayt, and following their example is the path to preserving blessings and achieving a good end.
