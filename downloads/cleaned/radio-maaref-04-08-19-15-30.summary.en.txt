This radio program examined the fundamental difference between conventional jurisprudence and specialized fields like political jurisprudence, emphasizing a consequentialist approach. Experts argued that in political jurisprudence, issuing a fatwa is insufficient; one must consider the social and economic impacts on society. Examples such as mandatory earthquake-resistant construction and third-party insurance demonstrated that legislators sometimes deviate from individual freedom to prevent negative consequences and maintain public order. The subject of political jurisprudence extends beyond individual actions and encompasses four main pillars: government, sovereignty, population, and territory. In discussing political legitimacy, various perspectives including theocratic, elitist, and contractarian views were analyzed. Key characteristics of political jurisprudence were identified as collective identity, volitional nature, and flexibility. Finally, reference books on the philosophy of political jurisprudence were introduced, and the subtle differences between theory and practice in the political system were addressed.
