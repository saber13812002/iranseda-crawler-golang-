In this program, Professor Mohammad Hadi Falahbakhsh identifies anger and rage as an inherent evil and the worst companion of a human being, based on the narrations of Imam Ali. Relying on the principle of the unity of the actor and the act, he explains that repeated anger causes a person to merge with evil, leading to a deviation from the path of spiritual perfection and happiness. Anger is described as the key to all vices, fostering injustice, violence, and corruption while distancing the soul from peace and truth. The professor emphasizes that anger acts like barren, salty soil that wastes any good deeds and prevents the harvesting of moral virtues. The program then recounts the story of Umar ibn al-Khattab and his wife Hind, highlighting his sincere desire to fight and attain martyrdom despite his legal exemption. This narrative illustrates the fundamental difference between a pure motivation for sacrifice in God's path and anger, which is rooted in the ego. The ultimate goal of the discussion is to warn listeners against anger and to encourage them to purify their existence from this moral vice.
