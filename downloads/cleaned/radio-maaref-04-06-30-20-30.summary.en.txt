Operation Kaman 99, also known as the 140-Plane Operation, was the first and largest air operation in the history of the Iranian Air Force during the eight-year war. Initiated under the orders of then-Air Force Commander Javad Fakhouri, 140 aircraft simultaneously targeted Iraqi military bases and facilities. The primary objectives included airbases, fuel depots, radars, and communication centers in cities such as Kirkuk, Mosul, and Hilla. The deployment of F-4 and F-14 fighters, along with refueling and intelligence-gathering planes, rendered the Iraqi airspace non-operational for 69 days following the strike. This heavy blow to Iraq's oil and military infrastructure significantly reduced the enemy's mobility, effectively grounding their forces. The second part of the program featured the captivity memories of Mr. Nouri, a freed prisoner, who described the harsh transport conditions and physical and psychological torture in Iraqi camps. He recounted the resistance of young Iranians against enemy pressure to change their beliefs and march before Saddam Hussein, as well as the martyrdoms during this period. The return of the freed prisoners in 1990, despite the grief of Imam Khomeini's passing, symbolized the victory of will and faith over oppression and torture.
