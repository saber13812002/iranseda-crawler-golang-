The radio program began by clarifying that Walid ibn Mughirah, though a member of the Quraysh tribe, had no close kinship with the Prophet. It then recounted the story of a shepherd in the Battle of Khaybar, highlighting the Prophet's command to return entrusted sheep to their owners despite the ongoing conflict. The main segment focused on Sheikh Tusi, a preeminent Shia scholar and author of key texts like "Tibyan" and "Rijal." It explained that after the Karkh riot in Baghdad destroyed his library, Sheikh Tusi migrated to Najaf in 448 AH. This migration transformed Najaf from a small town into the primary center of Shia scholarship and the Hawza. The program also addressed the Supreme Leader's ruling that travelers with the intention of long-term residence are immediately considered residents for prayer purposes. Finally, the broadcast concluded with condolences for Arbaeen and a live report from service camps along the pilgrimage route from Najaf to Karbala.
