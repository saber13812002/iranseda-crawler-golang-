The program begins by highlighting the historical role of Arabic and Persian as the primary carriers of Islamic concepts across the Muslim world and Asia. Western colonizers, particularly the British, sought to sever Muslims' connection to their religious identity by forcibly imposing their own languages and dismantling indigenous ones after the fall of Islamic powers. This process served as a tool for cultural and political dominance, fostering a sense of inferiority and dependency among colonized nations. The discussion then shifts to an ethical analysis of arrogance and its related concepts, explained by Hujjat al-Islam Fallah. He distinguishes between pride, arrogance, vanity, and self-satisfaction, noting that while they share a common root in the ego, they differ in their specific manifestations. Pride is defined as self-deception regarding one's abilities, distinct from arrogance, which is a sense of superiority, and vanity, which is boasting about one's attributes. The speaker emphasizes that language education should not follow a colonial or coercive model but should instead support the preservation of Islamic identity and the equitable development of societies.
