In the radio program "Ma'aref," Hojjat al-Islam Sadeghi addressed historical questions from listeners. He first explained how Abdullah ibn Zubayr, despite lacking a formal position in the six-member council, secured the caliphate for about 11 years through his close familial ties to the Prophet, the support of his aunt Aisha, and the influence of the Zubayrid clan. The second section examined the uprising of Mukhtar al-Thaqafi, which lasted only a year and a half and ended with Mukhtar's martyrdom and the death of his commander, Ibrahim al-Ashtar, in battle against the Umayyads. Sadeghi also traced the etymology of the term "Shia" from the Prophet's era to the post-Uthman distinction between "Shiat Ali" and "Shiat Uthman." The historical centrality of the Shia community was discussed, noting its initial base in Kufa and later hubs in Qom, Karakh, and Sabzevar. Finally, the conversation covered the sword Zulfiqar, which reliable sources identify as a war booty from Badr that the Prophet gave to Ali, becoming famous during the Battle of Uhud with the cry "No sword but Zulfiqar."
