This radio program examined the role of the Islamic Revolution in transforming family law and modernizing jurisprudence to meet contemporary societal needs. Professor Ahansafi presented examples such as medical expenses and contagious diseases to demonstrate how contemporary jurists employ methods like "qiyas awla" to address new challenges. However, Professor Hedayatnia clarified that the legislator has not yet updated the list of diseases causing divorce, instead relying on the legal mechanism of divorce to resolve these issues. He also highlighted the reform of women's blood money, emphasizing that these changes reflect progress in women's rights within the framework of Sharia rather than a regression. The ultimate goal of the system is to preserve the family structure while ensuring individual rights through dynamic jurisprudential interpretations and the oversight of seminaries. This approach is grounded in Article 4 of the Constitution, which mandates that all laws must be based on Islamic criteria. Finally, it was emphasized that Islamic jurisprudence possesses sufficient capacity to regulate family relations in the modern era.
