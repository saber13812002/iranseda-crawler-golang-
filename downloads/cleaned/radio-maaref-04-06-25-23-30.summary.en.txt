In this radio program, the commentator distinguishes between "fawt" (annihilation) and "mawt" (transition), arguing that the Quran never uses the former for death. He defines death not as the end of existence, but as the soul's separation from the earthly body and its attachment to a barzakh body. Consequently, humans experience two deaths (transition from the world to barzakh, and from barzakh to the afterlife) and three lives (world, barzakh, and resurrection), with the afterlife being eternal. The speaker also explains that angels undergo a form of death defined by the loss of the ability to answer God, rather than the destruction of their essence. In response to a listener's question, it is emphasized that no paradise blessing is exclusive to a specific gender, and worldly spouses of believers hold a higher status than heavenly houris. Finally, the selection of a spouse in the afterlife is entirely up to the individual, who may choose between their worldly or heavenly partner based on personal preference.
