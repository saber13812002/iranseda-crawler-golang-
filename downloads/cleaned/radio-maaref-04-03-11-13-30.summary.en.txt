The radio program "Andisheha" explored the methods used to propagate religious doubts within society. Dr. Seyed Hassan Hashemi, a faculty member at the University of Religions and Denominations, identified ancient Iranism and racism as primary tools for generating such doubts. He argued that these movements create a dichotomy between being Iranian and being Muslim, attempting to sow ideological discord by devaluing the pre-Islamic era. Hashemi also highlighted the redefinition of fundamental concepts like family, marriage, and reason as another strategy to conflict with religious teachings. He emphasized the necessity of shifting from a passive stance to an active, analytical approach when confronting these doubts. This active approach involves logically analyzing claims and employing Quranic methods in debate. Finally, the importance of "doubt literacy" for the audience and the need for religious leaders to thoroughly understand opposing currents were discussed.
