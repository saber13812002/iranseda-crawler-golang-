This segment of the Kalam lecture explores the conceptual nature of "harm" (zarar) and its relationship with "benefit" (nafa'). Various perspectives, such as harm being an existential matter or a non-existential counterpart to benefit, are examined and critiqued. Ayatollah Esfahani refutes both views, arguing that harm is fundamentally an ordinary, undesirable concept rather than strictly existential or non-existent. The speaker rejects the over-generalization of linguistic terms like "deficiency" and "contradiction," asserting that harm can manifest as either an existential or non-existential state. Consequently, imposing absolute conditions on the nature of harm is considered logically and ordinarily incorrect. The discussion then transitions to the concept of "probability" within the rule of "obligation to repel probable harm." This term is defined as a likelihood that falls below the threshold of certainty. This analysis sets the stage for a detailed examination of the degrees of probability and the jurisprudential debates surrounding the repulsion of probable harm.
