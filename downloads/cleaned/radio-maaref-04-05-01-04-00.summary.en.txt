The program begins by emphasizing the choice of permanence over transience and defines asceticism as the immediate fulfillment of duties without relying on the future.
The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) presents a model of humility and distance from material luxury by describing his simple lifestyle, such as wearing rough clothes and riding an unadorned donkey.
The core discussion revolves around narrations from the book of Kafi that identify the purity of the stomach and private parts as the highest form of worship and struggle.
Imam Baqir (AS) told a man who considered himself weak in worship that abstaining from the haram is the greatest jihad, outweighing the lack of voluntary acts.
The narrations stress that most sins stem from consuming the haram and sexual misconduct, which are the primary causes of entering hellfire.
The Prophet (PBUH) expressed three main concerns for his community after his departure, one of which was the corruption of the stomach and private parts.
The final conclusion is that piety and adhering to halal and haram are more important than performing recommended acts, as inner and outer purity are the keys to salvation.
