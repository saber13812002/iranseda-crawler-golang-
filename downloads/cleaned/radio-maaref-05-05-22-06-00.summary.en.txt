This radio program discusses the theological rule of "repelling probable harm" and the required degree of probability for its rational obligation. The second view, which insists on "certainty" (probability above fifty percent), is critiqued because fear arises even at lower degrees and no specific limit exists in rational judgment. The third view posits that "absolute probability" is sufficient for repelling harm in critical cases, such as eschatological or life-threatening dangers. Mulla Lahiji argues that the mere probability of religious news is enough to create fear and necessitate inquiry, even if certainty is not reached. Sheikh Bahai also considers repelling any probable harm essential for preserving life, as seen in the ruling on tayammum. Similarly, Sayyid Muhammad Kazim Yazdi deems repelling even vague probabilities necessary in cases of significant harm. The overall conclusion is that in vital matters, reason mandates caution without waiting for the threshold of certainty. These discussions demonstrate that in Shia theology, the boundary between doubt and certainty is set very low for important harms to preserve religion and life.
