The radio program "Tale of Freedom" began by sharing the memoirs of Kazem Zarandi, a freedom fighter from Qom, who recounted his journey from youth to the front lines of the Sacred Defense. He described his injury and transfer to Iraqi hospitals, where he was initially held at the Zubair military camp due to a lack of space. Zarandi detailed the harsh prison conditions, physical and psychological torture, and the enemy's attempts to force prisoners into anti-regime interviews. He also recalled the quarantine period in Mosul and Baghdad camps, enduring severe hunger and thirst under critical circumstances. The second part of the program featured the stories of freedom fighters' wives, with Sadegheh Kharra describing the shock of hearing her husband Ali Dastjerdi's release news, only to learn he was not in the first returning group. The broadcast concluded with tips on saving electricity and water, along with an introduction to the "Iran Jan" cultural event in the city of Minoodar.
