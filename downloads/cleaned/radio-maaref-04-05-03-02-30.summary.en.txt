In this radio broadcast aired on the dawn of the 29th of Muharram, the program begins with a jurisprudential discussion on the concept of "Ghina." Professor Vahidpour explains that Ghina refers to a style of singing inherently associated with sinful gatherings and pleasure-seeking, making it haram to listen to or perform even if the lyrics are from the Quran or religious poetry. He emphasizes the need for caution in religious gatherings to avoid such contamination, citing the Supreme Leader's warning regarding certain Quran recitations. The program then addresses several jurisprudential questions, including the ruling that a former spouse's mother remains a mahram to that spouse's children even after the divorce. It is also clarified that a vow made for a specific location that became inaccessible should be fulfilled elsewhere with the same intention. Regarding the Quran becoming impure with blood, the solution is to wash the affected page with running or stagnant water and bury it if it becomes unusable. Finally, the ruling on "Radd al-Mazalim" (return of usurped wealth) indicates that if the rightful owner cannot be reached, depositing the amount into a valid charity fund supervised by the Marja is permissible as a precautionary measure.
