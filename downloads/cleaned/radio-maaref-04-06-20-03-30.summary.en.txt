The program's fiqh section emphasized that disclosing a patient's medical secrets is religiously and legally forbidden, except in specific cases where it is necessary to save the patient's life or inform their family.
It was clarified that if a person discloses a secret out of ignorance of the ruling and with good intentions, they are not sinful, whereas those who do so knowingly commit a sin.
Medical records are considered confidential documents, and healthcare staff do not have unlimited access to share them with others.
Regarding prayer, joining a congregation in the first or second rakaat requires doing so before the imam goes into ruku, while in the third and fourth rakaats, the follower must recite Al-Fatiha and a surah.
Additionally, supplications in Persian during the Ghunut are only permissible if accompanied by the Arabic prayer, as Persian alone is insufficient.
The funeral prayer must be performed after death and before burial, with the optimal time being after the Maghrib prayer.
Although the skin and hair of cats and rabbits are not impure, their presence on a worshipper's clothing invalidates the prayer because these animals are considered haram to eat.
In the ethical segment, a narration from Imam Ali was cited to stress the importance of guarding one's soul and purifying it to achieve eternal happiness.
