In this radio program, Professor Mousavi examines the jurisprudential and theological rule of "removing probable harm" as a fundamental rational principle. He argues that this rule is not limited to specific issues but applies to any rational probability that entails potential damage. To prove the rule's rationality from a Quranic perspective, verses such as "Say, have you considered" and "Whoever hopes to meet his Lord" are interpreted. Commentators assert that even hope or fear of the afterlife, without absolute certainty, obligates a person to perform good deeds and exercise caution. This rational obligation stems from the principle that removing probable harm is mandatory, whereas seeking probable benefit is merely recommended. Therefore, blindly rejecting divine calls while acknowledging their potential validity is considered misguidance. The discussion concludes that reason dictates that humans should investigate religious claims rather than ignoring them completely to avoid potential eternal harm.
