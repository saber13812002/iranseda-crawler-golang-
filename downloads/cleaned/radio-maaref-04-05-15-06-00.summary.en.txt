This text examines the supplications of Abraham and Ishmael during the construction of the Kaaba, where they requested prosperity, security, and sustenance for the believers.
A key aspect of this prayer is the observance of divine etiquette, as Abraham never made suggestions to God but solely sought the good of this world and the hereafter.
God accepted these prayers, transforming the barren land into a secure and prosperous city where even plants and animals were granted sanctity.
The narrative also highlights the story of Ishmael's sacrifice as an example of monotheistic etiquette and complete submission to God's will, emphasized by the phrase "if God wills."
Furthermore, the concept of "good deeds of this world" is defined as material blessings accompanied by divine knowledge and gratitude, distinct from purely worldly desires.
Finally, the text discusses Jacob's etiquette during his grief over Joseph, noting that he attributed his sorrow and joy exclusively to God.
These accounts demonstrate that religious leaders maintained the highest levels of etiquette and reliance on God throughout all stages of their lives.
