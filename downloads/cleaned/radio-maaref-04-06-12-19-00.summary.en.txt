The radio program "Zamzame Ahkam" began with religious music and prayers before transitioning to a jurisprudential discussion on drug testing on animals. Expert Vahidpour explained that testing an owned animal requires the owner's permission, and if the animal dies, its current market value must be compensated. For ownerless or state-owned animals, testing is permissible if it serves medical science, although causing harm for mere entertainment is strictly forbidden. The discussion also covered the rules for human drug trials, stating they are only allowed if there is no life-threatening risk and the subject consents, or if it is necessary to save others' lives. The second part of the show addressed listener questions regarding a child's legal dependency on their parents for determining their country of residence. It was also clarified that no Khums tax is due on historical coins, even if they remain unused until the end of the year. The program concluded by highlighting Islam's comprehensive protection of animal rights and providing contact numbers for future inquiries.
