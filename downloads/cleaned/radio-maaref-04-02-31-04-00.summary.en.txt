The first segment outlines the jurisprudential rulings regarding staying in Mina during the nights of the 11th and 12th of Dhul-Hijjah, emphasizing that presence from sunset to midnight is obligatory, while recommended acts are encouraged thereafter. Following a prophetic narration on the importance of striving and avoiding sin, the discussion shifted to Imam Musa Sadr’s social initiatives in Lebanon. He established vocational schools, nursing centers, and support organizations for the underprivileged, effectively combating poverty and ignorance. These efforts led to the formation of the Action Organization and eventually Hezbollah, illustrating the link between religious practice and social service. The program then transitioned to a historical analysis of the late Uthmanic era, featuring Dr. Jafari. The role of Marwan ibn al-Hakam’s suspicious letter in escalating Egyptian protests and prompting their return to Medina was examined. Furthermore, Uthman’s responsibility for failing to punish Marwan after the letter was discovered was discussed, highlighting how this inaction fueled public outrage.
