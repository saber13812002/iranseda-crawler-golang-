The program begins with a narrative highlighting the simple lifestyle and rejection of luxury among prominent Islamic scholars.
Examples from the lives of Ayatollah Behjat and Imam Ali (AS) are shared to illustrate these spiritual values.
The discussion then shifts to the theological and philosophical foundations of Islamic governance and the rule of the jurist.
Hojjat al-Islam Borujkar explains Tawhid in three dimensions: Creatorship, Ownership, and Guardianship.
He argues that since God is the absolute Creator and Owner of humanity, ultimate authority belongs to Him.
Consequently, no human being has inherent authority over others or even themselves without divine permission.
During the occultation of the Imams, qualified jurists serve as their legitimate representatives in leadership.
The conclusion emphasizes that Islamic leadership is rooted in divine ownership and rational wisdom.
