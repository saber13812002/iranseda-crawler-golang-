The first section examines the jurisprudential basis for retribution against the killers of the Infallibles, noting that while Ibn Mujam's public murder necessitated execution due to his lack of remorse, the covert poisoning of the Prophet and Imam Hassan was not immediately punished due to societal uncertainty.
The discussion then shifts to the historical narrative of Prophet Musa, highlighting that his prophethood began at Mount Sinai after his exile to Madin and marriage to Shu'ayb's daughter.
In the final segment, Dr. Ra'imi-Pour argues that Iranian universities must abandon the "Third World" mindset to become civilization-building institutions.
He criticizes the current academic reliance on translation and mere citation of Western sources, asserting that such approaches lack the originality required for genuine scientific production.
To achieve civilization-building research, a mental pause and fundamental re-evaluation of research goals and methodologies are essential.
Universities must move beyond a submissive, imitative model and reconstruct their scientific identity through independent planning and self-awareness.
This shift is crucial not only in the humanities but across all disciplines to preserve intellectual and cultural independence.
Ultimately, accepting imposed Western labels is identified as the primary obstacle to achieving a true civilizational status.
