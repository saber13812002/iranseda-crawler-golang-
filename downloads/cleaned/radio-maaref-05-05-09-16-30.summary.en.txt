In this program, Professor Jafari first emphasized the historical significance of the Arbaeen pilgrimage, tracing its roots from Jabir ibn Abdullah Ansari to Imam Sadiq (AS).
He highlighted the historical challenges faced by pilgrims during the Umayyad and Abbasid eras, noting that the greatest freedom and revival in pilgrimages occurred after the fall of Saddam.
In response to a listener's question, he identified the holy sites in Iraq, including Najaf, Karbala, Kazimayn, and Samarra, as the burial places of six Imams.
The professor also placed special emphasis on reviving the shrines of the Four Deputies in Baghdad and visiting Madain to pay respects to Salman al-Farisi and Hudhayfa.
The second part of the program discussed the Sufi concept of "ukhbat" (humble submission), which is rooted in inner humility and modesty before God.
It was explained that reaching high spiritual levels, such as piety and excellence, does not guarantee immunity from spiritual decline, as stagnation on the path is equivalent to regression.
The concept of "Ihsan" was defined as performing good deeds in a beautiful manner, accompanied by perfection and grace.
Finally, constant attention to divine verses and internal reminders was deemed essential to prevent heedlessness and attain the nearness of God's mercy.
