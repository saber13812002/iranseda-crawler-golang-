The radio program begins by discussing the importance of sleep in Islamic traditional medicine, emphasizing that primary sleep belongs to the night while short daytime naps must be managed with specific intervals and rituals.
According to Dr. Pour Hosseini, individuals with a dry temperament should strictly manage their sleep to prevent worsening dryness, and performing precise tasks during the time between dawn and sunrise is recommended for higher productivity.
The second segment features a Quranic interpretation by Hujjat al-Islam Rasoul, who highlights the power of anger and the necessity of controlling it as a key moral trait of a believer.
Citing Nahj al-Balagha and verse 134 of Surah Al-Imran, he explains that a pious person is one who suppresses their anger and forgives the faults of others.
It is clarified that suppressing anger does not mean repressing it to the point of exploding at weaker individuals, but rather digesting it internally to maintain social harmony.
A short dramatic performance illustrates this virtue through the character of Agha Rahmat, who demonstrates patience and forgiveness despite severe provocations from his neighbor.
The program concludes with a speech by the Supreme Leader to the congress of Allameh Amini, praising the spiritual victory of the youth and their ability to master their own souls against Satan.
