Former Iranian national swimmer Mohammad Bidarian shares his experiences from training with the Croatian national team, highlighting the critical difference between practice and competition. He explains how the stress of racing can disrupt carefully planned strategies that work well in training. Bidarian recounts the 2012 London Olympic trials, where he qualified but chose to withdraw from the final due to an unfortunate draw against an Israeli competitor. Despite suffering a torn ligament, he competed in the Olympics driven by a sense of duty to his country and family. After retiring from competitive swimming, he transitioned into coaching and management roles within the swimming federation and the National Olympic Committee. He emphasizes the importance of trusting young generations and leveraging the experience of senior athletes in sports management structures. His ultimate wish is for Iranian sports to bring pride and joy to the people on the international stage.
