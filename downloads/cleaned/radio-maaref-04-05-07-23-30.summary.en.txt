The program distinguishes between genuinely needy individuals who preserve their dignity by not begging and professional beggars who have turned alms-seeking into a job.
The speaker warns that questioning the fundamental principle of charity undermines monotheism and the core of the religion, equating such doubt with disbelief.
To manage aid effectively, it is recommended that mosques and institutions establish committees to verify the actual needs of petitioners and prevent exploitation.
In response to a question about physical differences among people, it was explained that these traits are not God's direct will but results of material and spiritual causes.
This diversity serves a social purpose by fostering interaction, competition, and growth, rather than being an arbitrary divine decree.
The final segment critiques the claimed followers of Abrahamic prophets who, by clinging to material power, contradict the spiritual and simple lifestyle of their respective leaders.
It concludes that claiming to follow a prophet without emulating their practical conduct is invalid and must be measured against historical and social realities.
