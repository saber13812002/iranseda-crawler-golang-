This radio program examined the government's role in facilitating private sector investment and achieving the annual slogan from a jurisprudential and economic perspective.
The primary solution to prevent citizens from diverting capital into non-productive markets like real estate and currency is establishing complete transparency in administrative and economic processes.
Transparency not only prevents corruption and discrimination but also builds mutual trust between the government and the public, encouraging investors to enter real production sectors.
It was emphasized that the state should reduce its reliance on banks for financing and instead strengthen the capital market to foster more realistic and targeted interest rates.
Using supervisory tools such as rigorous credit scoring and transparent tender processes helps prevent resource allocation to unqualified individuals and the accumulation of overdue debts.
The government is obligated to remove unnecessary administrative price controls and ensure fair distribution of opportunities to create a healthy competitive environment.
Ultimately, the state's accountability for negligence and lack of supervision requires legal reforms to ensure the recovery of public assets and enforce liability for misconduct.
