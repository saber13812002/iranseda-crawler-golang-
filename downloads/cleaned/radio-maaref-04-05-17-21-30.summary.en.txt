Following a brief mention of the spiritual significance of Arbaeen, the program focuses on the philosophy behind the occultation of Imam Mahdi and addresses the common query regarding why he must remain hidden. Drawing on the insights of scholars like Sheikh Tusi, Sayyid Murtada, and Allameh Arbilli, the discussion highlights the critical difference between previous prophets and the Prophet of Islam, which lies in the presence or absence of a successor. Previous prophets could face martyrdom because another prophet was destined to continue the mission, but the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH), as the final prophet, had no successor and thus chose migration and concealment to avoid assassination. This logic is applied to Imam Mahdi (AJ), who, as the last Imam, has no replacement to fill the void if he were killed, making his occultation necessary to preserve the divine proof. The program also notes that tyrannical rulers are highly sensitive to the Twelfth Imam because they are aware of his role in overthrowing injustice, unlike previous Imams who did not threaten their rule with armed revolt. Citing narrations about the Umayyads and Abbasids attempting to eliminate the Imams' lineage, the text emphasizes that the Imam's birth and early life were kept secret to prevent his assassination. Ultimately, the occultation is presented as a strategic necessity to protect the continuity of Imamate and ensure the eventual establishment of justice.
