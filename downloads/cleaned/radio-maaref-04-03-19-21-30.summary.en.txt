In this program, Professor Talah Javadi interprets verses 106 to 108 of Surah Al-Ma'idah, focusing on the jurisprudential rules of wills and testimony. He explains that while the testimony of two Muslim witnesses is the primary requirement, the testimony of two People of the Book (Jews and Christians) is permissible in financial matters when Muslims are unavailable. This exception is strictly limited to emergency situations to prevent the loss of rights and does not apply to hostile non-Muslims or Zoroastrians. The interpretation is based on a narrative where two travelers falsely swore that they had not taken a deceased person's property, only to be exposed later. Once the truth was revealed, the initial testimony was invalidated, and the verse mandates that new witnesses must replace the false ones. The professor clarifies that the "rejection of the oath" in the verse does not refer to the standard legal procedure of transferring an oath from the defendant to the plaintiff, but rather to the nullification of the initial testimony due to discovered falsehood. Furthermore, he notes that the obligation to provide witnesses in such cases is a preliminary duty to ensure that rights are communicated to others. Finally, the non-Muslim witnesses must be trustworthy and just within their own religious community for their testimony to be valid.
