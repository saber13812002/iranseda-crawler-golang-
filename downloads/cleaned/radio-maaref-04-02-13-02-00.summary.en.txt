In this radio program, Professor Maleki addresses various Quranic questions from listeners. He explains that individuals who accept God but mock religious duties like prayer and fasting are often driven by ignorance, poor upbringing, or hypocrisy, noting that faith alone is insufficient for salvation without righteous deeds. He refutes the concept of "Quranism" by emphasizing that specific legal details, such as prayer units and zakat thresholds, are derived from the narrations of the Ahl al-Bayt and the Prophet's interpretation. The concept of "Margeh al-Bahrain" is interpreted as a divine boundary that maintains natural balance and benefits human health. Furthermore, he interprets verse 41 of Surah Zumar to clarify that the Prophet's role is strictly limited to conveying and teaching, rejecting any notion of coercion in guiding people. He stresses that while belief cannot be forced, enjoining good and forbidding evil remains a collective obligation for all Muslims. Finally, he asserts that Islamic governance must be strictly based on Quranic values, and rulers are responsible for upholding these divine principles.
