This radio program examines the historical connection between the Harra massacre and the Saqifah negotiations, specifically the fear of restricting the Caliphate to the Quraysh tribe. Following the tragedy of Karbala, Yazid invited the elders of Medina to Damascus to clear his reputation, but upon their return, Abdullah ibn Zubayr declared his independence from Yazid. Imam Sajjad (AS) remained uninvolved in these political movements, which led Yazid to order that the Imam's house be left untouched. Consequently, the house of Imam Sajjad (AS) became a safe haven for approximately 400 families in Medina, all of whom were spared from the three-day slaughter. The discussion also addresses the hadith "Imamate is from Quraysh" and the interpretive challenges faced by Sunni and Shia scholars regarding the twelve successors. The program highlights Hubab ibn Mundhir's earlier warning at Saqifah that limiting the Caliphate to Quraysh would eventually lead to oppression against the Ansar, a prophecy fulfilled during the Harra. Finally, the discussion emphasizes the need for precise identification of individuals' positions during this period and acknowledges that Imam Sajjad (AS) could not have predicted or prevented the full extent of the tragedy.
