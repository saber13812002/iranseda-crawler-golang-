The radio program "Enduring Memories" began by highlighting the multifaceted nature of the Holy Defense fighters. Sardar Haj Hossein Arouj recounted the harsh early conditions of the war, including the lack of weapons and training for civilian forces in Ahvaz and Susangerd. He described the grueling 42-kilometer night march for Operation Beit al-Moqaddas and the soldiers' reliance on faith and trust in God against the enemy. Mansour Mohammadi, another veteran, shared his experiences defending the Majnoon Island amidst reed beds and chemical contamination. He spoke of the fear caused by enemy wiretapping and the martyrdom of comrades during Operation Ramadan. The final segment of the program critiqued the Pahlavi regime's dependence on the United States and the legislative immunity granted to American advisors. This law, which Imam Khomeini termed "capitulation," fueled public outrage and became a catalyst for the Islamic Revolution.
