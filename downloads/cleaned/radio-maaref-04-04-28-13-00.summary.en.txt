This speech explores the functions of Muharram mourning and the importance of emotional arousal in the path of repentance. The speaker argues that emotion is the strongest human motivator and can compel addicts and sinners, who do not respond to rational advice, to reform their behavior. The story of the poet "Moqbel," who repented and was healed after mocking mourners while suffering from leprosy, is cited as evidence of the power of these rituals. Mourning Imam Hussein (PBUH) breaks the hardness of the heart, creating a foundation for accepting divine mercy and returning to the right path. Beyond individual impact, these rituals serve two key functions: establishing the identity of Shia Muslims and providing religious and historical awareness. The speaker emphasizes the role of Karbala's tragedies, particularly the martyrdom of Ali Asghar, in keeping the faith and revolution alive. The speech concludes with prayers for the preservation of the nation and a wish for success in the Arbaeen pilgrimage.
