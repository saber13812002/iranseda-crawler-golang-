Ayatollah Javadi Amoli, in his exegesis of Surah Al-Ma'idah, defines "adab" as the grace and beauty inherent in performing lawful and pure acts, distinguishing it from general ethics and ritual laws. Drawing on Quranic verses and narrations, he posits that adab transcends the outward forms of worship, serving as the spiritual essence that beautifies human behavior at all levels. The concept of "Tawhidic adab" is presented as the highest form of etiquette, where an individual respects others while viewing all events through the lens of divine will and monotheism. The Prophet (PBUH) emphasized that the adab of each prophet is commensurate with their prophethood, necessitating a study of the prophets' conduct to understand its true nature. Imam Ali (AS) further elaborated in Nahj al-Balagha that adab involves respecting the boundaries of all things and engaging in self-discipline to achieve spiritual perfection. Ultimately, Tawhidic adab enables a person to honor others as God's envoys while maintaining unwavering gratitude and recognition of God's rights. This perspective elevates adab from a mere social norm to a fundamental existential and theological principle rooted in the concept of Tawhid.
