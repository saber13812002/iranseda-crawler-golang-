In the radio program "Mr. Judge," a case was reviewed where a child was injured in a park due to the negligence of the facility's management.
The judge classified the incident as "quasi-intentional" under Article 291 of the Islamic Penal Code and sentenced the park's management and security officials to pay blood money.
Simultaneously, based on Civil Code Articles 1168 and 1173, the child's mother was also held accountable for her lack of adequate supervision over her child.
Following the incident, the park managers demonstrated good faith by visiting the child and providing financial assistance, leading to an agreement on the compensation.
Hojjat al-Islam Razaqi, head of Branch 51 of the Supreme Court, emphasized that protecting children's rights is the duty of both parents and public facility managers.
Citing religious narrations, he advocated for a compassionate and respectful approach to children, avoiding authoritarianism in their care and upbringing.
The program concluded with prayers for the Imam's reappearance and a call for Muslim unity against divisive nationalist agendas.
