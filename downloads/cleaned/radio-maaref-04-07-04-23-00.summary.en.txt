The program begins by emphasizing the concept of Mahdavi expectation as a permanent internal state rather than merely a future event.
The discussion then shifts to a theological analysis of the Imamate, specifically addressing objections to the Verse of Purification (Al-Ahzab, 33).
The primary critique was that the past tense verb "yurid" implies the present time, and "purification" necessitates a prior state of impurity.
The response clarified that divine will in the Quran represents God's eternal and timeless tradition, not limited to the current moment.
The use of the past tense here signifies the continuity and stability of this divine tradition, rather than a change in the timing of the will.
Regarding the second part of the objection, the semantic difference between "removal" (azalah) and "repelling" (azhak) was explained.
"Azhak" does not necessarily mean removing existing impurity but rather preventing the conditions for impurity from manifesting.
Thus, divine will signifies keeping the Ahl al-Bayt free from the causes of impurity and preserving their infallibility against animal instincts and Satan's whispers.
