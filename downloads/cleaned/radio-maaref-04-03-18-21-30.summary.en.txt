This text provides a jurisprudential analysis of the Quranic verse regarding the oath of two Christian witnesses to the Prophet's will.
A key distinction is made between witnessing a will and appointing an executor, noting that the latter is not mandatory in proprietary bequests.
Judicial rulings do not alter objective reality but serve to settle disputes; thus, if falsehood is discovered, a new legal case is initiated.
The prescribed oath consists of four parts: swearing by God, refusing personal gain even from relatives, not concealing divine testimony, and asserting truthfulness.
If perjury is proven, two new witnesses must replace the false ones, and the sinners are deemed among the people of Hell.
Due to its complex grammatical structure, this verse is considered one of the most difficult in the Quran, carrying over two hundred interpretive possibilities.
Ultimately, the text emphasizes that testimony is God's right and must be performed with complete honesty, driven by the fear of Hellfire or the preservation of honor.
