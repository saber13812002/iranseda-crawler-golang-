This text analyzes verses from the Quran that depict followers cursing their leaders in the Hereafter and demanding double punishment for them. A key point is that this scenario is not exclusive to the Prophet of Islam but serves as a general model of the relationship between leaders and followers across all nations. The discussion distinguishes between the leader and the follower, arguing that the leader incurs double punishment for two similar sins: their own misguidance and the act of misleading others. Conversely, a follower who merely follows blindly commits sins of a different nature and does not necessarily face double punishment unless their actions actively reinforced the leader's misguidance. The Quran confirms in other surahs that leaders carry the burden of their own sins and the sin of misleading others, but they do not bear the burden of the followers' personal misguidance. Therefore, the transfer of sin between leaders and followers is conditional upon the specific degree of involvement in spreading falsehood. This analysis demonstrates that divine justice in the Hereafter is applied based on the precise measure of each individual's participation in misguidance and misleading others.
