In this radio program, the concept of being a true Shia is defined as possessing two strong wings: a solid relationship with God and a strong relationship with people. The speaker emphasizes that genuine faith requires the simultaneous practice of strong worship, such as prayer and fasting, alongside active service to God's creation. Citing Quranic verses and narrations, he warns that neglecting either dimension removes the barrier against spiritual decline. The discussion then shifts to the jurisprudential and legal aspects of endowments and public ownership. The speaker argues that endowment assets belong to both current and future generations, with the ruler serving merely as a trustee. He stresses the necessity of preserving these assets and prohibits their direct conversion into cash without adhering to specific Sharia conditions. It is also noted that Islamic jurisprudence does not permit the direct conversion of endowments into liquid cash except under special circumstances. Finally, the importance of protecting the rights of future generations in managing public and endowment assets is highlighted.
