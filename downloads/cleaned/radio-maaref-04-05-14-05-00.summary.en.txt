The program begins by emphasizing the critical importance of prayer in Islam, citing Shia narrations that warn of severe consequences for neglecting it.
Imam Sadiq (AS) stated that those who take prayer lightly are deprived of the intercession of the Ahl al-Bayt and will be gathered with disbelievers on Judgment Day.
Prayer is described as the essential path to spiritual unity with God and a source of salvation during death and in the grave.
The discussion then shifts to a fiqh topic regarding the permissibility of adding a condition to a waqf (endowment) allowing its sale in case of extreme need.
Sheikh Ansari argues that such a condition is invalid because it contradicts the rational implication of waqf, which is its permanence and non-transferability.
Muhakkik Naini distinguishes between selling the waqf and converting it to another waqf, permitting the latter but still forbidding the direct sale of the original endowment.
The speaker refutes attempts to justify this condition by citing Quranic verses or narrations, arguing that these sources do not support the validity of such a stipulation.
Ultimately, the conclusion is that the condition to sell a waqf in times of need is legally and rationally invalid, as it fundamentally contradicts the eternal nature of an endowment.
