The program begins with a theological analysis of the Ayat al-Shahada, emphasizing divine monotheism, justice, and the high status of scholars as witnesses to God.
The instructor stresses the necessity of strengthening correct beliefs before performing religious duties and avoiding sins through absolute certainty in the afterlife.
Approaching the days of Ghadir, the importance of Muslim unity in accepting the principle of Wilayat and the Imamate of Ali (PBUH) is highlighted.
The second part features a narrative adapted from the novel "Jame'eh-ye Dast-Pesar," depicting the events of the 1963 uprising.
Salar, the protagonist, who left his family to join armed groups, is wounded during the crackdown, as is his son Sohrab.
Azar, Salar's ex-wife, visits the police station to find her missing son, confronting Colonel Sadri and Group Leader Rostami.
Despite claims that Salar collaborated with security forces, the group leader advises Azar to wait and leave the station to search for her son.
Azar, frustrated by the officers' coldness, is left in uncertainty after hearing rumors about Salar's injury and his potential return to Tehran.
