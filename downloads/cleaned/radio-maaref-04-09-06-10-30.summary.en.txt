The radio program "Shifa," featuring Dr. Pourhosseini, emphasized traditional and Islamic medical principles regarding diet and food storage. Key recommendations included avoiding the simultaneous consumption of eggs and fish, as well as maintaining a time gap between eating fish and cheese to prevent digestive conflicts. The expert also warned against storing acidic substances like vinegar and lemon juice in plastic, copper, tin, or aluminum containers, as this can cause poisoning, recommending glass as the safest alternative. In the second segment, Professor Ruhollah Rasoul, in the program "A Drop from the Sea," interpreted verse 31 of Surah Al-A'raf to stress the importance of correcting one's perspective on the world and avoiding waste in energy and resources. He argued that life is a competition for service and worship, and that a Quranic mindset should prioritize production over mere consumption. Finally, referencing the Prophet's (PBUH) example, the speaker urged Muslims to awaken and follow his practical model of establishing governance and fighting against oppression.
