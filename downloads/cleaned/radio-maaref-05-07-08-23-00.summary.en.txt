In this radio program, the necessity of the people's constant and prominent presence in protest fields is emphasized, alongside a discussion on the vital historical role of women in Islam. Citing Quranic verses, the host describes collective presence as a sign of faith and a path to divine proximity, while labeling unexcused absence as a sin. The main topic, "the mission of women," highlights how women have played crucial roles in preserving religion and Imamate during critical historical moments, despite their inherent gentleness. Examples such as Maryam, Asiyah, the grandmother of the Imam of the Time, and the mothers of the Imams are presented as models of this significant contribution. The program also references the role of women during the uprising and the appearance of the Imam, predicting their central role in establishing divine rule in the future. It further explores the eschatological dimensions of women's presence in history, noting the impact of Lady Masoumeh's martyrdom on the Islamic Revolution. Finally, the active participation of women in recent Iranian protests is praised as a continuation of this historical trajectory and a symbol of steadfast resistance and faith.
