This radio program discusses the hierarchy of major and minor sins, emphasizing the significant role of intention in committing them.
The legal distinction between sins that were merely intended and those prevented by external obstacles is clarified.
Approximately seven hundred major sins are identified, with murder and usury highlighted as the most severe categories.
The Quranic verse regarding the sanctity of life is interpreted to mean that killing one person is akin to killing all humanity, while saving one is like saving all.
The grave sin of falsely accusing chaste women of immorality is illustrated through a historical story from the era of Imam Ali.
Fleeing from the battlefield and consuming the property of orphans are also listed among the major sins discussed in the session.
The latter part of the program shifts to a jurisprudential debate on the validity of transmitted consensus (Ijma) in Islamic law.
The arguments used by Sunni scholars to justify the caliphate of Abu Bakr are critically analyzed and refuted based on logical and linguistic flaws.
