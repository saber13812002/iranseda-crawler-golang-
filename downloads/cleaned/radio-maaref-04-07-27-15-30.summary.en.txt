In this radio program, Dr. Mohammadi examined the role of the "principle of caution in blood" in Islamic jurisprudence. He explained that this principle has two aspects: it prevents the victim's blood from being wasted through the institution of "Qasamah" when there is no witness or confession, and it avoids unjust retribution via the rule "Hudud are repelled by doubts." The central discussion focused on whether this rule of doubt applies to retribution (Qisas) as well; although the primary narration is weak, the majority of jurists agree it covers all specific punishments, including Qisas. The distinction between Qasamah and the rule of doubt was also clarified, noting that Qasamah requires a strong "suspicion" of guilt by the judge, whereas the rule of doubt applies when there is genuine "uncertainty." The final conclusion emphasized that both institutions aim to preserve human life and prevent judicial errors in capital cases.
